# Best Practices: Territory Manager

> Design principles, patterns, and frameworks that guide high-quality territory design, account distribution, and regional sales performance optimization work.

## Design Principles

- **Balance Enables Motivation**: Territories with vastly different opportunity sizes create winners by circumstance and losers by assignment — balance is a prerequisite for fair performance assessment
- **Coverage Completeness Matters**: Uncovered market opportunity is revenue left on the table; territory design must ensure the full addressable market is assigned and actively worked
- **Account Capacity, Not Just Account Count**: Territory design must account for how many accounts a rep can actually work effectively — quality coverage requires realistic capacity
- **Account Quality Varies by Territory Type**: Named account territories need fewer, larger, higher-priority accounts; geographic territories need defined density thresholds
- **Territory Changes Require Pipeline Protection**: Mid-year territory realignments must include clear ownership rules for in-flight opportunities to prevent commission disputes and customer confusion
- **Coach the Plan, Not Just the Number**: Territory performance management requires coaching reps on their territory plan and account strategy, not just monitoring the quota tracker
- **Market Intelligence Flows Upward**: Territory managers are the intelligence channel from regional markets to central leadership — systematize what they learn about competitive presence, local customer needs, and market dynamics

## Key Patterns & Frameworks

- **Territory Design Framework**: Segmentation criteria (geography, industry vertical, company size) → opportunity sizing per segment → coverage model (named accounts vs. geographic) → rep capacity modeling → territory assignment → balance review → quarterly rebalancing trigger
- **Opportunity Scoring Model**: Account-level scoring based on ICP fit (firmographic match), intent signals (behavioral data), relationship quality (existing engagement), and opportunity size (revenue potential) — drives where to focus
- **Account Segmentation by Priority**: Tier A (strategic accounts — high value, high probability), Tier B (growth accounts — moderate value, active pipeline), Tier C (maintenance accounts — existing revenue, low expansion), Tier D (long-term nurture — low priority, future potential)
- **Territory Business Plan (TBP)**: Annual plan developed with each rep covering: target account list, Tier A account strategies, pipeline gap analysis, marketing activity requests, competitive account targets, and quarterly milestones
- **Pipeline Review Cadence**: Weekly (close week review), Monthly (territory health, stage distribution), Quarterly (TBP progress, account strategy updates, coverage gap analysis)
- **Whitespace Analysis**: Mapping where in the territory there are high-value accounts with no current coverage or engagement — identifies untapped opportunity for account planning
- **Coverage Model Audit**: Semi-annual review of whether the number of reps per territory is adequate relative to account count, opportunity value, and travel burden — triggers headcount requests or realignment
- **Competitive Account Map**: Identifying which major accounts in the territory are currently using competitor products — target list for displacement campaigns
- **New Rep Territory Ramp Plan**: Modified coverage model for new hire territories acknowledging reduced capacity during ramp — partner support, smaller account load, and defined milestones
- **Territory Handoff Protocol**: When a rep leaves or territory is realigned — account assignment rules, open opportunity ownership, customer communication, and onboarding for the incoming rep

## Domain Concepts & Terminology

### Territory Design
- **Territory**: A defined set of accounts, geography, or vertical market assigned to a specific sales rep or team
- **Named Account Territory**: A territory defined by a list of specific companies rather than a geographic boundary
- **Geographic Territory**: A territory defined by region, state, zip code cluster, or other geographic boundaries
- **Vertical Territory**: A territory defined by industry vertical (e.g., financial services, healthcare, manufacturing)
- **Coverage Model**: The ratio of reps to accounts that determines how much time and attention each account can receive
- **Account Capacity**: The number of accounts a rep can actively work at different intensity levels given available selling time

### Segmentation & Prioritization
- **ICP (Ideal Customer Profile)**: The firmographic and behavioral definition of your highest-value potential customers — the starting point for territory prioritization
- **Whitespace**: Accounts within the territory that represent untapped opportunity — not currently engaged and not current customers
- **Account Tiering**: Classification of accounts by priority level based on opportunity value and probability
- **Intent Data**: Third-party signals indicating an account is actively researching solutions in your category — used to prioritize outreach within the territory
- **Competitive Account**: An account currently using a competitor's product — potential displacement target

### Performance Management
- **Territory Quota**: The revenue target assigned to a specific territory — should reflect the realistic opportunity within the territory
- **Pipeline Coverage**: Total pipeline value as a multiple of the territory quota — healthy territories maintain 3–4× coverage
- **Account Penetration**: The percentage of Tier A and B accounts with at least one active engagement or pipeline opportunity
- **Win Rate by Territory**: Comparing win rates across territories — significant variance signals territory balance, rep skill, or market difficulty differences

## Anti-Patterns to Avoid

- **Unbalanced Territory Assignment**: Assigning territories of dramatically different opportunity value produces winners and losers by territory design, not performance — demoralizes low-opportunity reps
- **Territory Size Without Capacity Analysis**: Assigning territories with 500 accounts when a rep can only meaningfully work 80 creates false coverage that leaves most of the territory unworked
- **No Mid-Year Realignment Mechanism**: Leaving territories unchanged when significant account wins, losses, or market shifts change the opportunity balance creates persistent inequity
- **Territory Review Without Rep Input**: Designing territory realignments without input from the reps who know the ground-level market creates avoidable blind spots
- **Ignoring Rep Tenure in Territory Assignment**: Giving complex, high-value named account territories to new reps before they have product and competitive knowledge leads to poor performance in the accounts that matter most
- **No Handoff Protocol**: Territory reassignments without a structured customer transition process damage customer relationships and lose in-flight pipeline context
- **Coaching Only on Numbers, Not Plans**: Territory managers who only review quota attainment without discussing account strategy don't develop the strategic thinking that improves rep performance long-term

## Quality Indicators

- **Territory Balance Score**: Standard deviation of territory opportunity value — lower is better; high deviation indicates design imbalance
- **Account Coverage Rate**: Percentage of Tier A accounts with at least one active engagement in the past 30 days — below 70% indicates over-territory or coaching issues
- **Pipeline Coverage by Territory**: Is every territory maintaining the required pipeline coverage multiple against quota?
- **Win Rate Variance Across Territories**: Significant differences in win rate across territories with similar account profiles signal coaching or enablement gaps
- **Whitespace Penetration**: What percentage of identified high-value unengaged accounts have received at least one outreach attempt in the past quarter?
- **Territory Turnover Rate**: Rep attrition within territories — high turnover in specific territories may signal poor territory design or management issues
- **Forecast Accuracy by Territory**: Does each territory manager's forecast fall within ±15% of actual? Wide variance signals data quality or coaching issues

## Collaboration Touchpoints

- **With Sales Strategist**: Territory design operationalizes the sales strategy's segmentation model — territory managers implement the strategic segmentation; strategist validates that design serves the GTM model
- **With Field Marketing Manager**: Territory marketing plans require coordination between the territory manager (who knows the accounts and pipeline gaps) and field marketing (who executes the programs to fill those gaps)
- **With Sales Analyst**: Pipeline health, coverage gaps, and performance variance data are provided by the analyst; territory manager interprets account-level implications and coaching priorities
- **With Sales Ops Specialist**: CRM territory configuration, account assignment automation, and coverage reports are built by ops based on territory manager specifications
- **With Revenue Operations Manager**: Territory design impacts the entire revenue funnel coverage model — major territory changes should be modeled for revenue impact before implementation
