# Best Practices: Marketing Strategist

> Design principles, patterns, and frameworks that guide high-quality marketing strategy development, go-to-market planning, and competitive positioning work.

## Design Principles

- **Strategy is the Art of Saying No**: A credible marketing strategy defines what you won't do as clearly as what you will
- **Customer Insight Drives Strategy**: Strategy built on assumptions about buyers fails; start with evidence from customer research and market data
- **Positioning Before Messaging**: Getting the positioning right is the prerequisite — all downstream messaging and channel work depends on it
- **Segment Ruthlessly**: A strategy that targets everyone reaches no one; the tighter the focus, the more resonant the execution
- **Competitive Intelligence is Continuous**: Markets move; positioning that was accurate last year may be vulnerable today
- **Connect Strategy to Revenue**: Every strategic choice should be traceable to a pipeline or revenue impact — strategy disconnected from business outcomes is decoration
- **Plans Require Operationalization**: The best strategy without clear ownership, milestones, and measurement stays theoretical

## Key Patterns & Frameworks

- **STP Marketing Framework**: Segmentation (who's the market?) → Targeting (which segments to pursue?) → Positioning (how to win in chosen segments?) — the foundational sequence for any strategy development
- **Porter's Five Forces**: Supplier power, buyer power, competitive rivalry, threat of substitution, threat of new entrants — external environment analysis that informs strategic choices
- **Jobs-to-Be-Done (JTBD) Framework**: Customers don't buy products, they hire them to do a job; understanding the functional, emotional, and social jobs identifies true competitive alternatives and differentiation opportunities
- **Go-to-Market Strategy Framework**: Target segment + ideal customer profile + value proposition + messaging hierarchy + channel mix + sales motion + success metrics — the complete GTM architecture
- **Positioning Statement Template**: "For [target audience] who [has this problem/need], [brand] is the [category] that [differentiating benefit] because [reason to believe]"
- **Message Architecture**: Master value proposition → pillar messages (one per core benefit) → proof points and supporting evidence — provides structure for all downstream content and copy
- **Competitive Perceptual Map**: Plot brand and competitors on 2-dimensional axes representing key buying criteria; identify white space and competitive vulnerability
- **Market Sizing (TAM/SAM/SOM)**: Total Addressable Market → Serviceable Addressable Market → Serviceable Obtainable Market — sizes the opportunity and sets realistic expectations
- **Category Design**: Rather than competing in an existing category, define and name a new one where you are the default winner (e.g., "Inbound Marketing" owned by HubSpot)
- **Marketing Strategy Scorecard**: Strategy dimensions (positioning, channels, content, demand gen, brand) × health indicators (strong/adequate/at-risk) — periodic strategy health check

## Domain Concepts & Terminology

### Strategy Fundamentals
- **Market Segmentation**: Dividing a market into groups of buyers with common characteristics and needs
- **Ideal Customer Profile (ICP)**: The firmographic, technographic, and behavioral attributes of your best-fit customers
- **Buyer Persona**: A semi-fictional representation of a key decision-maker role within target accounts, including their goals, challenges, and information consumption habits
- **Positioning**: The mental space your brand occupies in the target audience's mind relative to alternatives
- **Differentiation**: The specific, defensible attributes that make your brand or product preferable to alternatives
- **Value Proposition**: The clear articulation of the specific benefit you deliver, to whom, and why you're uniquely qualified to deliver it

### GTM Planning
- **Go-to-Market (GTM) Strategy**: The comprehensive plan for reaching target customers and achieving competitive advantage in a new or existing market
- **Product-Market Fit (PMF)**: The degree to which a product satisfies a strong market demand — a prerequisite for efficient marketing spend
- **Revenue Targets**: The growth objectives that marketing strategy must be designed to achieve
- **Marketing Mix**: The combination of product, price, place, and promotion (4Ps) decisions that constitute the marketing strategy
- **Channel Strategy**: Selection of which marketing channels to invest in, at what levels, and in what sequence

### Competitive Intelligence
- **Competitive Landscape**: The mapping of all relevant competitors and their relative positioning, strengths, and weaknesses
- **Battlecard**: A sales-focused document summarizing key competitive differentiators, competitive objection responses, and when your product wins or loses against a specific competitor
- **Share of Voice (SOV)**: Your brand's presence in a given channel or medium relative to competitors
- **Competitive Moat**: A sustainable competitive advantage that is difficult or expensive for competitors to replicate

## Anti-Patterns to Avoid

- **Strategy Without Customer Research**: Assumptions about what buyers care about, even confident ones, are often wrong; validate before investing in execution
- **Positioning by Committee**: Strategy that tries to accommodate every stakeholder's opinion about positioning produces generic, undifferentiated messaging
- **Channel-First Strategy**: Choosing channels before defining positioning and audience leads to tactical fragmentation without strategic coherence
- **Ignoring Competitive Dynamics**: Building a marketing strategy without modeling how competitors will respond leads to strategies that work until they don't
- **Annual Plan Rigidity**: Annual marketing plans that don't have a mechanism for mid-year strategy revision in response to market changes are management rituals, not strategic tools
- **Disconnected Strategy and Measurement**: A marketing strategy without defined KPIs and attribution methodology cannot be evaluated, optimized, or defended
- **Market Size Overestimation**: TAM calculations that include everyone who could theoretically benefit from your product overstate the realistic opportunity and lead to overinvestment

## Quality Indicators

- **ICP Clarity Score**: Can the team describe the ideal customer with enough specificity to identify them in a target account list?
- **Positioning Differentiation**: Is the positioning statement clearly distinct from the three nearest competitors — not a variation on the same themes?
- **GTM Plan Completeness**: Does the plan include target segment, ICP, positioning, channel mix, content strategy, demand gen approach, and success metrics?
- **Strategy-to-Campaign Alignment**: Can every active campaign be traced back to a specific strategic objective and target segment?
- **Market Research Recency**: When was the last time customer and competitive research was updated? Strategies older than 12 months without a refresh are likely stale
- **Revenue Attribution**: What percentage of pipeline and revenue can be attributed to strategy-guided programs vs. ad hoc activities?
- **Competitive Coverage**: Does the team have current intelligence on the top three competitors' positioning, recent launches, and marketing moves?

## Collaboration Touchpoints

- **With CMO**: Ensure marketing strategy is aligned with company revenue targets, product roadmap, and investor narrative; escalate positioning decisions that require C-suite alignment
- **With Product Marketing Manager**: Strategy provides the positioning foundation; product marketing operationalizes it for product launches and sales enablement — strategy and PMM must share the positioning source of truth
- **With Campaign Manager**: Campaign briefs must be derived from the strategic messaging architecture; review briefs against strategy before campaigns launch to prevent strategic drift
- **With Marketing Analyst**: Strategy decisions should be grounded in market and performance data; analytics provides the evidence base for strategic choices
- **With Sales Strategist**: Marketing strategy and sales strategy must address the same target segments with compatible positioning and messaging — joint review quarterly
