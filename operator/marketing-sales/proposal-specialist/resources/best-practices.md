# Best Practices: Proposal Specialist

> Design principles, patterns, and frameworks that guide high-quality proposal writing, RFP response, and deal documentation work.

## Design Principles

- **Win Strategy Before Writing**: The document structure and emphasis should be driven by what you know about the buyer's priorities — write the strategy, then write the proposal
- **Customer Language Over Vendor Language**: Mirror the buyer's own terminology, concerns, and stated evaluation criteria — proposals that echo the buyer's words score better
- **The Executive Summary Wins or Loses the Deal**: Senior decision-makers may read only the executive summary; it must stand alone as a persuasive, complete argument
- **Proof Over Promise**: Claims without evidence are common; substantiated claims with specific customer proof are rare and persuasive
- **Brevity Signals Confidence**: Verbose proposals signal insecurity; concise, precise proposals signal expertise and respect for the reader's time
- **Comply, Then Compel**: In RFPs, meet all compliance requirements first; then find opportunities to differentiate within or beyond the prescribed format
- **Proposal is a Sales Document, Not a Technical Manual**: The purpose is to create conviction to choose you — not to catalog every feature you have

## Key Patterns & Frameworks

- **Proposal Win Strategy Framework**: Before writing, define: the buyer's top 3 decision criteria, your 3 strongest differentiators, the key risk/concern to neutralize, and the one competitor you're most likely displacing — this shapes emphasis
- **Executive Summary Formula**: Problem statement (their words) → proposed solution (one paragraph) → business outcomes (quantified) → why us (unique capability or proof) → proposed investment + timeline → recommended next step
- **RFP Response Matrix**: For formal RFPs, map each requirement to your response, compliance status, and the team member responsible — prevents gaps and enables QA review
- **Solution Design Narrative**: Challenge → requirements → proposed approach → rationale for approach choices → implementation plan → team & credentials → proof of similar work → investment → next steps
- **Value Quantification Framework**: For each stated business pain, estimate the cost of the problem (time × rate, or revenue at risk) and the value delivered by your solution — makes "ROI" concrete, not generic
- **Proof Point Integration Protocol**: For every positioning claim, identify the best-available substantiation: customer quote, case study reference, metric, or third-party validation — build into the review checklist
- **Section-Level Win Themes**: Identify 3–5 win themes (key reasons you should win) and ensure they appear in the executive summary, relevant sections, and conclusion — reinforces the strategic message
- **Graphic Design Brief for Proposals**: Proposals should have consistent visual design — cover page, section headers, callout boxes for win themes, data visualizations — briefed to a designer before production
- **Post-Award/Post-Loss Debrief Protocol**: For won proposals: what elements did the buyer highlight? For lost proposals: what would have changed the outcome? — builds institutional knowledge
- **Proposal Kickoff Meeting Agenda**: Deal context → buyer evaluation criteria → win strategy → section assignments → timeline → review process → approvals required — establishes alignment before writing begins

## Domain Concepts & Terminology

### Proposal Types
- **RFP (Request for Proposal)**: Formal document issued by a buyer requesting structured vendor responses — typically with required format and evaluation criteria
- **RFI (Request for Information)**: Less formal than RFP; used by buyers to gather market information before formal procurement — less likely to lead directly to a contract
- **RFQ (Request for Quote)**: Price-focused request where the solution is largely specified — buyer is primarily evaluating cost
- **SOW (Statement of Work)**: Defines deliverables, timeline, and terms for a specific engagement — part of contract documentation
- **Unsolicited Proposal**: A proactive proposal submitted without a formal RFP — requires more persuasion on problem framing since the buyer hasn't declared a need

### Proposal Components
- **Executive Summary**: The highest-priority section — the persuasive overview for senior decision-makers
- **Win Theme**: A specific, differentiating reason why the buyer should choose you, woven throughout the proposal
- **Evaluation Criteria**: The stated or implied factors the buyer will use to select a vendor — proposals must be written to score well on these
- **Past Performance Section**: References, case studies, and testimonials demonstrating successful delivery of similar work
- **Technical Approach**: Detailed explanation of how you will deliver the solution — must be credible, specific, and differentiated

### Deal Context
- **Discovery Summary**: The compiled insights from sales discovery conversations that inform the proposal's win strategy
- **Competitive Intelligence**: What you know about competing vendors' likely approaches and weaknesses — informs ghosting and differentiation
- **Ghosting**: Writing proposal language that highlights a weakness of a competitor without naming them — "unlike approaches that require [X]…"
- **Compliance Matrix**: A checklist confirming that every RFP requirement has been addressed in the response

## Anti-Patterns to Avoid

- **Boilerplate Overload**: Filling proposals with generic company description, marketing language, and feature lists that don't address the buyer's specific situation signals a non-customized response
- **No Executive Summary**: Beginning the document with background and history instead of a persuasive executive summary loses senior decision-makers before they see your best argument
- **Responding to the Wrong Question**: Answering what the RFP literally asks rather than what the buyer actually cares about; discovery context should inform interpretation
- **Claims Without Proof**: Making capability claims ("industry-leading," "best-in-class") without specific customer evidence or metrics invites skepticism
- **Exceeding Page Limits**: Submitting substantially longer proposals than requested signals inability to prioritize and may disqualify the response in formal procurement
- **Internal Team Focus Instead of Buyer Focus**: Spending pages on your company history, org chart, and internal processes rather than the buyer's outcomes and how you'll deliver them
- **Last-Minute Rush Without Review**: Submitting proposals without a structured QA review (compliance check, win theme check, proofread) produces avoidable errors that undermine credibility

## Quality Indicators

- **Win Rate on Proposal Submissions**: Percentage of submitted proposals that result in contract award — the primary outcome metric
- **Executive Summary Quality**: Does it stand alone as a compelling argument for selection in under 2 pages?
- **Compliance Rate**: For RFPs, percentage of requirements addressed without gaps — non-compliance disqualifies
- **Win Theme Coverage**: Do the 3–5 win themes appear in the executive summary, relevant sections, and conclusion?
- **Proof Point Density**: Ratio of substantiated claims (with specific evidence) to unsubstantiated claims — higher is better
- **Proposal Turnaround Time**: Average days from kickoff to submission — a throughput metric that affects deal participation capacity
- **Buyer Feedback Utilization**: Are debrief insights from won and lost proposals systematically applied to improve future responses?

## Collaboration Touchpoints

- **With Account Executive**: Discovery context from AE is the proposal's strategic foundation — conduct a structured proposal kickoff to transfer knowledge about buyer priorities, evaluation criteria, and competitive dynamics
- **With Sales Strategist**: Complex proposals should reflect the broader sales strategy for the account; validate win themes against the account strategy before writing
- **With Copywriter**: Long-form proposal copy benefits from copywriter collaboration on executive summary language and win theme articulation
- **With Sales Engineer**: Technical solution sections require accuracy validation; SE reviews technical approach sections before submission
- **With Product Marketing Manager**: Positioning, competitive differentiators, and proof points in proposals should align with PMM-approved messaging; use PMM materials as source of truth
