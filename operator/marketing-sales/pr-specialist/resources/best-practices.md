# Best Practices: PR Specialist

> Design principles, patterns, and frameworks that guide high-quality media relations, press strategy, and brand reputation management work.

## Design Principles

- **Journalists Write Stories, Not Ads**: Pitch a story that serves the journalist's audience, not a story that serves your marketing goals
- **Relationship Before Pitch**: Journalists respond to pitches from people they recognize; invest in relationship-building before you need coverage
- **Newsworthiness is the Filter**: Apply the journalist's test before pitching: Is this new? Is this surprising? Is this relevant to their readers? Would I cover this?
- **Proactive Over Reactive**: The best time to build your reputation is before a crisis; the best time to develop journalist relationships is before you need them
- **Speed in Crisis is Everything**: A 24-hour delay in crisis response allows the narrative to be written without you — respond within hours with a factual statement
- **One Spokesperson Speaks**: Inconsistent messaging from multiple sources during a sensitive story creates contradictions that become the story
- **Coverage Quality Over Quantity**: One story in a tier-1 publication read by your target audience beats ten stories in irrelevant outlets

## Key Patterns & Frameworks

- **PESO Model**: Paid (sponsored content, native advertising), Earned (PR/media coverage), Shared (social amplification), Owned (blog, website) — PR owns the earned layer and coordinates with others
- **Tier-1/2/3 Media Prioritization**: Tier 1 (flagship outlets read by target audience, highest impact), Tier 2 (vertical/trade publications, strong ICP coverage), Tier 3 (regional, niche, or online-only) — allocate pitch effort accordingly
- **Media Pitch Framework**: Hook (the news angle in one sentence) → context (why now, why relevant) → story angle (the unique perspective) → offer (exclusive, spokesperson availability, data) → CTA (simple, specific ask)
- **Press Release Format**: Headline + subhead + dateline + lead paragraph (who, what, where, when, why) + supporting body + quotes (executive + customer/partner) + boilerplate → For Immediate Release or Embargo date
- **Crisis Communication Protocol**: Immediate acknowledgment (within 2 hours) → factual holding statement → investigation + spokesperson assignment → full response → post-crisis narrative management
- **Spokesperson Preparation Checklist**: Message training, anticipated Q&A, key message reinforcement, what not to say, how to bridge from hostile questions to key messages
- **Analyst Relations Program**: Map relevant analysts (Gartner, Forrester, IDC) to product categories → brief analysts on major launches → respond to inquiry requests → manage placement in Magic Quadrant / Wave processes
- **Thought Leadership Pipeline**: Identify executive spokespersons → develop 5–10 POV themes → match themes to publication opportunities → ghostwrite bylines → place op-eds and guest columns
- **Media Monitoring Protocol**: Daily alerts on brand mentions, competitor coverage, and key industry topics → weekly coverage summary → monthly share-of-voice analysis
- **Embargo Management**: Pre-briefing key journalists under embargo before announcement date → tracking who has agreed → preparing embargo break contingency plan

## Domain Concepts & Terminology

### Media Relations
- **Pitch**: A brief, tailored message to a journalist proposing a story idea — one pitch should serve one journalist
- **Press Release / News Release**: Official written announcement of company news for media distribution
- **Embargo**: Agreement with journalists to hold a story until an agreed release date — enables more complete coverage on launch day
- **Exclusive**: Offering a story to one journalist/outlet before any other — in exchange for more prominent placement or favorable timing
- **Media List**: Curated set of journalists and outlets with relationship notes, beat coverage, and recent article history
- **Beat**: A journalist's area of coverage specialty (enterprise tech, cybersecurity, fintech, etc.)
- **Byline**: An article authored by your executive or spokesperson published under their name in a media outlet
- **Op-Ed**: Opinion piece placed in a publication that positions your spokesperson as a thought leader

### Reputation Management
- **Share of Voice (SOV)**: Your brand's media presence as a percentage of total coverage in your category or competitive set
- **Sentiment Analysis**: Categorizing media coverage as positive, neutral, or negative to track perception trends
- **Crisis Communications**: Strategic communication in response to an event that threatens brand reputation
- **Media Training**: Preparing spokespersons to deliver key messages clearly and handle difficult questions
- **Dark Site**: A pre-built holding website activated during a crisis that provides factual information and contact details

### Measurement
- **Media Impressions**: Estimated total readership or viewership of all coverage in a period
- **Domain Authority (DA)**: The SEO strength of a publication — higher DA coverage creates more valuable backlinks
- **Share of Voice (SOV)**: Your coverage volume relative to competitors in the same media space
- **Sentiment Score**: Aggregate positive vs. negative coverage ratio — a brand perception health metric

## Anti-Patterns to Avoid

- **Mass Pitch Blasts**: Sending the same press release to hundreds of journalists without personalization generates immediate deletion and reputation damage among journalists you've mass-spammed
- **No Story Angle**: Pitching company news without a story angle the journalist can use for their readers mistakes a press release for a pitch
- **Reactive PR Only**: Waiting for journalists to come to you or responding only to inquiries limits coverage to moments when you're not in control of the narrative
- **Over-Promising Coverage**: Guaranteeing media coverage to internal stakeholders is impossible; manage expectations to placements attempted and outcomes influenced
- **Slow Crisis Response**: Waiting to respond to a crisis story until legal has reviewed every word allows the narrative to calcify without your perspective
- **Spokesperson Proliferation**: Allowing multiple uncoordinated speakers during a sensitive news cycle creates contradictory quotes that become the story
- **Ignoring Negative Coverage**: Hoping negative stories fade without response or context leaves the narrative entirely in others' hands

## Quality Indicators

- **Tier-1 Coverage Volume**: Number of stories placed in tier-1 publications per quarter — the highest-impact coverage metric
- **Share of Voice Trend**: Is your brand's media presence growing relative to competitors?
- **Sentiment Ratio**: Ratio of positive to neutral to negative coverage — track trend, not just point-in-time
- **Journalist Relationship Depth**: How many tier-1 beat reporters have you briefed proactively (not pitch-only) in the past 90 days?
- **Crisis Response Time**: Average time from crisis event identification to first public statement — below 2 hours is the target
- **Analyst Inclusion Rate**: Presence in relevant analyst reports (Gartner, Forrester, IDC) as an indicator of category credibility
- **Byline Placement Velocity**: Number of thought leadership bylines published in tier-1 or tier-2 outlets per quarter

## Collaboration Touchpoints

- **With Brand Manager**: PR narratives and messaging must align with brand positioning and brand voice; brief on brand guidelines before any major announcement; escalate messaging decisions that require brand alignment
- **With Marketing Strategist**: Earned media strategy should reinforce the overall marketing strategy's target audience and positioning priorities
- **With Copywriter**: Press releases, executive bylines, and spokesperson quotes require copy quality review; collaborative writing ensures both journalistic credibility and brand voice
- **With Product Marketing Manager**: Product launch PR must reflect approved product positioning; share PMM messaging framework as input to PR narrative development
- **With Campaign Manager**: PR coverage amplification (sharing earned coverage on owned and paid channels) should be coordinated with campaign timing for maximum reach
