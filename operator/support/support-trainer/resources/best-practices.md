# Best Practices: Support Trainer

> Design principles, patterns, and frameworks that guide high-quality support team training, curriculum development, and agent onboarding work.

## Design Principles

- **Adult Learning Principles**: Support agents are adult learners — they engage when they understand why content is relevant to their job, when they can practice in realistic scenarios, and when training respects their existing knowledge
- **Job-Relevant from Day One**: Every training module should connect directly to behaviors agents will perform in their role; abstract knowledge without job application doesn't transfer
- **Show, Do, Reflect**: Effective skill training follows a three-step pattern — demonstrate the skill, have learners practice in a safe environment, debrief what worked and what didn't
- **Quality Data Drives Priorities**: Training content should be determined by quality gap analysis, not trainer intuition; develop training for the skills agents are actually struggling with
- **Certification Creates Commitment**: Formal assessment and certification gives agents a goal to work toward and creates a measurable competency standard; completion alone is insufficient
- **New Hire Ramp Is Critical**: The quality of onboarding directly determines time-to-productivity and early retention; under-investment in new hire training creates long-term performance problems
- **Continuous Learning Prevents Obsolescence**: Product changes, new ticket types, and evolving customer expectations make support knowledge obsolete quickly; training must be continuous, not front-loaded

## Key Patterns & Frameworks

- **4-Week New Hire Onboarding Program**: Week 1 (foundation — culture, tools, product basics, support process) → Week 2 (skill building — communication, troubleshooting, shadowing) → Week 3 (supervised practice — mentor-reviewed ticket work) → Week 4 (increasing independence — spot-checks, certification)
- **Kirkpatrick Evaluation Model**: Level 1 (Reaction: post-training satisfaction >85%) → Level 2 (Learning: knowledge assessment >85% passing) → Level 3 (Behavior: quality score improvement 30-60 days post-training) → Level 4 (Results: CSAT or FCR improvement correlation); measure at all four levels
- **Skills-Based Curriculum Design**: Map required skills from job description and quality rubric → identify which skills are teachable through training vs. hired for → design modules for teachable skills → sequence by prerequisite dependency
- **Roleplay and Simulation Design**: Design realistic support scenarios based on actual ticket types; roleplay before the queue prepares agents for emotional and technical challenges without customer impact
- **Train-the-Trainer Program**: Certify senior agents and supervisors to deliver specific training modules; multiplies training capacity and builds training into the team's DNA
- **Product Release Training Protocol**: When product releases are announced, trainer receives preview access → documents affected support workflows → develops training update → delivers before release date; no agent should handle a product change they weren't trained on
- **Knowledge Assessment Design**: Multiple-choice questions that test application, not recall; scenarios that mirror real ticket situations; minimum passing score with remediation path for non-passers
- **Learning Path by Role and Tenure**: Different paths for new hires, experienced agents cross-training, agents moving to technical support, and agents moving to team lead; avoid one-size-fits-all paths

## Domain Concepts & Terminology

### Instructional Design
- **Curriculum Map**: Complete inventory of all training modules with learning objectives, duration, delivery method, and prerequisites
- **Learning Objective**: Specific, measurable statement of what learners will be able to do after completing a module
- **Prerequisite**: Knowledge or skill required before attempting a module
- **Formative Assessment**: Low-stakes in-training checks (quizzes, practice scenarios) providing feedback during learning
- **Summative Assessment**: End-of-module evaluation confirming learning objectives were achieved; typically required for certification

### Delivery Methods
- **ILT (Instructor-Led Training)**: Synchronous instruction with a trainer facilitating a group; effective for complex skills and roleplay
- **e-Learning**: Self-paced online modules; effective for knowledge content and procedure reference
- **Microlearning**: Short (3-7 minute) focused modules on a single topic; effective for updates and reinforcement
- **Shadowing**: New hire observes experienced agent handling live tickets; effective for tone, style, and real-world examples
- **Mentoring**: Experienced agent reviews and provides feedback on new hire's tickets; bridges classroom to live queue

### Evaluation
- **Kirkpatrick Level 1-4**: Reaction, Learning, Behavior, Results — four-level evaluation framework
- **Knowledge Retention Curve**: Ebbinghaus's finding that learners forget 70% of new information within 24 hours without reinforcement; drives spaced practice design
- **Spaced Repetition**: Revisiting content at increasing intervals to combat the forgetting curve
- **ROI Measurement**: Business impact of training (CSAT improvement, ramp time reduction, quality score gain) relative to training cost

### Onboarding
- **Time-to-First-Ticket**: Days from start date to agent handling their first live customer contact
- **Ramp Period**: Duration from start date to full productivity; typically 4-6 weeks
- **Buddy System**: Pairing a new hire with an experienced agent for informal support during the onboarding period
- **Certification**: Formal credential awarded upon demonstrated competency; gates progression to independent work

## Anti-Patterns to Avoid

- **Content Dumping**: Loading new hires with product documentation to read rather than structured learning experiences; reading produces far less retention and application than active learning
- **Training Measured by Attendance Only**: Reporting training completion rates without measuring knowledge gain or behavior change; completion is a proxy for training effort, not training effectiveness
- **One-Time Onboarding**: Front-loading all training in the first four weeks and providing no continued learning; product knowledge becomes outdated and skills plateau without ongoing investment
- **Trainer Intuition Over Data**: Developing training content based on what the trainer thinks agents need rather than what quality scores say agents are actually struggling with; misallocates training effort
- **Roleplay in Controlled Conditions Only**: Practicing only ideal scenarios; agents need practice with frustrated customers, ambiguous issues, and time pressure to be truly prepared
- **Skipping Product Change Training**: Allowing product releases to launch before agents are trained on the changes; creates a predictable post-release support spike
- **Certification Without Remediation Path**: Requiring certification but not providing a structured path for agents who fail; agents who fail assessments and have no clear remediation become disengaged

## Quality Indicators

- **Onboarding Satisfaction >85%**: New hire rating of onboarding experience
- **Certification Pass Rate >85%**: Most new hires pass assessments on first attempt
- **Ramp Time Reduction**: New hire time to full productivity trending shorter over time
- **Post-Training Quality Score Improvement**: Agent quality scores measurably higher 30-60 days after training completion
- **Training Curriculum Freshness**: All modules updated within 90 days of relevant product or process changes
- **Knowledge Retention**: Assessment scores on quizzes 30 days after training indicate retention, not just immediate recall
- **Training Completion Against Schedule**: All required training delivered on planned schedule

## Collaboration Touchpoints

- **With Support Quality Analyst**: Receive quality gap analysis driving training curriculum priorities; quality data is the most reliable source of training needs
- **With Support Supervisor**: Identify agent-specific coaching needs from 1-on-1 discussions; supervisor knows which agents need what training
- **With Support Operations Manager**: Coordinate on new process training needs; every process change requires a training update before deployment
- **With Knowledge Base Manager**: Align on KB content and training content consistency; agents should be taught to use KB articles, not work around them
