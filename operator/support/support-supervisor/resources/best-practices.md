# Best Practices: Support Supervisor

> Design principles, patterns, and frameworks that guide high-quality front-line support team management, coaching, and real-time operations work.

## Design Principles

- **Present and Visible**: Supervisors who are present on the floor (virtual or physical) spot problems agents can't see, provide real-time coaching, and signal that leadership cares about agent experience
- **Coaching Is the Primary Job**: Ticket metrics and queue management are important, but the supervisor's highest-leverage activity is developing each agent's capability; agent improvement multiplies through every future customer interaction
- **Consistent Standards, Individual Approaches**: Hold all agents to the same performance standards while recognizing that each agent responds to coaching differently — one-size-fits-all coaching produces average results
- **Escalation Authority Is a Service**: When agents escalate to you, respond with urgency; your ability to resolve escalations quickly is what enables agents to do their jobs confidently
- **Queue Health Is a Team Responsibility**: Supervisors own the queue collectively, not just their individual productivity; proactive load-balancing prevents SLA breaches before they occur
- **Early Intervention Over Termination**: Performance issues rarely resolve themselves; identify struggling agents early and provide intensive support before the situation reaches a formal performance process
- **Recognition Drives Retention**: Agents who feel seen and appreciated outperform and stay longer; specific, timely recognition of excellent work costs nothing and pays significantly

## Key Patterns & Frameworks

- **Morning Readiness Checklist**: Review overnight queue backlog, check today's staffing and coverage, confirm SLA risk on aging tickets, brief team on known product issues, and set daily performance targets before the queue opens
- **Real-Time Queue Monitoring Protocol**: Monitor queue length, oldest unassigned ticket age, SLA breach risk, and active escalations every 30 minutes; intervene proactively when any metric approaches threshold
- **Weekly 1-on-1 Coaching Framework**: 30-minute weekly sessions — Agent challenges and goals (10 min) → Performance review with specific ticket examples (10 min) → Skill development planning (5 min) → Support needs (5 min)
- **GROW Coaching Model**: Goal → Reality → Options → Will — structure coaching conversations to help agents develop their own solutions rather than imposing prescriptions
- **Escalation Triage Process**: Assess escalated ticket for priority, customer tier, and complexity within 5 minutes; either resolve directly, engage specialist, or brief the next tier and communicate timeline to the agent and customer
- **Team Performance Dashboard**: Daily agent-level view of tickets handled, AHT, CSAT, quality score, and SLA compliance; review daily to identify outliers before weekly 1-on-1s
- **Knowledge Gap Identification Loop**: When an agent asks a question or makes an error, ask whether it's a knowledge gap, a skill gap, or a motivation gap; each requires a different intervention
- **Shift Handoff Protocol**: Brief the incoming team on queue status, active escalations, known product issues, and agents who need support; documented handoff prevents issues from slipping between shifts

## Domain Concepts & Terminology

### Team Management
- **Span of Control**: Number of agents reporting to a supervisor; typically 8-12 for support teams
- **Performance Review Cycle**: Cadence of formal performance evaluation — typically quarterly formal, weekly informal
- **PIP (Performance Improvement Plan)**: Formal structured plan for agents consistently below performance standards; defines improvement targets, support, and timelines
- **Attendance and Adherence**: Tracking agent presence and schedule compliance; critical for queue staffing
- **Escalation Handling**: Supervisor's responsibility to resolve Tier 2 escalations that exceed agent authority
- **Shift Coverage**: Ensuring adequate staffing across all hours the support queue is open

### Queue Operations
- **Queue Backlog**: Accumulated unresolved tickets in the queue; high backlog indicates staffing or efficiency problem
- **Oldest Unassigned Ticket Age**: Age of the ticket that has been waiting longest without assignment; leading indicator of SLA breach risk
- **Load Balancing**: Distribution of ticket work across available agents to prevent uneven workloads
- **SLA Breach Risk**: Tickets approaching their SLA deadline without resolution; requires immediate supervisory intervention
- **Real-Time Adherence**: Monitoring whether agents are following their assigned schedule moment-to-moment

### Coaching
- **Knowledge Gap**: Agent doesn't know the correct answer or procedure; solved by training and resources
- **Skill Gap**: Agent knows the answer but struggles to apply it effectively; solved by practice and coaching
- **Motivation Gap**: Agent knows what to do and how to do it but isn't doing it; requires understanding root cause (burnout, disengagement, personal circumstances)
- **Constructive Feedback**: Specific, behavioral, actionable feedback identifying what needs to change and how
- **Positive Reinforcement**: Recognition of specific high-quality behaviors; reinforces what should be repeated
- **SMART Coaching Goal**: Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, Time-bound improvement commitment from a coaching session

### Performance Metrics
- **CSAT**: Post-interaction customer satisfaction; agent-level metric
- **FCR (First Contact Resolution)**: Percentage of tickets resolved without follow-up
- **AHT (Average Handle Time)**: Efficiency metric; must be balanced against CSAT
- **Quality Score**: Internal audit score from quality analyst review

## Anti-Patterns to Avoid

- **Metric Surveillance**: Monitoring agent numbers without context or coaching; agents feel tracked rather than supported, eroding trust and engagement
- **Reactive-Only Management**: Waiting for problems to reach threshold before intervening; queue health, agent performance, and customer satisfaction all require proactive monitoring
- **One-Size Coaching**: Applying the same coaching approach to every agent; high-performers need development challenges, mid-range performers need targeted skill coaching, and struggling agents need structured support
- **Escalation Bottleneck**: Creating a backlog of escalations by being unavailable or slow to respond; agents waiting on supervisor decisions while customers wait creates compound dissatisfaction
- **Missing 1-on-1s**: Canceling or shortening 1-on-1 coaching sessions when the queue is busy; the times when the queue is busiest are when coaching investment pays back fastest
- **Feedback Without Follow-Up**: Providing coaching feedback and then never checking whether the agent applied it or whether the approach worked; coaching is a cycle, not a one-time event
- **Praise Without Specificity**: Saying "good job" without describing what was good; vague positive feedback doesn't reinforce the specific behaviors that produced excellent outcomes

## Quality Indicators

- **CSAT >95%**: Team-level customer satisfaction consistently above target
- **FCR >70%**: Most issues resolved in first contact across the team
- **SLA Compliance >98%**: Queue consistently meeting response time commitments
- **1-on-1 Completion Rate**: 100% of weekly coaching sessions held as scheduled
- **Quality Score >90%**: Team average quality audit score
- **Agent Retention Rate**: Low agent turnover indicates effective people management
- **Escalation Resolution Time**: Time from agent escalation to supervisor response and resolution

## Collaboration Touchpoints

- **With Support Quality Analyst**: Receive weekly quality scores and coaching summaries per agent; quality analyst provides the objective performance data while supervisor handles the coaching relationship
- **With Support Trainer**: Identify training needs from coaching conversations and quality gaps; supervisor is the primary source of intelligence on what agents need to learn
- **With Support Director**: Escalate critical incidents, team morale issues, resource requests, and performance cases requiring executive involvement
- **With Escalation Manager**: Coordinate on Tier 3+ escalations that exceed supervisor authority; supervisor owns Tier 2 while escalation manager owns Tier 3 and above
