# Competitive Analysis Framework

Structured approach to competitive intelligence gathering and analysis for strategic planning.

## Framework Overview

Competitive analysis for planning follows a four-phase cycle: Identify, Collect, Analyze, Apply. Each phase feeds the next and the cycle repeats as markets evolve.

## Phase 1: Identify Competitors

### Direct Competitors
- Companies offering the same product category to the same customer segment
- Compete on features, price, and distribution
- Typically 3-7 primary direct competitors in any market

### Indirect Competitors
- Companies solving the same problem with a different approach
- May address adjacent segments that could overlap with yours
- Often overlooked but represent the largest competitive risk

### Emerging Competitors
- Startups or adjacent players moving into your space
- Monitor via funding databases (Crunchbase, PitchBook), job postings, and patent filings
- Early detection gives 6-12 months of strategic lead time

### Substitute Threats
- Alternatives that eliminate the need for your product category entirely
- Examples: spreadsheets replacing specialized tools, AI replacing manual services
- Assess the switching cost and adoption friction for substitutes

## Phase 2: Collect Intelligence

### Information Sources (Ranked by Reliability)

| Source | Reliability | Freshness | Cost |
|--------|------------|-----------|------|
| Public filings (SEC, annual reports) | High | Quarterly | Free |
| Product trials and teardowns | High | Real-time | Low |
| Customer win/loss interviews | High | Monthly | Medium |
| Industry analyst reports | Medium-High | Quarterly | High |
| Job postings and org charts | Medium | Weekly | Free |
| Press releases and blogs | Medium | Daily | Free |
| Social media and forums | Low-Medium | Real-time | Free |
| Rumor and speculation | Low | Variable | Free |

### Structured Collection Template

For each competitor, collect across four profiles:
- **Company**: Founded, HQ, employee count, funding/revenue, mission, key leadership
- **Product**: Core features, pricing tiers, tech stack, integrations, recent launches (last 6 months)
- **Go-to-Market**: Target segments/ICPs, sales model (self-serve/sales-led/PLG), channels, messaging
- **Financial**: Revenue (known or estimated), growth rate, funding stage, profitability indicators

## Phase 3: Analyze

### Competitive Positioning Map

Plot competitors on a 2x2 matrix using the two dimensions most relevant to your market:

Common dimension pairs:
- Price vs. Feature Depth
- Ease of Use vs. Enterprise Readiness
- Breadth vs. Specialization
- Innovation Speed vs. Stability

### Feature Comparison Matrix

| Feature | Your Product | Competitor A | Competitor B | Competitor C |
|---------|-------------|-------------|-------------|-------------|
| Core Feature 1 | Strong | Strong | Weak | Medium |
| Core Feature 2 | Medium | Strong | Strong | Weak |
| Integration X | Yes | No | Yes | Yes |
| Pricing (per seat) | $25 | $30 | $20 | $35 |

Scoring scale: Strong / Medium / Weak / Absent

### SWOT per Competitor

For each major competitor, document:
- **Strengths**: What they do better than you
- **Weaknesses**: Where they fall short
- **Opportunities**: Gaps you can exploit
- **Threats**: Moves they could make that would hurt you

### Porter's Five Forces Assessment

Apply to the overall market:
1. **Rivalry intensity**: Number of competitors, growth rate, differentiation
2. **Buyer power**: Switching costs, price sensitivity, alternatives
3. **Supplier power**: Dependency on platforms, APIs, talent pools
4. **Threat of new entrants**: Barriers to entry, capital requirements
5. **Threat of substitutes**: Alternative solutions, technology shifts

## Phase 4: Apply to Planning

### Strategic Implications Matrix

| Competitive Finding | Planning Implication | Priority | Timeline |
|--------------------|---------------------|----------|----------|
| Competitor A launching AI feature | Accelerate AI roadmap | High | Q2 |
| Competitor B cutting prices 20% | Review pricing strategy | Medium | Q3 |
| No competitor serves mid-market | New segment opportunity | High | Q2-Q3 |
| Emerging player raised Series B | Monitor; prepare response | Low | Ongoing |

### Defensive Planning

When a competitor threatens your position:
1. Identify the specific threat vector (feature, price, segment, channel)
2. Assess your current defensibility (switching costs, network effects, data moats)
3. Determine response type: match, differentiate, or reposition
4. Estimate response timeline and resource cost
5. Build into planning roadmap with explicit milestones

### Offensive Planning

When you identify competitive opportunity:
1. Validate the gap with customer research (do not rely on competitive analysis alone)
2. Size the opportunity (revenue potential, customer count)
3. Assess your ability to execute (team, technology, timeline)
4. Define success criteria and measurement plan
5. Prioritize against other roadmap items using weighted scoring

### Update Cadence

- Feature comparison matrix: Monthly
- Positioning map and full competitive report: Quarterly
- Emerging competitor scan: Bi-weekly
- Win/loss analysis: Ongoing

### Anti-Patterns to Avoid

- **Competitor obsession**: Spending more time on competitors than on customers
- **Feature parity trap**: Copying features instead of solving customer problems
- **Stale intelligence**: Using data older than 90 days for planning decisions
- **Confirmation bias**: Seeking only data that supports your preferred strategy
- **Ignoring substitutes**: Focusing on direct competitors while a different approach disrupts the market
