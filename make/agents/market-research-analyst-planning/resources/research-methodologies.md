# Market Research Methodologies for Planning

Comprehensive guide to research methodologies used during strategic and product planning cycles.

## Primary Research Methods

### Qualitative Research

**Customer Interviews**
- Conduct 1-on-1 semi-structured interviews with 8-15 participants per segment
- Use open-ended questions to uncover unmet needs and pain points
- Record and transcribe for thematic coding
- Ideal for early-stage planning when hypotheses are still forming

**Focus Groups**
- Assemble 6-10 participants per group, run 2-3 groups per segment
- Use a discussion guide with both structured and open prompts
- Capture group dynamics and consensus points
- Best for validating messaging, positioning, and concept reactions

**Ethnographic Observation**
- Observe users in their natural environment (workplace, home, mobile context)
- Document workflows, workarounds, and unspoken friction
- Produces rich contextual insight that surveys cannot capture
- Time-intensive: plan 2-4 weeks of fieldwork per study

### Quantitative Research

**Surveys and Questionnaires**
- Deploy to statistically significant samples (n >= 384 for 95% confidence, 5% margin)
- Use validated scales (Likert, NPS, CSAT) for benchmarking
- Mix closed-ended and matrix questions; limit to 15 minutes completion time
- Distribute via email panels, in-app intercepts, or third-party panels

**Conjoint Analysis**
- Present trade-off scenarios to quantify feature value and willingness to pay
- Requires 200+ respondents for stable utility estimates
- Outputs: part-worth utilities, importance scores, market simulation
- Use for pricing strategy and feature prioritization during roadmap planning

**A/B and Multivariate Testing**
- Deploy controlled experiments on live products or landing pages
- Measure conversion, engagement, or retention lift
- Requires statistical power analysis to determine minimum sample size
- Results feed directly into planning KPIs and forecasts

## Secondary Research Methods

### Competitive Intelligence

**Public Filing Analysis**
- Review SEC filings, annual reports, and investor presentations
- Extract revenue figures, growth rates, and strategic priorities
- Track competitive R&D investment and headcount trends

**Product Teardowns**
- Systematically disassemble competitor products or features
- Document UX patterns, pricing tiers, and integration points
- Score against your own product on a standardized rubric
- Repeat quarterly to track competitive trajectory

**Patent and IP Monitoring**
- Search USPTO, EPO, and WIPO databases for relevant filings
- Identify emerging technology directions and potential blockers
- Feed findings into risk assessment during planning

### Market Sizing

**Top-Down (TAM/SAM/SOM)**
- Start with total addressable market from industry reports (Gartner, IDC, Statista)
- Apply filters for geography, segment, and pricing to derive SAM
- Estimate serviceable obtainable market based on go-to-market capacity
- Cross-reference with bottom-up estimate for triangulation

**Bottom-Up**
- Count known prospects and average deal value
- Multiply by segment penetration rate
- Validate against sales pipeline and historical close rates
- More accurate for near-term planning horizons (1-2 quarters)

**Value-Theory Approach**
- Estimate the economic value your product creates for the buyer
- Apply a capture ratio (typically 10-30% of value created)
- Useful when markets are nascent and traditional sizing data is sparse

## Synthesis and Deliverables

### Research Report Structure

1. Executive summary (1 page) with key findings and recommendations
2. Methodology description and sample demographics
3. Findings organized by research question
4. Competitive landscape matrix
5. Market sizing summary with assumptions
6. Implications for planning (prioritized recommendations)
7. Appendices: raw data tables, interview guides, survey instruments

### Insight-to-Action Mapping

| Insight Category | Planning Input | Example Output |
|-----------------|----------------|----------------|
| Unmet need | Feature backlog item | "Users need offline mode" |
| Market gap | New product opportunity | "No competitor serves SMB segment" |
| Competitive threat | Risk mitigation plan | "Competitor launching in Q3" |
| Pricing signal | Revenue model adjustment | "Willingness to pay is $15/mo higher" |
| Trend shift | Strategic pivot consideration | "Market moving from on-prem to SaaS" |

### Quality Checklist

- [ ] Sample is representative of target segments
- [ ] Methodology matches research question type
- [ ] Confidence intervals and margins of error are stated
- [ ] Competitive data is current (less than 90 days old)
- [ ] Assumptions for market sizing are explicit and sourced
- [ ] Findings are triangulated across at least two methods
- [ ] Recommendations link directly to planning objectives
- [ ] Limitations and biases are disclosed
