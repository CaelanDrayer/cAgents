# Facilitation Techniques for Planning Sessions

Detailed techniques and structured activities for running effective strategic planning workshops and sessions.

## Core Facilitation Principles

### The Facilitator Role

The facilitator is a process leader, not a content contributor. Key responsibilities:
- Design the session structure and flow
- Manage time, energy, and group dynamics
- Ensure every voice is heard, not just the loudest
- Capture decisions, rationale, and action items
- Remain neutral on content; guide the process

### The Diverge-Converge Pattern

Every facilitated activity follows this fundamental rhythm:

1. **Diverge**: Generate as many ideas, options, or perspectives as possible. No evaluation.
2. **Emerge**: Organize, cluster, and identify themes in the generated content.
3. **Converge**: Evaluate, prioritize, and select from the organized options.

Never diverge and converge simultaneously. Mixing generation and evaluation suppresses creative thinking and produces mediocre results.

## Idea Generation Techniques

### Silent Brainstorming (Brainwriting)

**When to use**: Groups of 4-12. When vocal participants dominate. When the topic is sensitive.

**Process**:
1. State the question clearly and visibly
2. Each participant writes ideas on sticky notes (1 idea per note), silently, for 5-8 minutes
3. Collect all notes and post on a wall or board
4. Read aloud as a group (no attribution)
5. Cluster into themes

**Why it works**: Eliminates anchoring bias, production blocking, and social loafing. Research shows brainwriting produces 20-40% more unique ideas than verbal brainstorming.

### Round Robin

**When to use**: Small groups (4-8). When you need equitable participation.

**Process**:
1. Pose the question
2. Go around the table; each person contributes one idea per round
3. Continue for 3-5 rounds or until ideas are exhausted
4. "Pass" is allowed but discouraged
5. Record all contributions visibly

### Crazy Eights

**When to use**: Design-oriented planning. Rapid concept sketching. Groups of 3-8.

Fold a sheet into 8 panels. Set an 8-minute timer (1 minute per panel). Sketch one idea per panel, then share and dot vote on the most promising concepts.

## Prioritization Techniques

### Dot Voting

**When to use**: Quick prioritization of 10-30 items. Any group size.

**Process**:
1. Display all items visibly (wall, whiteboard, digital board)
2. Give each participant 3-5 dot stickers (or marker dots)
3. Participants place dots on their highest priority items
4. Count dots; rank by votes
5. Discuss the top items and any surprises

**Tip**: Use silent voting (everyone places dots simultaneously) to prevent herd behavior.

### Impact-Effort Matrix

**When to use**: Evaluating 10-25 options. Strategy and roadmap planning.

**Process**:
1. Draw a 2x2 matrix: X-axis = Effort (low to high), Y-axis = Impact (low to high)
2. As a group, place each item on the matrix
3. Discuss contentious placements (these reveal misaligned assumptions)
4. Prioritize: Quick Wins (high impact, low effort) first, then Major Projects (high impact, high effort), Fill-ins (low impact, low effort) if capacity allows, Avoid (low impact, high effort)

### MoSCoW Prioritization

**When to use**: Scope definition. Distinguishing essentials from nice-to-haves.

**Categories**:
- **Must Have**: Non-negotiable. Plan fails without these.
- **Should Have**: Important but not critical. Plan weakened without these.
- **Could Have**: Desirable. Include if time and resources allow.
- **Won't Have (this time)**: Explicitly excluded from this planning cycle.

Validate: Must Haves should be less than 60% of total scope.

## Consensus Building Techniques

### Fist of Five

**When to use**: Quick consensus check on a specific proposal.

**Process**:
1. State the proposal clearly
2. On the count of three, everyone holds up 0-5 fingers
3. Interpret: 5 = strong support, 3 = can live with it, 0 = block
4. If anyone shows 0-2, hear their concerns before proceeding
5. Proceed when everyone is at 3 or above

### Gradients of Agreement

**When to use**: Nuanced decisions where binary yes/no is too coarse.

**Scale** (adapted from Sam Kaner):
1. Wholehearted endorsement
2. Agreement with minor reservation
3. Support with reservations I can state
4. Abstain / live with the group decision
5. Disagree but will not block
6. Disagree and want my objection noted
7. Block: I cannot support this decision

Discuss positions 5-7 before proceeding. Aim for everyone at position 4 or better.

### Nominal Group Technique

**When to use**: High-stakes decisions with strong opinions. Groups of 5-12.

**Process**:
1. Silent idea generation (5 minutes)
2. Round-robin sharing (no discussion yet)
3. Clarifying discussion (questions only, no advocacy)
4. Private ranking (each person ranks top 5 items)
5. Tally rankings to produce group priority order

## Managing Difficult Dynamics

- **Dominant voice**: Use silent brainstorming and structured turn-taking to equalize input
- **Silent participant**: Use written input before verbal sharing; gently invite contributions
- **Tangent**: Use a "parking lot" board; redirect to the current question
- **Disagreement**: Reframe from positions to interests; use gradients of agreement; time-box if stuck

## Session Closing Checklist

- [ ] All decisions documented with rationale
- [ ] Action items have owners and deadlines
- [ ] Parking lot items assigned for follow-up
- [ ] Next session or checkpoint date confirmed
- [ ] Feedback collected and session notes distributed within 24 hours
