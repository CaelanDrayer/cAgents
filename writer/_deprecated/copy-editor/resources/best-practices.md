# Best Practices: Copy Editor

> Design principles, patterns, and frameworks that guide high-quality line editing, grammar correction, style consistency, and prose accuracy work.

## Design Principles

- **Serve the Author's Voice**: The copy editor's job is not to impose their own style but to make the author's intended style more consistently and correctly itself. Changes that improve technical correctness while flattening voice are net losses.
- **Style Guide Governs**: Establish the applicable style guide (Chicago, AP, APA, house style) before beginning. Inconsistency within a style is an error; consistency with the wrong style is worse.
- **Query Rather Than Change**: When a sentence is ambiguous, possibly intentional, or where the correct fix is unclear, query the author rather than presuming. The author's intent is the authority.
- **Separate Passes for Separate Functions**: Grammar/mechanics, consistency, fact-checking, and style are distinct cognitive tasks. Attempting all simultaneously produces incomplete coverage of each. Use separate focused passes.
- **Track Changes Always**: Every copy edit should be in tracked changes or with clear before/after documentation. The author must see and approve every change.
- **Comma Principles Over Comma Rules**: Rules about commas are guidelines; the underlying principle (clarity, rhythm, preventing misreading) governs edge cases. Apply the principle, then check the rule.
- **Context Determines Correctness**: Sentence fragments, rule-breaking punctuation, and unconventional grammar are correct when they serve the voice and context. The error is not the deviation; it is the unintended deviation.

## Key Patterns & Frameworks

- **Multi-Pass Editing Protocol**: First pass: structural check (does every sentence say what it intends?). Second pass: grammar and mechanics. Third pass: consistency check (proper nouns, hyphenation, capitalization). Fourth pass: fact verification. Fifth pass: final read-aloud.
- **Style Sheet Development**: Build a project-specific style sheet documenting every decision made during the edit — hyphenation of compound modifiers, capitalization of specific terms, spelling of character names. The style sheet prevents contradictory decisions across a long document.
- **Consistency Audit Method**: After completing the primary edit, run targeted searches for every proper noun, technical term, and stylistic decision in the style sheet to confirm consistent application.
- **Query Taxonomy**: Classify queries by type — (1) Ambiguity ("Did you mean X or Y?"), (2) Possible error ("This appears to contradict page 43"), (3) Style choice ("Consider whether this aligns with your voice"), (4) Factual question ("Verify the year here"). Different query types require different responses from the author.
- **Read-Aloud Verification**: The final pass reads the edited text aloud or mentally "hears" it. Errors that survive visual reading often surface when processed auditorily — awkward rhythm, missing words, repeated phrases.
- **Fact-Verification Protocol**: Identify every specific factual claim (names, dates, statistics, titles, locations) and verify each against a reliable source. Flag unverifiable claims for author attention.
- **Chicago vs. AP Decision Matrix**: For documents where style guide isn't specified, use domain as the guide — academic/literary = Chicago; journalism/news = AP; business/web = house style or AP. Document the choice in the style sheet.

## Domain Concepts & Terminology

### Grammar and Mechanics
- **Restrictive vs. Nonrestrictive Clause**: Restrictive clauses (no commas) identify which one; nonrestrictive clauses (set off by commas) add information about an already-identified noun. "The dog that bit me" vs. "My dog, which bit me."
- **Serial Comma (Oxford Comma)**: Comma before the final "and" in a list of three or more items; required in Chicago style, optional in AP style; must be applied consistently
- **En Dash vs. Em Dash**: En dash (–) for ranges and compound modifiers; em dash (—) for parenthetical interruptions and dramatic pauses. Both distinct from the hyphen (-).
- **Subject-Verb Agreement**: In complex sentences with intervening clauses or collective nouns, agreement can be counter-intuitive; verify with the logical head noun
- **Dangling Modifier**: Participial phrase whose implied subject doesn't match the sentence's actual subject ("Walking home, the rain fell" — the rain wasn't walking)

### Style and Voice
- **House Style**: The publication- or publisher-specific set of usage rules that supersede general style guides
- **Style Sheet**: Document recording all project-specific editorial decisions for consistency across a long work
- **Stet**: Latin instruction meaning "let it stand" — author's instruction to restore a change made by the editor
- **Track Changes**: Editorial annotation system showing all changes with author approval/rejection capability
- **Query**: Editorial note to the author requesting clarification, flagging a possible error, or proposing a change without making it

### Editing Levels
- **Proofreading**: Final-stage check for mechanical errors in a nearly finished text — typos, spacing, formatting
- **Copy Editing**: Grammar, mechanics, consistency, style, and fact-checking
- **Line Editing**: Sentence-level attention to rhythm, clarity, word choice, and prose quality
- **Developmental Editing**: Structural assessment of argument organization, narrative structure, and overall effectiveness

### Reference Guides
- **Chicago Manual of Style**: Comprehensive style guide for academic, literary, and general trade publishing
- **AP Stylebook**: Style guide for journalism and news writing; contrasts with Chicago on many points
- **Merriam-Webster**: Standard American English dictionary for spelling verification
- **Fowler's Modern English Usage**: Reference for grammatical edge cases and evolving usage norms

## Anti-Patterns to Avoid

- **Voice Flattening**: Editing colloquial or deliberately unconventional prose toward formal standard English and thereby erasing the author's distinctive voice.
- **Correction Without Explanation**: Making changes without queries or explanations in complex or ambiguous situations, leaving authors unable to evaluate whether the change is correct.
- **Style Guide Overreach**: Applying a style guide's principles to every sentence even when context clearly calls for an exception; style guides describe norms, not laws.
- **Grammar Superstition**: Enforcing "rules" that are myths (never end a sentence with a preposition; never split infinitives; never start a sentence with "but" or "and") at the expense of natural prose.
- **Fact-Checking Omission**: Completing grammar and consistency passes while skipping factual verification; unverified claims are a category of error that grammar editing cannot catch.
- **Inconsistent Query Standards**: Querying minor stylistic preferences while passing over significant ambiguities, or querying everything and flooding the author with unnecessary questions.
- **Single-Pass Editing**: Attempting to catch all error types in a single read-through; different error types require different cognitive modes and different attentional focuses.

## Quality Indicators

- **Style Sheet Present**: A project-specific style sheet exists and was applied consistently throughout the edit
- **Zero Introduced Errors**: A check of the edited text finds no errors introduced by the edit that weren't present in the original
- **Query Appropriateness**: Every query genuinely requires author input; no unnecessary queries clog the author's review
- **Consistent Proper Nouns**: All character names, place names, and technical terms are spelled consistently with no variation
- **Fact Flags**: Every verifiable factual claim has been checked or explicitly flagged for author verification
- **Voice Preservation**: The edited text sounds like the author's intended register, not the editor's preferred style
- **Clean Style Guide Application**: The applied style guide's rules are consistently followed throughout, with documented exceptions in the style sheet

## Collaboration Touchpoints

- **With Editor (Developmental)**: Developmental editor addresses structural and voice issues before copy editor receives the text; copy editor works on a text that has already been confirmed to be structurally sound
- **With Prose Stylist**: For literary work, prose stylist may work alongside copy editor to distinguish voice-serving rule-breaking from genuine error; the line between style and mistake requires collaboration
- **With Continuity Checker**: Continuity checker manages narrative fact consistency (timeline, character details); copy editor manages textual consistency (proper nouns, hyphenation); both work from the story bible but on different aspects
- **With Literary Critic**: For academic or literary criticism contexts, copy editor verifies citation formatting, quotation accuracy, and reference consistency that literary critic's analysis depends on
