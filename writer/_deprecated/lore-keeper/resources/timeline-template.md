# Timeline and Continuity Management Framework

This framework is not a spreadsheet — it is a system for managing the deep complexity of fictional history. A well-managed timeline does two things: it prevents continuity errors that break reader trust, and it reveals narrative opportunities hidden in the chronological structure of your world.

## I. Chronological Architecture

### Calendar Definition
Before you can build a timeline, you need a calendar. Every fictional calendar answers these questions:

**Astronomical basis**: What celestial events mark the passage of time? A single sun and moon give you days and months like ours. Two moons give you complex tidal patterns, dual calendars, and cultures that argue about which moon governs festivals. A world with no moon has no months, no tides, and different mythology.

**Year structure**: How many days in a year? How many months? Are months regular or irregular? Named or numbered? Do seasons align with months, or are they tracked separately? Is there an intercalary day or month to correct drift?

**Epoch markers**: What event marks Year 1? The founding of the empire, the death of a god, the cataclysm that reshaped the world, the ascension of the current dynasty? Different cultures may use different epoch markers, which means the same year has different numbers in different systems — a source of both confusion and worldbuilding texture.

**Week structure**: How many days in a week (if weeks exist)? Are they named for gods, planets, elements, virtues? Which day is for rest, worship, market? The weekly rhythm shapes daily life more intimately than any larger cycle.

### Era Structure

Organize history into eras defined by dominant power, prevailing conditions, or transformative events:

```
ERA FORMAT:
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
Era Name: [Descriptive, evocative title]
Period: [Start date — End date]
Duration: [In years/generations]
Defining Character: [1-2 sentences: what made this era distinct]
Began Because: [What initiated this era]
Ended Because: [What terminated this era]
Key Developments: [Technology, culture, politics that emerged]
Major Figures: [People who shaped the era]
Primary Sources Surviving: [What records exist from this period]
Historical Reputation: [How later generations view this era]
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
```

**Era transitions**: The most dramatically rich moments in a world's history. An era doesn't end neatly — it frays, resists, collapses. The transition period (the interregnum, the dark age, the revolution) is where the most interesting stories live.

**Deep time**: For worlds with long histories, not every era needs equal detail. The deep past (thousands of years ago) can be sketched in broad strokes — dominant civilization, general technology level, major myths originating from this period. The recent past (last few centuries) needs more detail. The immediate past (last generation) needs scene-level specificity.

### Pivot Points

History lurches from crisis to crisis. Identify the pivot points — events that changed the trajectory of civilization:

```
PIVOT POINT FORMAT:
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
Event: [Name by which it's remembered]
Date: [When it occurred]
What Happened: [Factual account — what actually occurred]
What People Believe Happened: [Legendary account — how it's remembered]
Causes: [What led to this event — economic, political, environmental]
Immediate Consequences: [What changed within a generation]
Long-term Consequences: [What changed over centuries]
Who Won: [Who benefited from this event]
Who Lost: [Who was damaged by this event]
Historical Debate: [What do in-world historians argue about regarding this event]
Primary Sources: [What records survive from this event]
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
```

**Causation chains**: Events cause other events. The assassination leads to war, the war leads to famine, the famine leads to migration, the migration leads to cultural blending, the cultural blending leads to a new religion. Trace these chains — they give history its internal logic and prevent the "random events" feel.

## II. The Historiographic Record

### Source Registry

Track what historical sources exist within your world. Each source has biases that shape how characters understand their own history.

```
SOURCE FORMAT:
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
Source Title: [Name of the document, tradition, or record]
Type: [Chronicle / Scripture / Oral tradition / Legal code / Personal account / Inscription / Archaeological]
Author/Origin: [Who created it, under what circumstances]
Date of Creation: [When was it produced]
Period Covered: [What historical span does it address]
Bias: [What perspective does it serve — royal, religious, commercial, populist]
Reliability: [High / Moderate / Low / Propaganda — and why]
Surviving Copies: [How many exist, where are they kept]
Access: [Who can read/access this source — public, restricted, lost, forbidden]
Key Claims: [The most important assertions this source makes]
Known Inaccuracies: [Where this source is demonstrably wrong or misleading]
Narrative Value: [How this source could function in the story]
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
```

### Contested Events

For pivotal events, develop at least two accounts. The tension between accounts creates narrative richness:

```
CONTESTED EVENT FORMAT:
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
Event: [Name]
Account A (Official/dominant):
  Source: [Which tradition provides this version]
  Claims: [What this account says happened]
  Serves: [Whose interests this version protects]

Account B (Alternative/suppressed):
  Source: [Which tradition provides this version]
  Claims: [What this account says happened]
  Serves: [Whose interests this version protects]

What Actually Happened: [The author's "true" version — may be unknown even to the author]
Narrative Potential: [How the contradiction between accounts could drive plot]
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
```

## III. Genealogical Records

```
House/Family: [Name]

  Secrets: [What the records don't say — known only to the author]

  Resolution: [How it was resolved and what precedent it set]

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
```

### Bloodline Powers and Curses

If lineage carries supernatural significance, track it systematically:
- What ability or curse passes through the bloodline?
- Is it dominant (every generation) or recessive (skips generations)?
- Can it be acquired through ritual, marriage, or violence?
- What are its manifestations, costs, and limitations?
- How does it affect succession politics?
- Has it changed over generations (strengthening, weakening, mutating)?

## VI. Continuity Tracking System

### Canon Registry

The master record of every canonical fact. This is the most utilitarian section — its purpose is purely to prevent errors.

```
CANON ENTRY FORMAT:
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Established: [Where this fact was first stated — chapter, scene, document]
Referenced: [Every subsequent reference to this fact]
Dependencies: [Other canonical facts that depend on this one being true]
Contradiction Risk: [What kinds of new developments could contradict this fact]
Last Verified: [When this entry was last checked against the current manuscript]
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
```

### Character Knowledge Matrix

Track who knows what, and when they learned it. This is critical for maintaining dramatic irony and preventing characters from acting on information they shouldn't have.

```
KNOWLEDGE TRACKING:
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

True State: [What is actually true]

  [Character A]: Learned in [Chapter/Scene] via [how they found out]
  [Character B]: Learned in [Chapter/Scene] via [how they found out]

Who Doesn't Know (But Should Soon):
  [Character C]: Will learn in [planned scene] via [planned revelation method]

Who Believes Something False:
  [Character D]: Believes [false version] because [reason for false belief]
  Correction planned: [When/if they'll learn the truth]
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
```

### Timeline Verification Checklist

Run this checklist whenever adding significant new events or scenes:

- [ ] **Character ages**: Do all characters' ages make sense given their birth dates and the current date?
- [ ] **Travel times**: Is there enough elapsed time for characters to travel between locations?
- [ ] **Seasonal consistency**: Does the season match the time of year? Do weather descriptions match the season?
- [ ] **Pregnancy/gestation**: If any characters are pregnant, does the timeline allow for proper gestation?
- [ ] **Technology consistency**: Is the technology level consistent with the era? No anachronisms?
- [ ] **Political consistency**: Are the rulers, laws, and political arrangements consistent with the current date?
- [ ] **Knowledge consistency**: Do characters only act on information they've actually received?
- [ ] **Cause-effect ordering**: Do causes precede their effects? Are there no backwards dependencies?
- [ ] **Parallel timeline sync**: If multiple plotlines are running simultaneously, do they agree on what day/month/season it is?
- [ ] **Death consistency**: Are dead characters still dead? (Unless resurrection is established as possible)
- [ ] **Relationship consistency**: Do character relationships reflect everything that has happened between them?
- [ ] **Wound and injury tracking**: Do injuries persist appropriately? Does healing take reasonable time?

## VII. Living History Techniques

### History in the Present

The past manifests in the present through these channels. Track them to ensure historical depth:

**Linguistic traces**: Words from dead languages embedded in current speech. Place names whose etymology is forgotten. Slang derived from historical events ("to pull a Brennan" meaning to betray an ally, because Lord Brennan switched sides at the Battle of Three Rivers).

**Architectural layers**: Buildings modified over centuries. The castle with a medieval core, early modern additions, and modern repairs. The city wall now buried under newer construction, visible only where a basement was dug too deep.

**Cultural memory**: Festivals whose original meaning is forgotten but whose rituals persist. Food traditions from eras of scarcity. Superstitions rooted in genuine historical dangers that no longer exist. Songs whose words no one understands anymore.

**Inherited grudges**: "Our family hasn't spoken to the Ashfords since the Partition." "The people across the river stole our land." "We don't trade with the eastern cities — they betrayed us during the Siege." These living grudges connect the narrative present to the historical past without exposition.

**Material inheritance**: Heirloom objects passed down through generations. The sword, the ring, the book, the map, the key. Each heirloom is a story compressed into an object, and its significance may have been transformed by time and retelling.
