# Best Practices: Product Marketing Manager

> Design principles, patterns, and frameworks that guide high-quality product positioning, go-to-market planning, and competitive intelligence work.

## Design Principles

- **Positioning is the Foundation of All PMM Work**: Messaging, launch planning, and sales enablement all derive from a well-defined positioning; invest disproportionately here
- **Customer Obsession Over Feature Obsession**: Great product marketing translates technical capabilities into buyer outcomes and jobs-to-be-done
- **Sales is Customer #1**: PMM exists to make sales more effective — the best positioning framework is useless if reps don't internalize and use it
- **Launch is a System, Not an Event**: A product launch is a 3-month coordinated program (before, during, after) across multiple functions — not a single announcement
- **Competitive Intelligence is Continuous**: Categories shift, competitors launch, narratives evolve — PMM maintains ongoing intelligence, not periodic snapshots
- **Proof Points Must Be Earned**: Positioning claims need substantiation; customer quotes, case studies, and data are the evidence that makes claims credible
- **Internal Alignment Precedes External Messaging**: Marketing, sales, support, and customer success must all tell the same story before customers hear it

## Key Patterns & Frameworks

- **Positioning Framework (April Dunford)**: Competitive alternatives → differentiated capabilities → objective differentiators → customer value → target customers — builds positioning from market context up, not feature list down
- **Message Architecture**: Master value proposition → pillars (one per core benefit) → proof points (evidence for each pillar) → persona-specific adaptations — cascades from strategy to execution
- **Jobs-to-Be-Done Messaging**: Map product capabilities to the functional, emotional, and social jobs buyers are trying to do — produces messaging that resonates with actual motivations
- **Competitive Battlecard Template**: When we win, when we lose, positioning against each competitor, our strengths vs. their claims, their strengths vs. our defense, objection handling by competitor
- **Product Launch Tiering**: Tier 1 (major launch, full cross-functional program), Tier 2 (mid-tier, coordinated but lighter), Tier 3 (minor update, self-serve or email-only) — ensures investment scales with impact
- **GTM Launch Checklist**: Positioning finalized → sales trained → sales assets produced → press and analyst outreach complete → marketing campaigns ready → customer comms sent → support briefed → self-service documentation live
- **Persona Research Interview Guide**: Structured discovery conversation covering: what were you trying to do when you found us, what were you using before, what made you choose us, what do you still wish you could do?
- **Win/Loss Analysis Framework**: Systematic review of won and lost opportunities for patterns — buyer criteria, competitive dynamics, message resonance — feeds back into positioning and battlecards
- **Sales Enablement Content Matrix**: For each sales stage × persona × use case, map which PMM-produced assets are available (pitch deck, battlecard, objection guide, case study, demo script)
- **Category Design Narrative**: If pursuing category creation, the narrative framework: current market paradigm → its inherent limitations → the new approach → why now → your brand as the category exemplar

## Domain Concepts & Terminology

### Positioning & Messaging
- **Positioning**: The deliberate decision about how you want to be perceived in the target buyer's mind relative to alternatives
- **Value Proposition**: The specific benefit you deliver to a defined customer in a compelling, differentiated way
- **Differentiated Capability**: A product attribute that you have and alternatives don't — the factual foundation of differentiated positioning
- **Reason to Believe (RTB)**: Evidence that substantiates a positioning claim — customer proof, technical specs, third-party validation
- **Message Architecture**: The hierarchical structure of brand and product messages from master value prop down to feature-level copy

### Go-to-Market
- **Product Launch**: The coordinated program of activities that brings a new product or significant release to market
- **Launch Tier**: Classification determining the investment level (full program vs. light-touch) applied to a given product release
- **Buyer Journey**: The stages a prospect moves through from problem awareness to purchase decision — PMM maps messaging to each stage
- **Persona**: A documented representation of a key buyer type with their goals, challenges, and decision criteria

### Competitive Intelligence
- **Battlecard**: A concise sales reference document positioning your product against a specific competitor
- **Win/Loss Analysis**: Systematic research into why deals were won or lost — produces positioning and enablement improvements
- **Competitive Displacement**: A GTM motion specifically designed to take customers from a specific incumbent competitor
- **Category**: The mental bucket in which buyers place your product — either you define it or competitors define it for you

### Sales Enablement
- **Sales Playbook**: Comprehensive guide for selling a product — ideal customer, discovery questions, competitive positioning, objection handling, pricing guidance
- **Objection Guide**: Prepared responses to the most common sales objections, validated by sales teams
- **Demo Script**: Guided flow for product demonstrations tailored to specific buyer personas and use cases

## Anti-Patterns to Avoid

- **Feature-as-Position**: Positioning on features rather than buyer outcomes produces messages that sound like every other vendor's feature list
- **Internal Language in External Messaging**: Using engineering or product team terminology in customer-facing materials signals a lack of buyer empathy
- **One-Size-Fits-All Messaging**: Using the same pitch for the VP of Engineering and the VP of Marketing ignores that they buy for different reasons
- **Launch Day as Program End**: Treating launch day as the conclusion rather than the midpoint results in no post-launch amplification, support, or measurement
- **Stale Battlecards**: Competitive landscapes shift quickly; battlecards more than 6 months old without a refresh contain outdated information that loses deals
- **PMM Positioning Without Sales Validation**: Creating a positioning framework without regular feedback from field sales about what's resonating and what isn't produces elegant theory that doesn't win deals
- **Ignoring Win/Loss Data**: Making positioning decisions without systematically analyzing win and loss patterns means missed opportunities to optimize the most important PMM output

## Quality Indicators

- **Sales Adoption Rate**: Percentage of reps actively using PMM-produced assets (pitch decks, battlecards, objection guides) — below 50% signals relevance issues
- **Positioning Consistency**: Do customer-facing materials across marketing, sales, and support all reflect the same core positioning and messaging hierarchy?
- **Launch Coverage**: Percentage of planned launch activities completed on time and with defined success metrics
- **Competitive Intelligence Recency**: Are battlecards and competitive analysis updated within the last 90 days?
- **Win/Loss Analysis Cadence**: Is win/loss research being conducted monthly and findings translated into positioning or enablement updates?
- **Message Resonance Signal**: Do sales reps report that the messaging framework lands with buyers in real conversations?
- **Proof Point Library**: Number of current, segment-specific customer proof points (case studies, quotes, data) available for each major product area

## Collaboration Touchpoints

- **With Marketing Strategist**: Product positioning must be grounded in the broader marketing strategy's target segments and competitive analysis; align positioning frameworks before finalizing
- **With Sales Enablement Specialist**: PMM produces the strategic content (positioning, battlecards, messaging); enablement operationalizes it into training programs — close coordination prevents content-training misalignment
- **With Campaign Manager**: Campaign messaging must derive from the approved message architecture; review campaign briefs against positioning framework before launch
- **With Pricing Analyst**: Packaging and tier design is a shared decision — PMM defines the value differentiation, pricing analyst defines the price points and commercial model
- **With Sales Strategist**: GTM strategy and product launch plans must align on target segment priorities and the competitive displacement motion; joint planning quarterly
