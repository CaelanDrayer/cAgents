# Best Practices: Sales Trainer

> Design principles, patterns, and frameworks that guide high-quality sales training program design, coaching delivery, and skill development work.

## Design Principles

- **Behavioral Change is the Measure, Not Knowledge Transfer**: The goal of training is different behavior in the field, not higher quiz scores
- **Practice Over Presentation**: Role-play, scenario exercises, and call coaching produce skill improvement; lectures produce awareness at best
- **Train for the Real Conversation**: Every training scenario should reflect the actual buyer conversations reps will have — abstract case studies disconnect from field application
- **Spaced Repetition Beats Bootcamp**: A skill practiced repeatedly over 8 weeks develops more durably than the same hours concentrated in one week
- **Manager Enablement Multiplies Impact**: Training that equips managers to coach is more scalable than training delivered directly to all reps
- **Measure Outcomes, Not Participation**: Training completion rates are a leading indicator; win rate and ramp velocity are the outcomes that matter
- **Continuous Improvement Requires Feedback Loops**: Training programs that don't incorporate field feedback from reps and managers become irrelevant within 6 months

## Key Patterns & Frameworks

- **Adult Learning Model (ADDIE)**: Analysis (what skill gap? what outcome?) → Design (learning objectives, format) → Development (content, scenarios) → Implementation (delivery, practice) → Evaluation (skill assessment, outcome measurement)
- **Kirkpatrick Training Evaluation Model**: Level 1 (Reaction — did reps find it useful?), Level 2 (Learning — did they acquire the skill?), Level 3 (Behavior — are they applying it?), Level 4 (Results — did it improve revenue metrics?)
- **Sales Onboarding Ramp Structure**: Week 1 (company, product, market context) → Week 2–3 (sales methodology, discovery techniques) → Week 4 (demo certification) → Month 2 (shadowing + supervised calls + coaching) → Month 3 (first independent deals with manager support)
- **Role-Play Scoring Rubric**: Defined competency criteria for each skill (active listening, discovery question quality, objection handling, close technique) with observable behavioral indicators at each performance level
- **Coaching Conversation Framework**: Manager coaching model — Observe (call recording or shadow) → Data (specific behavioral observation) → Impact (connection to deal outcome) → Question (invite rep reflection) → Plan (agreed improvement action)
- **Sales Certification Program**: Structured assessment where reps demonstrate mastery of key skills before being certified to sell specific products, enter specific segments, or advance to senior roles
- **Blended Learning Architecture**: Self-paced e-learning (knowledge) + live workshop (application) + role-play (practice) + coaching (reinforcement) + performance review (accountability) — multiple modalities for durable skill development
- **Call Scoring Card**: Standardized rubric for evaluating recorded sales calls against key competencies — used by managers and trainers for coaching consistency
- **Peer Learning Program**: Structured format for top performers to share their approaches with the team — recorded call reviews, win story presentations, peer role-play coaching
- **Skills Gap Assessment Process**: Competency model definition → baseline skill assessment → gap analysis → prioritized training roadmap → progress reassessment at 90-day intervals

## Domain Concepts & Terminology

### Training Design
- **Learning Objective**: A specific, observable behavior the trainee should be able to demonstrate after training — "rep can articulate the top three differentiators against Competitor X in under 60 seconds"
- **Competency Model**: The defined set of skills, knowledge, and behaviors required for success in a specific sales role
- **Curriculum**: The complete sequence of learning experiences organized to develop a defined set of competencies
- **Modality**: The format of learning delivery — instructor-led, e-learning, role-play, coaching, job aid, etc.
- **Scenario-Based Training**: Learning exercises built around realistic sales situations that require applying skills to solve a problem

### Coaching
- **Sales Coaching**: One-on-one or group feedback and guidance from a manager or trainer focused on improving specific skills
- **Call Recording Review**: Analyzing recorded sales conversations to identify coaching opportunities and skill patterns
- **Deal Review**: Structured conversation between manager and rep about a specific active opportunity — combining pipeline management with skill coaching
- **Shadow**: A training method where a newer rep observes an experienced rep's calls or visits — accelerates pattern recognition
- **Peer Coaching**: Structured feedback exchange between two reps at similar performance levels

### Metrics
- **Time to Ramp**: Days from hire to achieving a defined productivity milestone (often 75% of quota) — primary onboarding effectiveness metric
- **Quota Attainment Pre/Post Training**: Comparing win rates and attainment before and after a specific training intervention
- **Training Completion Rate**: Percentage of target reps who completed required training — leading indicator, not outcome metric
- **Assessment Score**: Performance on role-play rubrics or knowledge checks — measures skill acquisition
- **Coaching Frequency**: Average number of structured coaching conversations per rep per month — signal of manager engagement

## Anti-Patterns to Avoid

- **Death by Slide Deck**: Delivering 40-slide product presentations and calling it sales training creates passive attendees who retain 10% of content
- **One-Time Bootcamp Without Reinforcement**: Intensive new hire training without spaced reinforcement produces skill decay within 30 days
- **Certification Without Calibration**: Allowing reps to self-certify or applying inconsistent rubrics produces certification that doesn't reflect actual competency
- **Training Without Manager Buy-In**: Managers who don't reinforce training through coaching undermine everything delivered in the training room
- **Skills Training Without Process Alignment**: Teaching objection handling techniques that contradict the official sales playbook creates rep confusion
- **No Post-Training Outcome Measurement**: Measuring only completion and satisfaction scores without measuring field behavioral change provides no evidence of training effectiveness
- **Ignoring Underperformer Root Cause**: Sending underperforming reps to training without diagnosing whether the issue is skills, motivation, territory, or fit sends the wrong intervention

## Quality Indicators

- **Time-to-First-Deal Trend**: Are successive hire cohorts closing their first deal faster than prior cohorts?
- **Role-Play Certification Pass Rate**: What percentage of reps demonstrate competency on the first role-play certification attempt?
- **Manager Coaching Frequency**: Are sales managers conducting structured coaching conversations at the defined cadence (typically weekly)?
- **90-Day Quota Attainment**: What percentage of reps complete the first 90 days at or above the expected ramp pace?
- **Skill Retention**: Post-training assessment scores at 30 and 60 days vs. immediately post-training — measures retention
- **Training NPS (from Reps)**: Do reps find the training valuable? Scores below 30 indicate relevance or delivery issues
- **Win Rate Delta**: Is there a measurable improvement in win rate for reps who complete specific training programs vs. those who haven't?

## Collaboration Touchpoints

- **With Sales Enablement Specialist**: Trainer delivers the skill development programs; enablement specialist produces the content assets those programs use — tight coordination ensures training and content reinforce each other
- **With Sales Strategist**: Sales methodology choice and competitive strategy must be reflected accurately in training scenarios and role-plays — validate training content against current strategy before delivery
- **With Sales Analyst**: Win/loss data and performance metrics by skill area identify which training investments produce the highest revenue impact
- **With Sales Manager**: Managers are the most important reinforcement mechanism for training; equip them with coaching frameworks and call scoring rubrics that extend training into the daily workflow
- **With Product Marketing Manager**: Product knowledge and competitive positioning content must be current and accurate in all training materials — PMM should review and approve PMM-adjacent training modules
