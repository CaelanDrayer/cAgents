# Best Practices: Prose Stylist

> Design principles, patterns, and frameworks that guide high-quality prose style development, voice refinement, sentence rhythm, imagery, and figurative language work.

## Design Principles

- **Style Is Not Decoration**: Prose style is not ornament applied to content — it is a fundamental aspect of meaning. How something is said is part of what is said. Style choices carry semantic and emotional weight.
- **Voice Is Character**: Every piece of prose has an implied author, and the reader forms a relationship with that implied presence. A consistent, distinctive voice creates trust; an inconsistent or absent voice produces prose that readers cannot attach to.
- **Rhythm Is Felt Before It Is Understood**: Readers experience prose rhythm as pace, energy, and tone before they can articulate it analytically. Sentence length variation, syntactic complexity, and phonetic patterns all produce rhythm effects that precede conscious analysis.
- **Imagery Earns Its Place**: Every image — every comparison, concrete detail, or figurative moment — must do work. Decorative imagery clutters; purposeful imagery illuminates, characterizes, or deepens theme.
- **Simplicity Is Not Poverty**: The hardest thing to write is a simple, clear, beautiful sentence. Complexity is not sophistication; Hemingway is not less accomplished than Faulkner for being simpler. Simplicity serves some subjects; complexity serves others.
- **The Sentence Is the Unit**: Prose style operates at the sentence level. Paragraphs are constructed from sentences; chapters from paragraphs. Excellence at the sentence level is the necessary (if not sufficient) condition for excellent prose.
- **Show, Don't Tell Has Limits**: "Show don't tell" is a useful heuristic, not a law. Some things are better told; some narrative distances require summary; some emotional states are more effectively stated than enacted. The skill is knowing which approach serves each moment.

## Key Patterns & Frameworks

- **Sentence Rhythm Variation System**: Identify the dominant sentence pattern in a passage and deliberately introduce structural variation — length variation (very short following very long), syntactic variation (periodic following cumulative), and complexity variation (simple following subordinate). Monotonous sentence rhythm is the most common prose quality problem.
- **The Three Prose Registers**: (1) Close (intimate distance, present moment, sensory and internal access), (2) Middle (narrative distance, selective access, controlled pace), (3) Far (summarizing distance, compression, retrospective framing). Every sentence operates in one of these registers. Unintentional register shifts are the source of much prose awkwardness.
- **Image Quality Assessment**: For every image in a passage, evaluate — (1) Is it visual? (Does it make something seen?), (2) Is it specific? (A Ford Taurus, not a car), (3) Is it fresh? (Not a used metaphor), (4) Does it do work? (Does it add meaning or characterization, not just decoration?). Images that fail all four criteria should be cut or replaced.
- **The Concrete-Abstract Ladder**: Abstract statements are weaker than specific statements. Build the habit of asking "can this abstract claim be made specific?" for every generalizing sentence. The more abstract, the weaker the sentence; the more specific, the stronger.
- **Figurative Language Taxonomy**: Metaphor (identity), simile (comparison), metonymy (substitution of attribute for entity), synecdoche (part for whole), personification (human qualities to non-human things). Each creates different effects; each is appropriate in different contexts. Habitual reliance on simile misses the subtler tools.
- **Voice Consistency Audit**: Read the entire piece and mark every sentence that sounds like a different person wrote it. Voice breaks are typically caused by register inconsistency, vocabulary range inconsistency, or syntactic habit inconsistency. The audit identifies where the implied author temporarily disappears.
- **The Rhythm Test**: Read any passage aloud. Stumbling points indicate rhythm problems. Passages that could be cut to silence indicate verbal waste. Passages you want to hear again indicate rhythmic success.

## Domain Concepts & Terminology

### Sentence Architecture
- **Periodic Sentence**: A sentence that withholds the main clause until the end; creates anticipation and releases tension at the period
- **Cumulative Sentence**: Main clause first, followed by elaborating modifiers; releases information progressively
- **Fragment**: An incomplete sentence used for emphasis or rhythmic effect; legitimate when purposeful, an error when accidental
- **Coordination**: Joining clauses with "and," "but," "or"; creates parallel structure and balanced rhythm
- **Subordination**: Embedding one clause within another; creates complexity and hierarchy of emphasis

### Voice and Register
- **Voice**: The implied author's presence in the prose — personality, intelligence, attitude, and way of perceiving the world
- **Narrative Distance**: How close the narration is to the character's immediate experience; ranges from intimate (close third, stream of consciousness) to distant (omniscient summary)
- **Free Indirect Discourse**: Narrative mode that blends third-person narration with the character's interior voice without quotation marks or attribution
- **Register**: The formality level of language; mismatch between content formality and register formality is a common voice problem
- **Idiolect**: The distinctive vocabulary, syntax, and rhetorical habits that characterize a specific voice

### Figurative Language
- **Metaphor**: Direct statement of identity between two unlike things ("life is a journey")
- **Simile**: Comparison using "like" or "as" ("life is like a journey"); more tentative than metaphor
- **Metonymy**: Substituting an attribute or associated element for the thing itself ("the Crown" for the monarchy)
- **Synecdoche**: Using a part to represent the whole or the whole to represent a part ("boots on the ground" for soldiers)
- **Extended Metaphor / Conceit**: A single metaphor developed across multiple sentences or an entire passage

### Prose Quality Markers
- **Concrete Detail**: Specific, particular, sensory information (not "she wore a coat" but "she wore her grandmother's green wool coat, the one with the missing button")
- **Abstraction**: Generalized, non-specific claim that weakens prose when used to replace specific detail
- **Dead Metaphor**: A figurative expression so overused that its figurative nature is no longer felt ("a bright idea")
- **Purple Prose**: Overly ornate, excessively elaborate prose that calls attention to its own style rather than serving the content
- **Nominalization**: Converting verbs into nouns ("make a decision" instead of "decide"); weakens prose energy

## Anti-Patterns to Avoid

- **Nominalization Habit**: Systematically converting strong verbs into weak verb-noun combinations ("conduct an investigation" instead of "investigate"); drains prose of energy.
- **Passive Voice Overuse**: Defaulting to passive constructions ("the door was opened") instead of active ("she opened the door"); removes agency and energy unless the passive is specifically purposeful.
- **Adverb Dependence**: Using adverbs to compensate for weak verbs ("she ran quickly" instead of "she sprinted"); adverbs are typically symptoms of imprecise verb choice.
- **Adjective Stacking**: Two or more adjectives before a noun; usually indicates that one strong specific adjective should replace two vague ones.
- **Mixed Metaphor**: Combining incompatible figurative comparisons in a single passage ("he took the bull by the horns and ran with it"); undermines imagery coherence.
- **Rhythm Monotony**: Writing extended passages in the same sentence length and structure; produces lulling, undifferentiated prose regardless of content interest.
- **Abstract Statement Without Concrete Support**: Making general claims without grounding them in specific, sensory, or concrete detail; "the room was depressing" without the specific details that make it depressing.

## Quality Indicators

- **Sentence Length Variation**: Passage analysis shows meaningful variation in sentence length — not uniform, not randomly varied, but varied in response to content and rhythm intent
- **Image Specificity**: Concrete images use specific rather than generic nouns and verbs (the Ford Taurus, not the car; she sprinted, not she ran fast)
- **Voice Consistency**: Voice audit marks no more than 10% of sentences as possible voice breaks
- **Active Construction Predominance**: Active constructions outnumber passive constructions by at least 3:1, with passive used deliberately for specific effects
- **Fresh Figurative Language**: Less than 10% of figurative expressions are dead metaphors or commonly repeated comparisons
- **Register Coherence**: All sentences in a passage operate in the same register or make intentional, purposeful register shifts
- **Rhythm Audit Success**: Reading the passage aloud produces no stumbling, no urge to cut, and at least one moment of wanting to hear a sentence again

## Collaboration Touchpoints

- **With Editor**: Editor addresses structural and voice issues at a macro level; prose stylist implements sentence-level refinements after structural editing is complete. Prose stylists should not begin line-level work until structural issues are resolved.
- **With Dialogue Specialist**: Dialogue specialist writes spoken language; prose stylist works on narration. The two must coordinate on how narration and dialogue interact rhythmically and on maintaining tonal coherence across the register shift between speech and narration.
- **With Voice Coach**: Voice coach defines the target voice profile for a piece or author; prose stylist implements that profile at the sentence level. The two roles require close coordination to ensure technical prose choices embody the voice.
- **With Narrative Director**: Director holds the creative vision; prose stylist serves that vision at the sentence level. When tonal and rhythmic choices are consequential to the work's overall effect, prose stylist presents options for directorial selection.
