# Continuity Verification Checklists and Tracking Templates

## Master Continuity Checklist

### Character Continuity

**Names and Identification**:
- [ ] Every character's name is spelled consistently throughout
- [ ] Nicknames, titles, and modes of address are consistent per relationship
- [ ] No two characters share confusingly similar names (unless intentional)
- [ ] Character introductions are clear (no ambiguous "he said" with two male characters present)

**Physical Descriptions**:
- [ ] Eye color consistent across all mentions
- [ ] Hair color/style consistent (or changes are noted in narrative)
- [ ] Height, build, distinguishing features consistent
- [ ] Age matches timeline (a 34-year-old in 2024 was born ~1990, not 1985)
- [ ] Scars, tattoos, and permanent marks persist once established
- [ ] Physical limitations remain consistent (a character with a limp still limps)

**Knowledge and Awareness**:
- [ ] Characters only act on information they've received on-page or plausibly off-page
- [ ] Secrets remain secret until explicitly revealed
- [ ] Characters who were absent during events don't reference those events without learning about them
- [ ] Memory and knowledge states are appropriate to the character (a child doesn't recall events from age 2)

**Abilities and Skills**:
- [ ] Skills established early are available later (a character who can pick locks in ch.3 can still pick locks in ch.20)
- [ ] New skills have a plausible acquisition path (training, natural talent established, etc.)
- [ ] Skills don't appear or vanish based on plot convenience
- [ ] Physical capabilities are consistent with established attributes (a small, untrained character shouldn't overpower a trained fighter without explanation)

**Relationships**:
- [ ] Relationship dynamics are consistent with established history
- [ ] Changes in relationships are motivated by on-page events
- [ ] Characters react appropriately to relationship status (enemies don't suddenly cooperate without reason)
- [ ] Family trees are internally consistent

### Timeline Continuity

**Chronological Tracking**:
- [ ] Every chapter can be placed on a timeline
- [ ] Time gaps between scenes/chapters are plausible
- [ ] Cumulative time matches larger timeline markers ("three months later" actually sums to ~90 days)
- [ ] Day of week references are accurate (if Monday in ch.1, "two days later" should be Wednesday)
- [ ] Time of day is consistent within continuous scenes

**Duration Verification**:
- [ ] Travel times match established distances
- [ ] Healing times are medically plausible (or magic/technology explains faster healing)
- [ ] Pregnancy durations are accurate (~40 weeks)
- [ ] Growth and aging are appropriate to time elapsed
- [ ] Seasonal markers match the timeline (events in "November" should have autumnal/winter setting)
- [ ] Deadlines and countdowns count down accurately

**Concurrent Events**:
- [ ] Events happening simultaneously in different locations are temporally consistent
- [ ] Time zones are accounted for in cross-location communication
- [ ] "Meanwhile" transitions maintain timeline coherence
- [ ] Characters in different plotlines can't meet if they're simultaneously in distant locations

### World Logic

**Magic/Technology Systems**:
- [ ] Rules established for the system are followed in every instance
- [ ] Costs/limitations of magic/technology are consistently enforced
- [ ] Exceptions to rules are explained, not ignored
- [ ] Power levels are consistent (a spell that exhausts the mage in ch.5 shouldn't be cast casually in ch.15 without explanation)
- [ ] Newly introduced capabilities don't contradict earlier limitations

**Geography and Setting**:
- [ ] Distances between locations are consistent
- [ ] Maps (if provided) match narrative descriptions
- [ ] Terrain and climate match geography (no tropical forests at high altitude without explanation)
- [ ] Building layouts remain consistent within scenes
- [ ] Compass directions are consistent (if the river is east of town in ch.2, it's still east in ch.22)

**Political and Social Rules**:
- [ ] Government structures operate consistently
- [ ] Laws and customs are applied consistently (or inconsistency is addressed as corruption/favoritism)
- [ ] Social hierarchies are maintained or changed through in-story events
- [ ] Cultural practices established early are maintained

**Economic Logic**:
- [ ] Prices and values are internally consistent
- [ ] Character wealth matches their spending
- [ ] Resources (food, water, fuel) are consumed realistically on long journeys
- [ ] Economic systems are plausible (or explicitly fantastical)

### Object Tracking

**Significant Items**:
- [ ] Every significant object has a clear location at all times
- [ ] Objects don't teleport between scenes (unless the world allows teleportation)
- [ ] Objects that are destroyed/lost don't reappear
- [ ] Objects retain their established properties
- [ ] Ammunition, supplies, and consumables are tracked (a six-shooter doesn't fire eight shots)

**Outfit and Appearance**:
- [ ] Characters in continuous scenes maintain their clothing
- [ ] Weather-appropriate clothing is worn (or its absence is noted)
- [ ] Disguises are maintained or removed at logical points
- [ ] Items carried (bags, weapons, tools) are consistently present or accounted for

### Plot Logic

**Cause and Effect**:
- [ ] Every major event has a cause
- [ ] Consequences of events persist (a fire in ch.5 leaves damage in ch.6)
- [ ] Character decisions are motivated by their established knowledge and personality
- [ ] Coincidences are limited and don't drive the resolution

**Setup and Payoff**:
- [ ] Every prominently introduced element either pays off or serves clear atmospheric purpose
- [ ] Every payoff has an earlier setup
- [ ] Foreshadowing is consistent (doesn't hint at events that don't occur)
- [ ] Prophecies, promises, and plans are fulfilled, subverted, or explicitly abandoned

**Thread Resolution**:
- [ ] Every subplot reaches a resolution (even if it's deliberate ambiguity)
- [ ] Every question raised for the reader is eventually answered
- [ ] Character arcs reach completion or clear continuation points
- [ ] Mystery clues are fair (the reader has access to the information needed for the solution)

## Severity Rating Guide

### Critical (Must Fix)
- Dead characters appearing alive without resurrection mechanism
- Major plot events contradicting each other
- Character using knowledge they cannot possess (and it drives the plot)
- Timeline impossibilities (character in two places simultaneously)
- World rules flagrantly violated (teleportation when established as impossible)

### Major (Should Fix)
- Character name spelling changes (Katerina/Katarina)
- Timeline arithmetic errors ("three days later" that should be five)
- Object location inconsistencies (sword appears without being retrieved)
- Chekhov's guns that never fire (prominent setup, no payoff)
- Character age inconsistencies

### Minor (Consider Fixing)
- Eye/hair color discrepancies
- Minor physical description inconsistencies
- Small timeline ambiguities (not errors, just unclear)
- Outfit continuity in extended scenes
- Minor world-rule edge cases

### Note (For Author Awareness)
- Potential issues depending on author intent
- Ambiguities that might confuse some readers
- Near-inconsistencies that are technically fine but feel off
- Suggestions for strengthening continuity

## Special Cases

### Unreliable Narrator
When the narrator is established as unreliable:
- Track BOTH the narrator's version and the "true" version (as implied by text)
- Inconsistencies may be intentional -- check whether they serve the unreliability
- The author should have a clear map of what's true vs. what the narrator claims
- Flag only if an inconsistency seems unintentional (doesn't serve the unreliability)

### Multiple Drafts
When checking continuity across revisions:
- Changes in one chapter may create new inconsistencies in others
- Track what changed in the revision and cross-reference against the full manuscript
- Deleted scenes may leave orphaned references ("Remember what happened at the lake?" -- but the lake scene was cut)

### Series Bible Additions
For multi-book series:
- Maintain a master bible that spans all books
- Track character development trajectory (a character who overcame cowardice in Book 1 shouldn't be cowardly in Book 2 without regression explanation)
- Track world-state changes (if a city was destroyed in Book 2, it's still destroyed in Book 3)
- Note potential retcons for author awareness
