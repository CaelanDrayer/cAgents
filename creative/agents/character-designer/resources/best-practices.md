# Best Practices: Character Designer

> Design principles, patterns, and frameworks that guide high-quality character creation, psychological depth, and ensemble dynamic work.

## Design Principles

- **Want vs. Need**: Every compelling character wants something they believe will fix their life (external goal) and needs something different that will actually fix them (internal wound). The gap between want and need is where the story lives.
- **Wound Drives Everything**: The character's formative wound shapes their worldview, their defenses, their relationship patterns, and their arc. Design from the wound outward.
- **Contradiction Creates Dimension**: Real people hold contradictory qualities — the brave character who fears intimacy, the cynic who secretly hopes. A character without contradiction is a type, not a person.
- **Character Reveals Through Choice**: Character is not what people say about themselves or what the narrative tells us; it is what they choose when choosing is costly. Design the character through their choices, then work backward to psychology.
- **Ensemble Dynamics Are Relational**: Characters are not just individuals; they are nodes in a network. Each character's role in the ensemble should create productive friction and resonance with the others.
- **Arc Requires Genuine Change**: A character arc is not just events happening to a character — it is a fundamental change in the character's worldview, behavior, or self-understanding. Trace the arc through before/after comparison.
- **Specificity Over Universality**: A character's specific details (the food they eat, their nervous habits, their opinion of dogs) make them feel real. Universal archetypes are starting points, not destinations.

## Key Patterns & Frameworks

- **Wound/Want/Need Framework**: The primary character design architecture. Wound = formative damage; Want = what the character believes they need (often external, often wrong); Need = what will actually heal them (often internal, often what they resist). The arc is the journey from Want toward Need, powered by the Wound.
- **Ghost and Story**: The Ghost is the specific event or series of events in the character's past that created the wound and defined their worldview. The Story is the present narrative that forces them to confront it. The Ghost must be specific enough to generate specific behavior.
- **Character Arc Typology**: Positive arc (character overcomes flaw/wound, achieves need), negative arc (character refuses to change, corruption or tragedy), flat arc (character is already complete, story tests their values), and anti-change arc (character appears to change but reverts). Each serves different narrative purposes.
- **Enneagram as Diagnostic Tool**: Nine personality types with core fears, core desires, defense mechanisms, and growth/stress directions. Use as a diagnostic to identify consistent behavioral logic, not as a box to trap the character in.
- **Myers-Briggs Functional Stack**: Use the cognitive function stack (dominant, auxiliary, tertiary, inferior) to model how the character processes information and makes decisions under stress. The inferior function is the character's blind spot and often their greatest vulnerability.
- **Mask and Face Architecture**: Characters present a public face (the mask they wear in the world) that protects their private face (their true self). The mask is designed to prevent the core wound from being exposed. Dramatic revelation is often the mask slipping.
- **Ensemble Role Mapping**: Define each character's function in the ensemble — Protagonist, Antagonist, Mentor, Trickster, Shadow, Shapeshifter, Threshold Guardian — and ensure each role serves the story's thematic and structural needs.
- **Relationship Geometry**: Map characters in relationship pairs and triangles. Each relationship should reveal a different facet of each character. Ensemble dynamics emerge from the geometric arrangement of relationships.
- **Voice Differentiation Protocol**: Each character should have a distinct vocabulary range, sentence structure preference, primary metaphor domain, and conversational habit. Voice distinction is the fingerprint of a fully realized character.

## Domain Concepts & Terminology

### Psychological Architecture
- **Core Wound**: The formative psychological damage that defines the character's worldview, defenses, and relational patterns
- **Core Fear**: What the character's worldview is organized to avoid; the deepest dread beneath the surface behavior
- **Core Desire**: What the character believes will resolve their wound; the driving motivation beneath all behavior
- **Defense Mechanism**: The psychological strategy the character uses to avoid confronting their wound (intellectualization, projection, humor, aggression, withdrawal)
- **Character Flaw**: A behavior or belief pattern that stems from the wound and that the story will challenge; the area of potential growth

### Arc Mechanics
- **Inciting Wound**: The specific event that created the wound; the Ghost made concrete
- **Lie the Character Believes**: The false worldview or self-concept created by the wound; what they must unlearn to achieve their need
- **Truth the Story Teaches**: The thematic argument the story makes through the character's arc; the corrective to the Lie
- **Character Change Moment**: The specific scene where genuine change is demonstrated through behavior, not just realization

### Ensemble Dynamics
- **Foil**: A character whose contrasting qualities highlight specific traits in the character being foiled
- **Mirror Character**: A character who represents what the protagonist could become — either with positive change (aspirational mirror) or without it (cautionary mirror)
- **Thematic Double**: A character exploring the same theme from a different angle or with a different outcome
- **Ensemble Function**: The role a character plays in the group dynamic — stabilizer, provocateur, peacemaker, disruptor

### Voice & Behavior Markers
- **Verbal Tic**: Characteristic speech pattern that identifies the character — word preference, rhythm, question frequency
- **Behavioral Tell**: Specific, repeated physical behavior that signals the character's emotional state (pacing when anxious, going quiet when hurt)
- **Relationship Mode**: How the character typically engages in relationships — protective, competitive, dependent, avoidant, nurturing

## Anti-Patterns to Avoid

- **The Backstory Dump**: Inserting the character's wound as expository backstory rather than revealing it through behavior and present choices; backstory belongs in the Ghost, not in dialogue.
- **Static Perfection**: Creating a character who is admirable but not in genuine conflict with their own limitations; perfect characters are uninteresting because they cannot grow.
- **The Wound as Excuse**: Treating the character's wound as sufficient justification for all their behavior without exploring their agency and responsibility for change.
- **Ensemble Redundancy**: Designing two characters with identical wounds, arcs, and ensemble functions; redundant characters should be merged or differentiated.
- **Arc Without Change**: A character going through dramatic events without genuinely changing their worldview or behavior is not an arc — it is plot happening to a statue.
- **Type Over Person**: Defaulting to archetype or trope without adding the specific, individual details that transform a type into a person.
- **Articulated Psychology**: Characters who explain their psychology in dialogue ("As someone with abandonment issues, I tend to push people away") rather than demonstrating it through behavior.

## Quality Indicators

- **Want/Need Distinction**: The character's want and need can be stated separately and are meaningfully different from each other
- **Wound Specificity**: The core wound can be described as a specific event or pattern, not just a general quality ("feels unloved")
- **Arc Traceability**: The character's change can be tracked through at least three specific scenes showing before/during/after the arc
- **Voice Distinctiveness**: The character's dialogue is distinguishable from other ensemble members without dialogue tags
- **Contradiction Presence**: At least two genuinely contradictory qualities that generate productive internal tension
- **Behavioral Consistency**: The character's choices throughout the narrative are legible as expressions of their wound, want, and need
- **Ensemble Differentiation**: Each character in the ensemble has a distinct function, voice, and arc that does not duplicate another's

## Collaboration Touchpoints

- **With Character Psychologist**: Character psychologist performs diagnostic analysis of character behavior patterns; character designer uses those diagnostics to refine wound architecture and ensure behavioral consistency
- **With Dialogue Specialist**: Character designer provides voice profiles (vocabulary range, sentence structure, speech habits) that dialogue specialist translates into specific dialogue
- **With Story Architect**: Story architect builds the structural scaffolding that character arcs must inhabit; character designer must ensure arc timing aligns with structural beats (midpoint, dark night, climax)
- **With Narrative Director**: Director maintains the creative vision that all character designs must serve; character designer presents designs for tonal and thematic alignment review
