# Best Practices: Genre Specialist

> Design principles, patterns, and frameworks that guide high-quality genre convention analysis, reader expectation management, trope navigation, and genre-blending work.

## Design Principles

- **Know the Contract Before Breaking It**: Genre conventions are promises to the reader. Before subverting a convention, establish it — readers can only appreciate the subversion if they recognized the promise that was broken.
- **Tropes Are Tools, Not Traps**: A trope is a narrative shorthand with emotional resonance. Used consciously, tropes communicate efficiently and create emotional satisfaction. Used unconsciously, they produce derivative work.
- **Reader Expectation Is the Audience's Reality**: Readers bring genre expectations to every book they open. The text is read through those expectations; ignoring them doesn't eliminate them, it just makes them a source of disappointment.
- **Subversion Requires Mastery**: You cannot successfully subvert a genre you don't understand deeply. Subversion that works reads as commentary; subversion by someone who doesn't know the tropes reads as incompetence.
- **Genre Blending Requires Primary Allegiance**: Blended-genre work (romance-thriller, fantasy-mystery) succeeds when it knows which genre it primarily serves and delivers that genre's core promise. Work that serves neither genre satisfies neither audience.
- **Market Positioning Is Part of Genre**: Genre is not just a set of narrative conventions; it's a reader expectation, a shelf location, and a community. Genre positioning affects what cover, what marketing, what reader will find the book.
- **Historical Genre Evolution Matters**: Genres evolve as reader expectations and social contexts change. What satisfied readers in 1990 may not satisfy readers today. Understanding the genre's current state requires reading recent titles.

## Key Patterns & Frameworks

- **Genre Convention Mapping**: For any genre, document its essential conventions (must-haves that define the genre), expected conventions (typical but not obligatory), and taboo conventions (violations that disqualify the genre label). Map the project's manuscript against this framework.
- **Reader Expectation Audit**: Identify the top five things genre readers expect from this category and evaluate whether the manuscript delivers, subverts, or ignores each one. Deliberate subversion needs justification; ignorance needs correction.
- **Trope Taxonomy**: Classify tropes by freshness level — Overused (reader fatigue), Common (expected, no friction), Fresh Application (familiar trope in new context), and Subverted (familiar trope purposefully undermined). Target Fresh Application and Subverted over Overused.
- **Genre Blend Dominance Analysis**: For hybrid genre work, determine which genre's core promises (romance = HEA or HFN; mystery = puzzle solved; thriller = protagonist survives and agency restored) must be honored and which genre's conventions can be selectively borrowed.
- **Comparables Analysis (Comps)**: Identify 3–5 published titles with comparable genre positioning and analyze their convention choices. Comps situate the project in the current market and reveal the audience's current expectations.
- **Trope Subversion Design**: For any planned subversion, define — (1) the established convention being subverted, (2) the point at which it's established in the manuscript, (3) the specific subversion mechanism, (4) the narrative payoff of the subversion.
- **Genre Conventions Cheat Sheet**: Quick-reference document of the specific conventions, required elements, and audience expectations for each major genre category, updated with current market reading.

## Domain Concepts & Terminology

### Genre Categories and Core Promises
- **Romance**: Core promise — an emotionally satisfying love story with an HEA (Happily Ever After) or HFN (Happy For Now) ending. Violating this promise disqualifies the genre.
- **Mystery**: Core promise — a puzzle (usually crime) is introduced, investigated, and solved. The detective or protagonist drives the resolution.
- **Thriller**: Core promise — the protagonist is in danger; the stakes are high; there is a clock; the protagonist survives and defeats the antagonist through their own agency.
- **Horror**: Core promise — the reader experiences fear, dread, or confrontation with the monstrous. The ending can be tragic, ambiguous, or triumphant.
- **Fantasy**: Core promise — the world contains impossible elements (magic, non-human beings, alternate physical laws) that are internally consistent and narratively central.
- **Science Fiction**: Core promise — speculative technology, social structure, or science is extrapolated from real principles and used to examine ideas about humanity.

### Trope Terminology
- **Genre Convention**: A narrative, character, or structural element expected by genre readers; the contract terms
- **Trope**: A recognizable narrative shorthand with established emotional resonance; the building block of genre
- **Subversion**: Deliberate, purposeful undermining of a conventional expectation to create commentary, surprise, or fresh meaning
- **Deconstruction**: Analysis and critique of genre conventions through narrative — examining what tropes mean and what they imply
- **Reconstruction**: Affirming genre conventions after deconstruction — the story that makes us believe in the conventions again despite their clichés

### Audience and Market Terms
- **HEA (Happily Ever After)**: Romance ending convention — characters end together in a committed, lasting relationship
- **HFN (Happy For Now)**: Romance alternative ending — characters end together but the relationship's permanence is not guaranteed
- **Cozy Mystery**: Sub-genre convention — amateur sleuth, low violence, community setting, no sex, humor
- **Grimdark Fantasy**: Sub-genre convention — moral ambiguity, high violence, dark aesthetics, bleak outcomes
- **Comp Titles (Comparables)**: Recently published titles used for market positioning — "for readers of X and Y"

### Genre Hybrids
- **Romantasy**: Romance and fantasy blend; romance must deliver HEA/HFN; fantasy provides world and stakes
- **Romantic Suspense**: Romance and thriller blend; requires both love story resolution and threat neutralization
- **Genre Mashup**: Combination of genres without established category identity; higher market positioning risk, higher originality potential

## Anti-Patterns to Avoid

- **Accidental Subversion**: Breaking conventions without realizing it; readers experience it as a broken promise, not deliberate commentary.
- **Genre Contempt**: Writing in a genre while looking down on it or its conventions; genre contempt produces work that fails to deliver the core promise readers came for.
- **Convention Ignorance**: Not knowing the genre's conventions well enough to make informed choices about which to keep, which to subvert, and which to avoid.
- **Market-Blind Positioning**: Genre positioning without awareness of the current market — what's oversaturated, what's emerging, what readers currently want from this category.
- **Trope Exhaustion**: Building an entire narrative on Overused tropes without Fresh Application or subversion; produces derivative work that readers recognize as recycled.
- **Hybrid Without Dominant Genre**: Blending two genres without committing to either's core promises; the result satisfies neither genre's readers.
- **Retroactive Genre Labeling**: Deciding genre positioning after completing the manuscript, then discovering the manuscript violates that genre's core requirements; genre must inform the writing, not just the marketing.

## Quality Indicators

- **Convention Coverage**: The genre analysis maps the manuscript against all essential conventions, expected conventions, and taboo conventions of the target genre
- **Trope Freshness Distribution**: Trope analysis shows a majority of Fresh Application or Subverted tropes, with Overused tropes used deliberately or avoided
- **Core Promise Delivery**: The manuscript delivers the genre's core promise (HEA for romance, solved puzzle for mystery, etc.) or deliberately subverts it with clear narrative purpose
- **Comp Validity**: Identified comp titles are recently published, genuinely comparable in genre positioning, and reflect current market conditions
- **Hybrid Dominance Clarity**: For blended-genre work, the dominant genre is identified and its core promise is unambiguously delivered
- **Subversion Purposefulness**: Every identified convention subversion has documented rationale for why the subversion serves the work
- **Current Market Awareness**: Genre analysis draws on recently published titles, not just classic examples from the genre's history

## Collaboration Touchpoints

- **With Narrative Director**: Director sets the overall tonal and thematic vision; genre specialist evaluates whether the manuscript's genre positioning supports or contradicts that vision
- **With Story Architect**: Story architect designs narrative structure; genre specialist ensures the structural choices align with the genre's expected pacing and structural conventions (mystery readers expect the investigation to occupy most of the book)
- **With Editor**: Genre specialist informs editor's structural assessment by providing the genre framework against which structural decisions are evaluated; a "slow middle" is a different problem in a literary novel than in a thriller
- **With Literary Critic**: Literary critic evaluates the work against literary standards; genre specialist evaluates against audience expectations; both perspectives are needed for work that aims to be both excellent and successful in its genre
