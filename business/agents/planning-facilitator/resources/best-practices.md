# Best Practices: Planning Facilitator

> Design principles, patterns, and frameworks that guide high-quality strategic planning facilitation, workshop design, consensus building, and group decision-making work.

## Design Principles

- **Design the Container, Not the Content**: The facilitator's job is to create conditions for great thinking — not to have the right answers. Own the process; let participants own the content.
- **Diverge Before Converging**: Great group decisions require full exploration before narrowing. Rushing to consensus without divergence produces anchoring to the first idea voiced.
- **Every Voice Deserves Airtime**: Loud voices and senior titles naturally dominate groups — structured facilitation techniques actively create space for quieter or junior participants.
- **Conflict Is Data**: Disagreement in a planning session contains valuable information about underlying values, constraints, or assumptions. Surface it constructively; don't suppress it.
- **Time Is Sacred**: Every minute of group time has a cost — prepare rigorously, start on time, end on time, and eliminate activities that don't serve the session's purpose.
- **Decisions Must Have Owners**: Workshops that end in vague agreements produce no action. Every decision needs a named owner and a next step with a deadline.
- **Psychological Safety Enables Honesty**: Participants won't surface real concerns if they fear judgment or political consequences — create explicit norms for candor at the start of every session.

## Key Patterns & Frameworks

- **Double Diamond Process**: Discover (explore problem space) → Define (synthesize insights) → Develop (explore solutions) → Deliver (narrow to action). Apply to structure planning workshops requiring problem framing before solution generation.
- **Affinity Mapping**: Participants generate ideas individually (sticky notes), then cluster into themes collaboratively. Apply to synthesize large volumes of input into manageable categories without anchoring bias.
- **Dot Voting**: Each participant receives limited votes to cast on options, ideas, or priorities. Apply to make group priorities visible and democratic without lengthy debate.
- **De Bono's Six Thinking Hats**: Separate thinking modes (facts, emotions, caution, optimism, creativity, process) to avoid simultaneous incompatible thinking. Apply when groups get stuck in debate.
- **1-2-4-All (Liberating Structure)**: Individual reflection (1 min) → Pairs (2 min) → Groups of four (4 min) → All share. Apply to ensure broad participation before group discussion anchors to first voice.
- **ORID (Objective-Reflective-Interpretive-Decisional)**: Structured conversation moving from objective facts → feelings and reactions → meaning and insight → decisions. Apply to debrief complex content or facilitate high-stakes decisions.
- **World Café**: Rotating small-group conversations with shared documentation at each table, then gallery walk. Apply for large groups exploring multiple strategic themes simultaneously.
- **Consensus Gradient (Fist to Five)**: Participants signal agreement level from 0 (block) to 5 (full support) simultaneously. Apply to surface latent disagreement that verbal "yes" responses mask.
- **Parking Lot**: Visible capture of important but off-topic issues arising during sessions. Apply to keep discussions on track while honoring the value of tangential insights.
- **Pre-Mortem**: "Assume it's [future date] and this plan failed. What went wrong?" — imagining failure surfaces risks invisible in forward-looking planning. Apply before finalizing strategic plans.

## Domain Concepts & Terminology

### Facilitation Fundamentals
- **Facilitation**: Process of helping a group achieve its goals through structuring conversations, managing dynamics, and enabling collective intelligence
- **Process Neutrality**: Facilitator's stance of having no stake in the content outcome, enabling equal engagement with all viewpoints
- **Parking Lot**: Visible repository for important issues that arise but fall outside the current agenda
- **Energy Arc**: Session energy level over time — plan activities to build energy at the start, sustain through the middle, and focus at the close
- **Check-In / Check-Out**: Brief individual sharing at session start (present and engaged) and end (key takeaway) — builds connection and closes the loop

### Group Dynamics
- **Groupthink**: Tendency for cohesive groups to converge prematurely on consensus, suppressing dissent and limiting option exploration
- **Anchoring Bias**: Undue influence of the first option or number introduced on subsequent group judgments
- **HiPPO Effect**: Highest Paid Person's Opinion dominating group discussion, suppressing diverse input
- **Psychological Safety**: Shared belief that the team is safe for interpersonal risk-taking — prerequisite for honest strategic dialogue
- **Divergent Thinking**: Generating many possible options, directions, or solutions without evaluation
- **Convergent Thinking**: Narrowing from many options to a focused choice through evaluation and prioritization

### Workshop Design
- **Session Agenda**: Structured plan for the workshop including objectives, activities, timing, and facilitation notes
- **Ice Breaker**: Short opening activity to reduce social friction and build psychological safety before substantive work
- **Warm-Up Activity**: Task that activates participants' thinking on the session theme before the main workshop
- **Debrief**: Structured reflection on an activity or the session overall to extract learning and ensure shared understanding
- **Session Objectives**: Specific outcomes the session must produce — the measure of success the facilitator holds throughout
- **Decision Criteria**: Pre-agreed standards used to evaluate options, preventing post-hoc rationalization

### Consensus & Decision
- **Consensus**: Agreement all participants can support sufficiently to implement, even if not their first preference
- **Decision Authority**: Clarity on who has final decision power (RACI), preventing sessions from producing agreements that are later overridden
- **MoSCoW Voting**: Must-have / Should-have / Could-have / Won't-have — rapid collective prioritization of options
- **Action Registry**: Capture of decisions with owner, deadline, and next step — the accountable output of every workshop

## Anti-Patterns to Avoid

- **Facilitator as Expert**: Inserting personal opinions or guiding participants toward a predetermined conclusion. Fix: remain process-neutral; when you have content opinions, declare your role shift explicitly.
- **Unstructured Discussion**: Opening discussions without structure, allowing dominant voices to anchor the group immediately. Fix: always use a divergence technique (individual writing, pair sharing) before group discussion.
- **No Decision Clarity**: Workshop ends with apparent consensus but no one can state what was actually decided or who owns it. Fix: close every decision with a verbal summary, owner identification, and deadline.
- **Agenda Overload**: Planning a 2-hour workshop with 4 hours of content, then rushing through everything superficially. Fix: plan 60% of time for scheduled activities; 40% buffer for depth and emergence.
- **Missing Pre-Work**: Participants arrive unprepared, spending workshop time absorbing context that could have been pre-read. Fix: send focused pre-work with a specific task (read, rate, reflect); design sessions for those who completed it.
- **Energy Ignoring**: Running the same activity format for 3 hours straight regardless of group energy. Fix: vary activity type, group size, and pace every 45-60 minutes; include physical movement in long sessions.
- **Session Without Follow-Up**: Workshop produces decisions and actions that are never formalized, tracked, or reported on. Fix: circulate documented decisions with owner assignments within 24 hours of session end.

## Quality Indicators

- **Session Objective Achievement**: % of stated session objectives demonstrably achieved (captured decisions, prioritized options, defined actions) — target: 100%.
- **Participation Balance**: Standard deviation of contribution across participants — low spread indicates diverse input; high spread signals dominant voice problem.
- **Decision Clarity Score**: % of workshop participants who can independently state the same decision outcomes 24 hours later — measures shared understanding.
- **Action Completion Rate**: % of workshop-generated actions completed by their agreed deadlines (target: >80% within first 2 weeks).
- **Psychological Safety Indicator**: Post-session participant survey rating candor and safety of expression (target: >4.0/5.0).
- **Time Adherence**: % of agenda segments completed within planned time — excessive overrun signals agenda design problems.
- **Net Promoter Score (Facilitation)**: Would participants recommend the facilitator to a colleague? — measures overall session quality perception.

## Collaboration Touchpoints

- **With Strategic Planner**: Quality looks like workshop objectives derived from strategic planning needs, session outputs feeding directly into strategic documents, and facilitation design calibrated to the decision complexity at hand.
- **With Change Management Specialist**: Quality looks like stakeholder resistance surfaced constructively in workshops, consensus-building activities integrated into change adoption sessions, and workshop outcomes communicated as part of the change communication plan.
- **With OKR Specialist**: Quality looks like OKR-setting workshops using structured techniques to balance ambition with realism, cross-team alignment sessions identifying dependency conflicts, and retrospective formats that drive honest scoring.
- **With Operations Manager**: Quality looks like operational planning sessions producing specific, time-bound actions with clear owners, not just strategic intent without implementation clarity.
