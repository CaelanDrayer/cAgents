# Best Practices: Business Researcher

> Design principles, patterns, and frameworks that guide high-quality market research, competitive analysis, and strategic intelligence work.

## Design Principles

- **Question Clarity Before Data Collection**: Define the specific decision the research will inform before gathering a single data point — unfocused research produces impressive-looking outputs that change no decisions.
- **Primary and Secondary Balance**: Combine primary research (interviews, surveys) with secondary research (reports, databases) for both validation and discovery — neither source alone is sufficient.
- **Bias Awareness**: Actively identify and mitigate confirmation bias, availability bias, and sampling bias in methodology design — bad research conclusions cost more than no research.
- **Synthesis Over Summarization**: The value of research is insight (the "so what"), not data aggregation — push every finding toward an implication for the decision at hand.
- **Source Triangulation**: Validate every major finding through at least two independent sources before treating it as reliable enough to base decisions on.
- **Research as Ongoing Intelligence**: Markets are not static; build repeatable research processes rather than one-off studies for domains that change continuously.
- **Actionable Output Format**: Structure deliverables to match how stakeholders will use them — executive briefings for strategic decisions, data tables for operational teams.

## Key Patterns & Frameworks

- **Research Design Framework**: Research question → Hypothesis → Methodology selection → Data collection → Analysis → Synthesis → Recommendations. Apply as a quality gate before committing research time.
- **Porter's Five Forces**: Competitive rivalry, supplier power, buyer power, threat of substitutes, threat of new entrants — apply to assess industry attractiveness and strategic positioning.
- **PESTEL Analysis**: Political, Economic, Social, Technological, Environmental, Legal — macro-environment scanning framework for identifying external forces affecting strategy.
- **SWOT Analysis**: Strengths, Weaknesses, Opportunities, Threats — apply to synthesize internal and external findings into strategic implications.
- **Competitive Intelligence Matrix**: Structured comparison of competitors across dimensions (product, pricing, positioning, GTM, culture) enabling side-by-side analysis.
- **Win/Loss Analysis**: Systematic interviews with won and lost customers to identify drivers of competitive outcomes — most reliable source of market reality.
- **Market Sizing (TAM/SAM/SOM)**: Total → Serviceable → Obtainable market cascade. Apply bottom-up (unit economics × addressable customers) to validate top-down estimates.
- **Primary Research Pyramid**: Expert interviews first → industry surveys second → customer interviews third — layer sources for progressive depth and validation.
- **Synthesis Matrix**: Research questions mapped to data sources and findings — ensures all questions are answered and prevents confirmation-bias cherry-picking.
- **Insight Ladder**: Data → Information → Knowledge → Insight → Recommendation. Each rung adds interpretation; most research stops at information. Push to insight.

## Domain Concepts & Terminology

### Research Methodology
- **Primary Research**: Original data collected directly from sources (interviews, surveys, focus groups, observation)
- **Secondary Research**: Analysis of existing data sources (industry reports, public filings, academic literature, databases)
- **Qualitative Research**: Non-numerical data focused on understanding motivations, behaviors, and perceptions (interviews, ethnography)
- **Quantitative Research**: Numerical data amenable to statistical analysis (surveys, transaction data, market share figures)
- **Desk Research**: Secondary research conducted from available published sources without direct field contact
- **Field Research**: Primary research conducted through direct engagement with market participants

### Market Analysis
- **TAM (Total Addressable Market)**: Revenue opportunity if 100% share of the defined market were captured
- **Market Penetration**: Current share of the addressable market captured by a player
- **Growth Rate (CAGR)**: Compound Annual Growth Rate — standard measure for comparing market or segment growth trajectories
- **Market Concentration**: Degree to which a market is dominated by a few large players (measured by HHI or top-N share)
- **Adjacency Markets**: Markets neighboring the core that share customer segments, technologies, or distribution channels
- **Whitespace**: Unaddressed or underserved market segments representing expansion opportunity

### Competitive Intelligence
- **Competitor Profile**: Structured summary of a competitor's products, pricing, positioning, GTM, financials, and strategic direction
- **Share of Voice**: % of market conversation or media coverage owned by a brand or product
- **Positioning Map**: Two-dimensional visualization of how competitors occupy the market across key attributes (price, quality, breadth)
- **Category Creation**: Strategic move to define a new market category where you are the default leader

### Research Quality
- **Sample Size**: Number of respondents in a study — must be large enough to achieve statistical significance for quantitative claims
- **Confidence Interval**: Range within which the true value likely falls (e.g., ±3%) at a given confidence level
- **Selection Bias**: Systematic error introduced when research participants are not representative of the full population
- **Triangulation**: Validating a finding using multiple independent methods or sources
- **Source Reliability**: Assessment of whether a data source has the incentives and methodology to produce accurate information

## Anti-Patterns to Avoid

- **HiPPO Research**: Research designed to confirm the Highest Paid Person's Opinion rather than genuinely test hypotheses. Fix: require explicit, falsifiable hypotheses before research begins.
- **Cherry-Picking Data**: Highlighting findings that support the desired conclusion while ignoring contradicting evidence. Fix: use a synthesis matrix covering all research questions; document contradictions explicitly.
- **Confusing Correlation with Causation**: Concluding that correlated trends have a causal relationship without testing the mechanism. Fix: separate "correlated with" from "drives" in all findings language.
- **Over-Indexing on Analyst Reports**: Treating analyst firm projections as ground truth without evaluating their methodology, sample, and incentive structure. Fix: triangulate analyst data with primary sources.
- **Research Without a Decision**: Conducting comprehensive research for a decision that has already been made or that nobody has authority to act on. Fix: confirm the decision, decision maker, and timeline before research begins.
- **Single-Source Conclusions**: Basing a major strategic recommendation on a single interview, survey, or report. Fix: require at least two independent sources for every key finding.
- **Stale Intelligence**: Presenting market data that is 2+ years old as current without checking for significant changes. Fix: date-stamp all data points and flag those older than 18 months for validation.

## Quality Indicators

- **Research Question Coverage**: % of original research questions answered with evidence in the final deliverable (target: 100%).
- **Source Diversity**: Average number of independent sources per major finding (target: ≥2 for strategic claims).
- **Stakeholder Decision Adoption**: % of research recommendations incorporated into final strategic decisions — measures actual research influence.
- **Time to Insight**: Days from research brief to actionable deliverable — measures process efficiency without sacrificing rigor.
- **Finding Validity Rate**: % of research findings confirmed accurate in post-decision retrospectives — long-term measure of research quality.
- **Primary vs. Secondary Balance**: At least 30% of major findings should be supported by primary research for high-stakes decisions.
- **Synthesis Quality**: Does every section end with "so what" implications, not just data? Measured by stakeholder feedback.

## Collaboration Touchpoints

- **With Strategic Planner**: Quality looks like research aligned to specific strategic questions, delivered in time for planning cycles, and formatted as executive briefings with clear implications.
- **With Business Development Manager**: Quality looks like competitor profiles, market sizing, and partnership landscape analysis that directly inform target partner and market prioritization decisions.
- **With Product Owner**: Quality looks like customer interview insights, JTBD findings, and competitive feature analysis formatted as validated assumptions ready for backlog decisions.
- **With Finance Manager**: Quality looks like market size and growth projections with methodology documentation, enabling financial modeling with appropriate confidence intervals.
