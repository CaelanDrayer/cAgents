# Best Practices: Business Development Manager

> Design principles, patterns, and frameworks that guide high-quality partnership development, market expansion, and business growth work.

## Design Principles

- **Relationships Before Transactions**: Build genuine long-term relationships before pursuing deals — trust compounds and creates deal flow that can't be manufactured through outreach alone.
- **Strategic Fit Over Volume**: Pursue fewer, better-aligned partnerships rather than a large portfolio of low-synergy relationships that consume disproportionate management overhead.
- **Value-First Framing**: Lead every partnership conversation with the value delivered to the partner, not the value extracted — mutual benefit is the only durable foundation for deals.
- **Qualify Ruthlessly Early**: Use structured qualification to eliminate poor-fit opportunities before significant resources are invested — time spent on wrong deals is not recoverable.
- **Land and Expand**: Prioritize getting a small, well-defined initial deal done over holding out for a comprehensive agreement — prove value first, expand scope on demonstrated success.
- **Data-Driven Market Entry**: Base expansion decisions on validated market signals (TAM analysis, customer interviews, competitive mapping) rather than executive intuition alone.
- **Internal Alignment Is Half the Work**: External deals fail because internal stakeholders (legal, product, finance) weren't aligned early. Treat internal selling as critical as external negotiations.

## Key Patterns & Frameworks

- **MEDDIC / MEDDPICC Qualification**: Metrics, Economic Buyer, Decision Criteria, Decision Process, Identify Pain, Champion — framework for qualifying partnership and sales opportunities rigorously.
- **Partnership Tiers Model**: Stratify partners into tiers (strategic, preferred, transactional) with differentiated investment levels, SLAs, and joint business plans per tier.
- **TAM / SAM / SOM Analysis**: Total Addressable Market → Serviceable Addressable Market → Serviceable Obtainable Market — cascading market sizing for prioritizing expansion targets.
- **Jobs-to-be-Done (JTBD)**: Understand the functional and emotional "job" a partner hires your organization to do. Apply to craft value propositions that resonate with partner motivations.
- **Partner Business Plan (PBP)**: Jointly authored annual plan with a strategic partner defining mutual goals, investment commitments, go-to-market activities, and success metrics.
- **Ecosystem Mapping**: Visualize the competitive landscape, complementary players, and potential allies. Identify white spaces and integration opportunities before outreach.
- **Business Model Canvas**: Map the partner's value proposition, customer segments, channels, revenue streams, and cost structure to identify where collaboration creates mutual value.
- **Deal Structure Patterns**: Rev-share, co-sell, white-label, OEM, reseller, referral — understanding trade-offs of each structure for different relationship types and risk profiles.
- **Competitive Intelligence Framework**: Systematic collection and analysis of competitor partnership activity, pricing, and positioning to inform negotiation strategies and market entry timing.
- **Win/Loss Analysis**: Structured debrief after every closed/lost deal to identify patterns in what drives success and failure, feeding back into qualification and pitch refinement.

## Domain Concepts & Terminology

### Partnership Types
- **Strategic Alliance**: Deep, long-term collaboration with significant co-investment from both parties in joint go-to-market, product integration, or R&D
- **Channel Partnership**: Partner resells, distributes, or refers your product/service in exchange for margin, fee, or commission
- **Technology Integration Partner**: Complementary product integration creating combined value neither party delivers alone
- **OEM (Original Equipment Manufacturer)**: Partner embeds your technology into their product, selling it as their own
- **Co-Marketing Partner**: Joint demand generation activities (webinars, content, events) to reach shared audiences

### Market Development
- **TAM (Total Addressable Market)**: Total revenue opportunity if 100% market share were captured
- **Beachhead Market**: Initial focused segment used to prove the model before broader expansion
- **Market Penetration Rate**: Current share of the serviceable addressable market captured
- **White Space**: Unaddressed market segments or customer needs not currently served by existing offerings
- **Land and Expand**: Start with a limited initial deployment, prove value, then grow the engagement

### Deal Mechanics
- **LOI (Letter of Intent)**: Non-binding document outlining key deal terms before formal contract negotiation
- **MOU (Memorandum of Understanding)**: Formal agreement capturing mutual intent without binding legal obligation
- **Co-sell Motion**: Joint sales engagement where both partners participate in closing deals together
- **Revenue Share**: Percentage of revenue generated through the partnership allocated to each party
- **MDF (Market Development Funds)**: Partner co-op marketing budget funded by the vendor to drive demand

### Pipeline & Metrics
- **Pipeline Coverage**: Ratio of pipeline value to target — typically 3-5x coverage required for plan attainment
- **Deal Velocity**: Average time from first contact to closed deal — improving velocity requires removing friction at each stage
- **Partnership Activation Rate**: % of signed partners generating revenue within 90 days — measures onboarding effectiveness
- **NDA (Non-Disclosure Agreement)**: Confidentiality agreement signed before sharing sensitive information in partnership discussions

## Anti-Patterns to Avoid

- **Logo Collection**: Signing many partner agreements for brand credibility without activating them for revenue or mutual value. Fix: define activation criteria before signing; audit portfolio quarterly.
- **Skipping Legal Alignment**: Moving into partnership conversations without legal, finance, and product aligned on acceptable terms. Fix: run internal alignment checklist before external negotiation begins.
- **Asymmetric Value Deals**: Structuring deals that heavily favor one party — the disadvantaged party will deprioritize the relationship the moment a better option appears. Fix: ensure both parties have clear, proportionate value from day one.
- **No Champion**: Pursuing partnerships without an internal champion at the partner organization who is personally invested in success. Fix: qualify for a named champion before investing significant negotiation time.
- **Spray and Pray Outreach**: Mass-emailing potential partners with generic pitches. Fix: invest in personalized outreach to a smaller, highly qualified list with tailored value propositions.
- **Stale Pipeline**: Deals sitting in negotiation stages for months with no movement or next steps. Fix: enforce deal velocity standards; move stale deals to inactive or disqualify.
- **Integration-Only Thinking**: Assuming technology integrations automatically create partnership value. Fix: co-sell, joint marketing, and customer success motions must accompany technical integrations.

## Quality Indicators

- **Partnership Activation Rate**: % of signed partners generating ≥1 qualified opportunity within 90 days (target: >60%).
- **Qualified Opportunity Contribution**: % of total pipeline sourced through partnerships (target depends on channel strategy, typically 20-40%).
- **Deal Velocity by Stage**: Average days in each pipeline stage — trending increases signal friction or misalignment worth investigating.
- **Partner NPS**: Net Promoter Score from active partners measuring relationship health and likelihood to invest more.
- **Revenue per Active Partner**: Average revenue generated per activated partner — rising metric indicates partnership quality over quantity.
- **Time to First Deal**: Days from partner agreement signature to first closed deal — shorter cycles indicate better onboarding and enablement.
- **Pipeline Conversion Rate**: % of qualified partnership opportunities that close — below 20% suggests qualification criteria are too loose.

## Collaboration Touchpoints

- **With Strategic Planner**: Quality looks like new partnership opportunities evaluated against strategic priorities, expansion markets validated with market data, and a clear rationale for each opportunity in the portfolio.
- **With Legal / Contracts Manager**: Quality looks like deal terms reviewed before verbal commitment is made, standard templates applied where possible, and legal engaged early enough to influence (not just review) negotiation.
- **With Product Team**: Quality looks like integration requirements scoped before partnership announcement, partner-driven product requests evaluated through a defined process, and no secret commitments made to partners about product roadmap.
- **With Finance Manager**: Quality looks like revenue share structures approved within margin guardrails, MDF spend with clear attribution metrics, and partnership investment ROI tracked quarterly.
