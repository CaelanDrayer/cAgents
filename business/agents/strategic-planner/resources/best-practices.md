# Best Practices: Strategic Planner

> Design principles, patterns, and frameworks that guide high-quality long-term strategy development, competitive positioning, strategic initiative planning, and organizational alignment work.

## Design Principles

- **Strategy Is a Choice, Not a Plan**: Strategy means explicitly choosing where to compete and where not to — comprehensive plans that try to do everything are not strategies.
- **Outside-In Before Inside-Out**: Start with the external environment (market dynamics, competitive threats, customer needs) before assessing internal capabilities — inside-out thinking produces strategically irrelevant plans.
- **Constraints Are Strategic Information**: Resource, capability, and time constraints aren't obstacles to strategy — they are inputs that determine which strategies are actually achievable.
- **Strategy Without Execution Is Theory**: A strategy is only as good as the operational plans and organizational changes that translate it into action — bridge strategy and operations explicitly.
- **Competitive Advantage Must Be Defensible**: Strategies built on advantages competitors can easily replicate produce temporary wins at best. Identify and develop advantages that are hard to imitate.
- **Communicate Until It Sounds Obvious**: Strategy that is understood by 10% of the organization has zero chance of being executed. Over-communicate with clarity and repetition until the strategy is widely internalized.
- **Plans Must Be Falsifiable**: Strategic plans with no measurable outcomes and no timeline cannot be tested — if a plan can't fail, it can't succeed either.

## Key Patterns & Frameworks

- **Porter's Five Forces**: Competitive rivalry, supplier power, buyer power, threat of substitutes, threat of new entrants. Apply to assess industry attractiveness and identify strategic positioning opportunities.
- **SWOT Analysis**: Strengths, Weaknesses, Opportunities, Threats — synthesize internal and external analysis into strategic options. Apply as a structured way to move from analysis to strategy generation.
- **Blue Ocean Strategy**: Value Innovation — simultaneously increase value to buyers while reducing costs by eliminating and reducing industry-standard factors. Apply when seeking to create uncontested market space rather than competing on existing dimensions.
- **Jobs-to-be-Done (Strategic)**: Understand what customers fundamentally "hire" products or services to do. Apply to identify where disruption risk is highest (when JTBD is poorly served) and where differentiation opportunities exist.
- **Strategy Canvas / Four Actions Framework**: Eliminate-Reduce-Raise-Create (ERRC Grid) for value curve redesign. Apply to operationalize Blue Ocean Strategy by specifying which competitive factors to change.
- **Competitive Positioning Matrix**: Map the organization against competitors on dimensions that matter to customers. Apply to visualize where differentiation exists and where competitive overlap creates vulnerability.
- **Balanced Scorecard**: Financial + Customer + Internal Process + Learning & Growth — four perspectives ensuring strategy is implemented across all organizational dimensions. Apply to translate strategy into measurable objectives across perspectives.
- **Scenario Planning Integration**: Develop strategy against multiple plausible future scenarios, not just the base case. Apply to identify robust strategic elements (perform across scenarios) vs. conditional bets (only in specific scenarios).
- **OGSM (Objectives, Goals, Strategies, Measures)**: Hierarchical framework linking vision to measurable plans. Apply to create a one-page strategy summary communicable to all organization levels.
- **Strategic Priority Matrix**: Plot strategic initiatives by strategic importance vs. implementation complexity. Apply to sequence investments and avoid over-investing in complex initiatives with marginal strategic value.
- **Ansoff Matrix**: Market Penetration / Market Development / Product Development / Diversification — growth strategy options with increasing risk levels. Apply to frame growth strategy discussion and risk appetite conversation.

## Domain Concepts & Terminology

### Strategy Fundamentals
- **Competitive Advantage**: Unique combination of factors enabling an organization to outperform competitors in ways that are difficult to replicate
- **Strategic Position**: Where an organization chooses to compete relative to competitors and customer segments
- **Core Competency**: Deep organizational capability that delivers competitive advantage across multiple products and markets
- **Value Proposition**: The specific combination of benefits delivered to customers that competitors cannot easily match
- **Moat**: Durable competitive advantage protecting an organization from competitive erosion (network effects, switching costs, scale, brand)
- **Strategic Fit**: Alignment between environmental opportunities/threats and organizational capabilities/resources

### Market Analysis
- **Total Addressable Market (TAM)**: Revenue opportunity if 100% of the defined market were captured
- **Market Positioning**: How an organization differentiates itself in customers' minds relative to competitors
- **Value Chain Analysis**: Decomposition of organizational activities (primary and supporting) to identify where competitive advantage is built
- **Competitive Intelligence**: Systematic gathering and analysis of information about competitors' strategies, capabilities, and moves
- **Disruption**: Innovation that creates a new market by initially serving overlooked segments before displacing incumbents

### Strategy Process
- **Environmental Scan**: Systematic analysis of external macro-environment (PESTEL) and industry forces (Porter's Five Forces)
- **Strategic Planning Cycle**: Typically annual process: environmental scan → strategic assessment → options generation → evaluation → selection → implementation planning → monitoring
- **Strategy Horizon**: Time frame of strategic focus — typically Horizon 1 (1-2 years, core business), H2 (2-5 years, adjacent), H3 (5+ years, transformational)
- **Strategic Initiative**: Major effort required to achieve a strategic objective — distinct from business-as-usual operations
- **Cascade**: Process of translating company strategy into business unit, team, and individual objectives and plans
- **Strategic Review**: Regular senior leadership meeting to assess strategy execution progress and adjust as needed

### Implementation
- **Implementation Gap**: Common failure mode where strategy is well-designed but organizational capabilities, resources, or will to execute are insufficient
- **Change Readiness**: Organizational capacity to absorb and execute the changes required by the strategy
- **Strategic Resource Allocation**: Directing investment (people, capital, time) toward strategic priorities rather than distributing evenly across existing activities

## Anti-Patterns to Avoid

- **Consensus Strategy**: Strategy designed to satisfy all stakeholders by saying yes to everything — produces no real prioritization and no competitive differentiation. Fix: strategy requires trade-offs; the best strategies say "no" more than "yes."
- **Inside-Out Only**: Strategy built exclusively from internal capability assessment without rigorous external market and competitive analysis. Fix: start with external analysis; internal capability assessment is a filter on external opportunity, not the starting point.
- **Strategy Disconnected from Operations**: Elegant strategy documents that bear no relationship to operational planning, budgeting, or people management. Fix: require explicit operational translation of every strategic initiative — owners, budgets, milestones, dependencies.
- **Annual Strategy Season**: Strategic planning conducted once a year as a formal ritual, then put on the shelf until next year. Fix: integrate strategic review into quarterly leadership reviews; treat strategy as a living system, not an annual deliverable.
- **Missing Competitive Dimension**: Strategy that treats the market as a vacuum, ignoring how competitors will respond to strategic moves. Fix: explicitly model competitor responses to strategic initiatives; strategies that assume passive competitors underestimate the risk.
- **Aspirational KPIs Only**: Strategy metrics that are inspiring ("be the leader in customer experience") without quantified targets or measurement plans. Fix: every strategic objective requires a specific, measurable target and a measurement method.
- **No Kill Criteria**: Continuing strategic initiatives long after evidence suggests they aren't working because there's no predefined threshold for stopping. Fix: define kill criteria or decision points for every major strategic initiative at launch.

## Quality Indicators

- **Strategic Initiative Execution Rate**: % of strategic initiatives progressing on plan at each quarterly review (target: >75%).
- **Strategy Awareness Rate**: % of employees who can accurately describe the top 3 strategic priorities — measures communication effectiveness.
- **OKR-Strategy Alignment**: % of company OKRs directly linked to a strategic priority (target: >90%) — measures strategy-to-execution translation.
- **Competitive Position Trajectory**: Trend in market share, win rate, or NPS relative to named competitors — measures whether strategy is achieving competitive intent.
- **Strategic Investment Allocation**: % of discretionary budget directed to strategic vs. sustaining activities — ratio should reflect strategy's growth ambitions.
- **Decision Speed**: Average days from strategic question identification to decision made — slow strategic decisions allow competitors to move while the organization deliberates.
- **Scenario Coverage**: % of current strategic initiatives evaluated against defined future scenarios — measures strategic robustness and planning rigor.

## Collaboration Touchpoints

- **With Operations Manager**: Quality looks like strategic priorities translated into operational improvement initiatives, operational constraints surfaced as strategic inputs, and operational metrics linked to strategic outcomes.
- **With Finance Manager**: Quality looks like strategic initiatives reflected in long-range financial model, investment requirements phased in capital allocation plan, and strategic scenario assumptions feeding financial sensitivity analysis.
- **With OKR Specialist**: Quality looks like company OKRs derived from strategic priorities with clear line-of-sight, strategic planning cycles aligned to OKR cadence, and strategic objective measurement consistent with OKR framework.
- **With Scenario Planner**: Quality looks like strategic decisions explicitly tested against scenario set, strategy refresh triggered when scenario signposts indicate environmental shift, and contingency plans aligned to strategic priorities.
