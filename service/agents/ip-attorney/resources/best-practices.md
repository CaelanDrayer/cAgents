# Best Practices: IP Attorney

> Design principles, patterns, and frameworks that guide high-quality intellectual property portfolio management and protection work.

## Design Principles

- **Strategic Portfolio, Not Defensive Filing**: File patents that align with business strategy and product roadmap, not simply everything that could be patented; quality portfolio beats quantity
- **IP Captures Value Only When Enforced**: A patent that's never enforced provides minimal competitive advantage; build IP rights with an enforcement strategy in mind from the filing stage
- **Prior Art Awareness**: Searching for existing prior art before filing saves significant prosecution cost and prevents the embarrassment of pursuing unpatentable inventions
- **Trade Secret Discipline**: For technologies that can be kept secret, trade secret protection often outlasts patents and costs far less; but trade secret status requires active, documented secrecy measures
- **IP Assignment is Foundational**: Every founder, employee, consultant, and contractor must assign IP to the company before creating it; retroactive assignment is expensive and sometimes impossible
- **Brand Integrity Through Trademark**: Trademarks are the company's public identity; protecting them consistently prevents third-party dilution and maintains enforcement standing
- **License Economics Matter**: Licensing terms must reflect fair market value; licensing too cheaply undervalues assets while licensing too expensively drives litigation risk

## Key Patterns & Frameworks

- **IP Audit Process**: Comprehensive inventory of all company IP assets (patents, applications, trademarks, copyrights, trade secrets, licenses) with ownership confirmation, maintenance status, and coverage gaps
- **Invention Disclosure Program**: Internal process for employees to submit invention disclosures; triggered by engineers and product teams, reviewed by IP attorney to select prosecution candidates
- **Patent Prosecution Timeline Management**: USPTO prosecution has predictable milestones; maintain a prosecution docket with all deadlines, responses due, and maintenance fees; missed deadlines abandon rights permanently
- **Freedom-to-Operate (FTO) Analysis**: Before launching a new product or entering a new market, search for third-party patents that might be infringed; FTO analysis drives product design decisions and acquisition strategy
- **Trademark Clearance Search**: Before adopting a new brand, name, or logo, conduct comprehensive clearance search covering identical and similar marks in relevant classes and geographies
- **IP Due Diligence Framework for M&A**: In acquisitions, validate ownership chain for all claimed IP, check for encumbrances, review license terms and change-of-control provisions, assess litigation history, and evaluate portfolio quality
- **License Agreement Term Negotiation Framework**: Scope (what's licensed), territory, exclusivity, royalty rate and structure, sublicensing rights, auditing rights, term and termination, and IP ownership of derivatives
- **DMCA Takedown Process**: Systematic process for identifying infringing content online and issuing takedown notices; automate monitoring and response where possible

## Domain Concepts & Terminology

### Patents
- **Patent Application**: Document filed with patent office claiming an invention and requesting protection
- **Claims**: The legally operative section of a patent defining the scope of protection; broader claims provide broader protection
- **Prior Art**: Existing public knowledge that can prevent patentability or invalidate a patent
- **Utility Patent**: Patent for a functional invention; 20-year term from filing date
- **Design Patent**: Patent for ornamental appearance; 15-year term (in US)
- **Provisional Patent Application**: Placeholder filing establishing priority date without formal claims; must be followed by non-provisional within 12 months
- **Non-Provisional Application**: Formal patent application with full claims; begins examination by the patent office
- **Office Action**: USPTO examiner's written response rejecting or allowing claims; requires timely response
- **Patent Maintenance Fees**: Periodic fees required to keep a granted patent in force; missed fees abandon the patent

### Trademarks
- **Mark**: A word, symbol, logo, or combination that identifies the source of goods or services
- **Class of Goods/Services**: International Classification of Nice designating the products or services covered by a trademark registration
- **Use in Commerce**: Required for US trademark registration; mark must be actually used to identify goods or services
- **Likelihood of Confusion**: Legal standard for trademark infringement; whether consumers would confuse the marks' source
- **Trademark Watch**: Service monitoring new trademark filings and use for potentially confusing marks
- **Madrid Protocol**: International trademark registration system allowing filing in multiple countries through a single application

### Copyrights
- **Copyright**: Automatic protection for original works of authorship (software, literature, art, music)
- **Copyright Registration**: Optional but required before suing for infringement in the US; provides statutory damages and attorney fees
- **Work for Hire**: Work created by an employee in the scope of employment or under a written WFH agreement; company owns the copyright
- **DMCA (Digital Millennium Copyright Act)**: US law creating safe harbors for online platforms hosting user content and providing takedown procedures
- **Fair Use**: Doctrine permitting use of copyrighted material without permission for comment, criticism, education, or parody

### Trade Secrets
- **Trade Secret**: Confidential business information with economic value that is actively protected from disclosure
- **Misappropriation**: Improper acquisition, disclosure, or use of a trade secret
- **NDA (Non-Disclosure Agreement)**: Contract protecting confidential information shared between parties
- **Defend Trade Secrets Act (DTSA)**: Federal civil cause of action for trade secret misappropriation
- **Reasonable Measures**: Active, documented efforts to maintain secrecy; required to qualify for trade secret protection

### Licensing
- **Exclusive License**: Right granted to only one licensee to use the IP in the defined scope
- **Non-Exclusive License**: Right granted to multiple licensees
- **Royalty**: Fee paid by licensee to licensor, typically percentage of revenue or per-unit amount
- **Cross-License**: Agreement where two parties each license their IP to the other; common in patent disputes

## Anti-Patterns to Avoid

- **Filing Without Strategy**: Pursuing patents for technology that doesn't align with the business model or that can't be monetized through licensing or enforcement; wastes prosecution budget
- **Missing Deadlines**: Allowing patent maintenance fees, Office Action response deadlines, or trademark renewal deadlines to pass; these failures abandon rights permanently with no remedy
- **IP Assignment Gaps**: Engaging contractors or consultants without IP assignment agreements; creates ownership disputes and chain-of-title defects that delay or kill transactions
- **Trademark Dilution Through Non-Enforcement**: Allowing third parties to use confusingly similar marks without challenge; failure to enforce weakens the mark and can result in abandonment
- **Broad License Terms Without Audit Rights**: Granting licenses without the right to audit royalty reports; non-payment is the most common licensing dispute
- **FTO Analysis After Launch**: Discovering potentially infringing third-party patents after product launch rather than during design; earlier analysis allows design-around while later analysis requires expensive pivots
- **Trade Secret Without Secrecy Measures**: Claiming trade secret protection for information that isn't actively kept confidential; courts require reasonable measures to qualify as a trade secret

## Quality Indicators

- **Prosecution Docket Compliance**: Zero missed USPTO or trademark office deadlines
- **IP Assignment Coverage**: 100% of employees, contractors, and founders with signed IP assignment agreements on file
- **Patent Portfolio Alignment**: Percentage of active patents mapping to current product or strategic business areas
- **FTO Clearance Rate**: Percentage of new product launches with completed FTO analysis before launch
- **Trademark Watch Coverage**: Active monitoring of relevant trademark classes in key markets
- **License Revenue vs. Forecast**: IP licensing revenue performance against projections
- **IP Audit Currency**: Comprehensive IP audit completed within the past 24 months

## Collaboration Touchpoints

- **With General Counsel**: Escalate enforcement decisions, major licensing transactions, and litigation strategy; GC owns overall legal strategy while IP Attorney owns portfolio management
- **With Corporate Counsel**: Coordinate IP due diligence in M&A transactions; IP ownership and chain-of-title is typically one of the highest-value and highest-risk areas of tech company M&A
- **With Product and Engineering**: Embed invention disclosure program into product development process; IP attorney reviews technical disclosures and advises on prosecution candidates
- **With Business Development**: Advise on licensing terms and IP representations in partnership and distribution agreements; IP terms require legal review before commercial commitment
