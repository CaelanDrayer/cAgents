# Best Practices: Customer Advocacy Manager

> Design principles, patterns, and frameworks that guide high-quality customer advocacy program and community initiative work.

## Design Principles

- **Advocacy Is Earned, Not Assigned**: Customers become advocates because they've experienced genuine value; start with the most successful customers, not the loudest ones
- **Mutual Value Exchange**: Advocacy programs only sustain themselves when customers get something back — recognition, early access, peer connection, or influence over the product
- **Customer Voice Amplification**: The advocacy manager's job is to give already-happy customers a platform, not to manufacture satisfaction that doesn't exist
- **Story Over Statistics**: A vivid customer story with specific numbers creates more influence than a general case study; help customers articulate their transformation
- **Reference Asset Freshness**: Old case studies and dated testimonials undermine credibility; maintain a living reference library with regular updates
- **Advocate Relationship Investment**: Top advocates deserve regular personal engagement; reference-only relationships that activate only when sales needs a call are extractive and churn
- **Cross-Functional Advocacy Governance**: Marketing, sales, and customer success all need advocates; manage the reference pool centrally to prevent advocate fatigue from over-utilization

## Key Patterns & Frameworks

- **Advocacy Candidate Scoring**: Score accounts by NPS, health score, product adoption depth, executive relationship strength, and story articulation ability; highest-scoring accounts are your first outreach targets
- **Tiered Advocate Program Design**: Bronze (testimonials, G2 reviews), Silver (case studies, reference calls), Gold (speaking engagements, advisory board); tier based on level of commitment and reward
- **Case Study Story Arc**: Challenge → Solution → Results with specific metrics → What's Next; every case study needs all four components to be compelling and credible
- **Reference Call Preparation Protocol**: Before any sales reference call, brief the advocate on the prospect's specific concerns, what questions to expect, and what story elements are most relevant
- **Advocate Fatigue Monitoring**: Track reference call frequency per account; any account used more than once per quarter risks fatigue; rotate the pool actively
- **Customer Advisory Board (CAB) Meeting Rhythm**: Semi-annual meetings with a structured agenda split between product input and peer networking; CAB members must feel influential to stay engaged
- **Advocacy ROI Measurement**: Track influenced ARR from reference calls, case study downloads, and testimonial page views; connect advocacy activity to pipeline and revenue
- **Lifecycle Integration**: Embed advocacy identification into the customer success process; CSMs identify candidates at 90-day milestones when early success is confirmed

## Domain Concepts & Terminology

### Advocacy Program Elements
- **Customer Advocate**: Customer who actively promotes your product or service through testimonials, references, case studies, or public speaking
- **Reference Customer**: Customer willing to speak with prospects about their experience; the most directly sales-impactful form of advocacy
- **Case Study**: Documented narrative of a customer's success with specific metrics; used in marketing and sales enablement
- **Testimonial**: Short, attributed quote from a customer endorsing the product; used across marketing channels
- **Customer Advisory Board (CAB)**: Formal group of select customers who provide strategic product feedback to leadership
- **Voice of Customer (VoC)**: Systematic collection and analysis of customer feedback, opinions, and preferences

### Community Programs
- **Community Forum**: Online discussion space where customers ask questions, share tips, and connect with peers
- **User Group**: Geography- or industry-specific customer community sub-group with regular meetings
- **Beta Program**: Early access program for engaged customers to test and provide feedback on pre-release features
- **Community Ambassador**: Recognized community leader with elevated status and access to the company
- **Peer Review Program**: Structured campaign encouraging customers to post reviews on G2, Capterra, or similar platforms

### Measurement
- **Reference Pool Size**: Total number of customers available and willing to participate in reference activities
- **Reference Utilization Rate**: How often individual advocates are called upon; high rate signals fatigue risk
- **Case Study Pipeline**: Number of case studies in development across stages (identified, interview scheduled, drafted, approved, published)
- **Influenced Pipeline**: Sales opportunities where advocacy content or reference calls contributed to progression
- **Advocacy Program NPS**: Satisfaction score from advocates about their experience in the program itself
- **G2/Capterra Review Volume**: Number of current, verified reviews on third-party platforms; key input to buyer research

### Story Development
- **Quantified Outcome**: Specific, measurable result the customer achieved (e.g., "reduced onboarding time by 40%")
- **Before/After Narrative**: Story structure contrasting the customer's situation before and after adoption
- **Named Executive Quote**: Attribution of a testimonial to a specific named person with title; far more credible than anonymous quotes
- **Logo Permission**: Formal approval to use the customer's company name and logo in marketing materials

## Anti-Patterns to Avoid

- **Pushing Advocacy on Unhappy Customers**: Asking for references or case studies from accounts with low health scores; it strains the relationship and produces poor or dishonest materials
- **Over-Utilizing the Same Advocates**: Repeatedly calling the same reference accounts for every sales deal; advocate fatigue leads to withdrawal from the program
- **Unprepared Reference Calls**: Connecting a prospect to a reference customer without briefing either party; unprepared calls produce generic, unconvincing conversations
- **Logo-Only Case Studies**: Publishing case studies with vague outcomes and no specific metrics; buyers discount unnamed benefits without quantification
- **One-Way Advocacy Relationships**: Building a program that takes advocate time without giving back value; advocates who only give eventually stop showing up
- **Siloed Reference Management**: Allowing marketing, sales, and CS to each manage their own reference lists without coordination; creates advocate fatigue and inconsistent messaging
- **Stale Content Library**: Maintaining case studies older than 2 years without updates; outdated materials undermine credibility and may no longer reflect product capabilities

## Quality Indicators

- **Reference Pool Health**: Active, willing advocates representing diverse use cases, industries, and company sizes
- **Reference Call Satisfaction**: Post-call feedback from both the prospect and the advocate; both parties should find value
- **Case Study Cadence**: Regular publishing rhythm with new case studies representing current customers and capabilities
- **Advocate Program Retention**: Percentage of advocates who remain active year over year; declining retention signals poor program experience
- **Influenced Revenue Attribution**: Pipeline or ARR influenced by advocacy content and reference calls
- **CAB Attendance Rate**: Consistent executive attendance at customer advisory board meetings
- **Review Platform Ratings**: Current, high-quality reviews on G2 and similar platforms; average rating and recency both matter

## Collaboration Touchpoints

- **With Customer Success Manager**: Identify advocacy candidates at the 90-day success milestone; CSM confirms health and relationship quality before advocacy outreach
- **With Community Manager**: Align on community ambassador programs and user group activation; advocacy and community programs reinforce each other
- **With Sales/Account Executive**: Coordinate reference calls — sales provides prospect context, advocacy manages the reference relationship and advocate experience
- **With Marketing**: Supply case studies, testimonials, and logos for campaigns; advocacy program is the primary source of authentic customer proof
