# Best Practices: General Counsel

> Design principles, patterns, and frameworks that guide high-quality enterprise legal strategy and coordination work.

## Design Principles

- **Legal as Business Enabler**: The General Counsel's job is to help the business achieve its goals within legal bounds, not to create obstacles; advisors who only say no lose credibility and influence
- **Risk Quantification Over Risk Avoidance**: Leaders can't make good decisions from "there's legal risk" — quantify probability and impact, offer mitigation options, and let business leaders make informed decisions
- **Proactive Legal Architecture**: The best time to structure a deal, draft a policy, or design a process is before it creates a problem; GC must be at the table early, not called in to fix the wreckage
- **Outside Counsel as Strategic Resource**: External counsel provides specialized expertise and surge capacity; GC's role is to select the right firms, direct their work efficiently, and manage costs without sacrificing quality
- **Privilege and Confidentiality Discipline**: Attorney-client privilege is a strategic asset; GC must manage it intentionally, particularly in written communications and investigations
- **Board Relationship Investment**: The board is the GC's ultimate stakeholder and governance partner; invest in those relationships and maintain independent board access
- **Integrated Legal Team**: Legal functions (corporate, litigation, employment, IP, privacy, compliance) must operate as an integrated team with consistent standards, shared information, and coordinated strategies

## Key Patterns & Frameworks

- **Matter Management Framework**: Every legal matter requires a matter intake, assigned attorney, budget, strategy document, and regular status review; undocumented matters create surprises
- **Legal Hold Protocol**: When litigation or investigation is reasonably anticipated, immediately suspend auto-deletion and preserve relevant documents; failure to preserve creates spoliation sanctions
- **Outside Counsel Management Program**: RFP-based selection, billing guidelines, matter-specific budget approvals, regular performance reviews, and AFA (Alternative Fee Arrangement) negotiations; active management reduces outside counsel costs 20-30%
- **Legal Department OKRs**: Quarterly objectives and key results for the legal department (cycle time, spend efficiency, training completion, contract SLAs); GC should be held to measurable performance standards
- **Board Reporting Template**: Quarterly board report covering material litigation, regulatory matters, significant transactions, compliance status, and emerging risks; standardized format enables efficient board oversight
- **Crisis Legal Response Protocol**: Legal hold → Immediate assessment team → Outside counsel engagement → Regulator/law enforcement coordination plan → Communications strategy → Board notification → Investigation structure
- **Contract Playbook Development**: Standardized positions on key contract terms with escalation thresholds; empowers junior attorneys to handle routine matters independently
- **Regulatory Landscape Scan**: Annual review of regulatory developments affecting the business; assign ownership for monitoring each major regulatory area and report on changes quarterly

## Domain Concepts & Terminology

### Legal Strategy
- **Legal Risk Matrix**: Inventory of all significant legal risks ranked by probability and impact with assigned mitigation strategies
- **Litigation Reserve**: Financial accrual for estimated resolution cost of active or anticipated litigation
- **Settlement Authority**: Pre-approved limits for resolving matters without additional approval at different dollar thresholds
- **Materiality Threshold**: Level at which a legal matter or risk becomes significant enough to require board notification or financial disclosure
- **Legal Hold**: Suspension of document destruction policies to preserve evidence relevant to litigation or investigation

### Corporate Governance
- **Board of Directors**: Governing body with oversight responsibilities for the corporation's management and strategy
- **Audit Committee**: Board committee overseeing financial reporting, internal controls, and legal and regulatory compliance
- **Fiduciary Duties**: Legal obligations of directors (duty of care, duty of loyalty) to act in the corporation's best interest
- **D&O Insurance**: Directors and Officers liability insurance protecting personal assets in claims arising from business decisions
- **Corporate Secretary Function**: Responsibility for board meeting administration, minutes, corporate records, and governance compliance

### Legal Operations
- **ALT Fee Arrangement (AFA)**: Alternative to hourly billing — fixed fee, capped fee, success fee, blended rate, or retainer; reduces cost unpredictability
- **e-Billing**: Electronic invoice submission and processing with automated billing guideline enforcement
- **Matter Management System**: Technology platform for tracking legal matters, deadlines, budgets, and documents
- **Legal Department Benchmark**: Comparison of legal department metrics (spend as % of revenue, headcount per $B revenue) to industry peers

### Regulatory & Compliance
- **Regulatory Horizon Scanning**: Systematic monitoring of proposed and enacted regulatory changes affecting the business
- **Consent Decree**: Court-ordered compliance agreement, often following regulatory enforcement; requires ongoing monitoring
- **Deferred Prosecution Agreement (DPA)**: Prosecution withheld in exchange for compliance program and monitorship; requires sustained attention
- **Regulatory Investigation**: Government inquiry into potential violations; typically requires counsel, legal holds, and voluntary cooperation decisions

## Anti-Patterns to Avoid

- **The Department of No**: Reflexively blocking business initiatives without offering alternatives; GC who can't say yes in any context will be excluded from early-stage discussions where input would be most valuable
- **Unauthorized Settlements**: Allowing settlements without proper approval authority; creates precedent for future claims and violates governance controls
- **Undermanaged Outside Counsel**: Engaging outside counsel without billing guidelines, budget approval, or regular performance review; outside legal costs become uncontrollable
- **Privilege Waiver Carelessness**: Circulating legal advice to business partners, forwarding privileged emails without redaction, or copying consultants on legal advice; waives privilege and makes advice discoverable
- **Reactive Compliance**: Learning about regulatory changes after they take effect rather than monitoring proactively; compliance gaps and penalties result from reactive posture
- **Siloed Legal Functions**: Operating corporate, litigation, IP, employment, and compliance as completely separate teams without information sharing; creates gaps, inconsistencies, and missed connections between related matters
- **Board Surprise**: Board members learning about material litigation, regulatory actions, or legal risks from sources other than the GC; destroys trust and may constitute a governance failure

## Quality Indicators

- **Legal Spend as % of Revenue**: Trending efficiently while maintaining quality; benchmark against industry peers annually
- **Litigation Reserve Accuracy**: Estimated vs. actual resolution costs; high accuracy indicates strong case evaluation methodology
- **Outside Counsel Cost per Matter**: Tracked and benchmarked; declining trend indicates effective management
- **Contract SLA Compliance**: Percentage of contract review requests completed within the committed turnaround time
- **Board Reporting Currency**: Legal status reports delivered before every board meeting with zero material surprises
- **Legal Hold Compliance**: Zero spoliation sanctions; all holds implemented promptly
- **Regulatory Filing Compliance**: All required regulatory filings submitted on time with zero enforcement actions for missed deadlines

## Collaboration Touchpoints

- **With Corporate Counsel**: Delegate entity structure, M&A diligence, and securities compliance; receive regular status on material transactions and corporate housekeeping
- **With Litigation Manager**: Receive regular case status updates; provide settlement authority and strategy direction; coordinate on matters exceeding thresholds
- **With Compliance Officer**: Align compliance program priorities with legal risk assessment; receive escalation of material compliance violations requiring legal response
- **With Privacy Officer**: Coordinate on data breach response, regulatory investigations, and privacy litigation; privacy is both a legal and compliance function requiring both roles
