# Best Practices: Litigation Manager

> Design principles, patterns, and frameworks that guide high-quality litigation management and dispute resolution work.

## Design Principles

- **Early Case Assessment Is Everything**: The first 30-60 days of a case shape its entire trajectory; invest heavily in early case assessment to make informed strategy and settlement decisions
- **Budget Discipline from Inception**: Litigation costs spiral when allowed to; set case budgets at intake and require approval for overruns, not just approval when they've already happened
- **Settlement Is Usually the Right Answer**: Most commercial disputes are better resolved through settlement than trial; calculate expected value early and use it to guide negotiation strategy
- **Outside Counsel as Partner, Not Autonomous Agent**: Outside litigation counsel must operate within the company's strategic direction and budget constraints, not autonomously; GC/litigation manager must direct, not just receive updates
- **Preservation First**: When litigation is reasonably anticipated, legal holds must be issued immediately; spoliation sanctions can destroy an otherwise strong case
- **Witness Preparation Is Mandatory**: Unprepared witnesses are dangerous witnesses; invest in thorough witness preparation for every deposition and trial appearance
- **Litigation Data Feeds Strategy**: Track case outcomes, settlement amounts, litigation costs, and opposing counsel patterns; the data improves future strategy and settlement benchmarking

## Key Patterns & Frameworks

- **Early Case Assessment (ECA) Framework**: Within 30 days of case initiation: assess liability exposure, identify key witnesses and documents, estimate litigation cost to resolution, model settlement value, and recommend strategy (defend, settle, or negotiate)
- **Litigation Budget Model**: Structured budget with phases (pleadings, discovery, motions, trial preparation, trial) and monthly/quarterly checkpoints; variance triggers executive review
- **Theory of the Case Development**: Articulate the company's strongest narrative and legal theory early; all subsequent decisions (witnesses, discovery focus, experts) should serve this theory
- **Discovery Management Protocol**: Custodian identification, legal hold implementation, collection methodology, processing and review workflow, production scheduling; each step must be defensible if challenged
- **Outside Counsel Management Framework**: Case inception meeting (strategy alignment), monthly status calls, quarterly budget reviews, milestone billing approvals, and year-end performance evaluation
- **Settlement Decision Framework**: Compare expected value of continued litigation (probability of win × damages avoided) against proposed settlement amount, accounting for litigation cost, management distraction, precedent risk, and reputational factors
- **Class Action Defense Protocol**: Early assessment of class certification vulnerabilities, expert retention strategy, communication controls, and settlement authority framework; class actions require different management than individual cases
- **Litigation Portfolio Review**: Quarterly review of all active matters for status, budget adherence, strategy changes, and settlement opportunities; prevents cases from drifting without active management

## Domain Concepts & Terminology

### Litigation Phases
- **Pre-Litigation**: Demand letters, informal negotiations, and pre-filing assessment; often the best settlement opportunity
- **Pleadings Phase**: Filing of complaint, answer, counterclaims, and pre-answer motions
- **Discovery**: Exchange of evidence — documents, data, interrogatories, admissions, and depositions
- **Dispositive Motions**: Motions for summary judgment seeking dismissal or partial judgment without trial
- **Trial Preparation**: Witness preparation, exhibit organization, trial brief, mock trial, and jury research
- **Trial**: Presentation of evidence and argument to the fact-finder (judge or jury)
- **Appeal**: Challenge to trial court decision in appellate court

### Discovery
- **Legal Hold**: Suspension of document destruction to preserve evidence; must be issued when litigation is reasonably anticipated
- **ESI (Electronically Stored Information)**: Digital documents, emails, and data subject to discovery obligations
- **Custodian**: Person whose documents and communications are subject to collection and review
- **Document Review**: Review of collected documents for responsiveness and privilege before production
- **Deposition**: Sworn oral testimony taken outside of court before trial
- **Interrogatory**: Written question answered under oath during discovery
- **Spoliation**: Destruction or failure to preserve evidence subject to a legal hold; triggers sanctions

### Case Assessment
- **Expected Value (EV)**: Probability-weighted average outcome = (P(win) × damages avoided) − litigation cost
- **Liability**: Legal responsibility for the conduct alleged by the plaintiff
- **Damages**: Financial remedy sought by the plaintiff
- **Indemnification Obligation**: Contractual obligation to cover another party's losses; creates contingent liability
- **Coverage Analysis**: Assessment of whether insurance policies cover the claims alleged

### Resolution Mechanisms
- **Settlement**: Negotiated resolution between parties without judicial decision; most common outcome
- **Mediation**: Facilitated negotiation with a neutral third party; non-binding but often effective
- **Arbitration**: Private adjudication with a neutral arbitrator; binding when agreed to
- **Summary Judgment**: Judicial ruling that eliminates claims or the entire case without trial when facts are undisputed

## Anti-Patterns to Avoid

- **Litigate-and-Review**: Allowing litigation to proceed on autopilot without actively evaluating settlement opportunities at every major milestone; cases that should settle continue to accumulate cost
- **Budget Passivity**: Approving outside counsel invoices without budget-to-actual review; litigation costs regularly exceed initial estimates by 30-50% without active monitoring
- **Discovery Overbreadth**: Conducting discovery without proportionality analysis; collecting and reviewing millions of documents for a $100K dispute is not cost-effective and antagonizes courts
- **No Legal Hold**: Failing to implement legal holds when litigation is reasonably anticipated; spoliation sanctions can include adverse inference instructions, monetary penalties, or case dismissal
- **Witness Under-Preparation**: Allowing witnesses to testify without thorough preparation on the facts, the legal theory, and likely deposition tactics; unprepared witnesses create damaging testimony
- **Outside Counsel Autonomy**: Allowing litigation counsel to make strategic decisions (expert selection, settlement posture, discovery scope) without company direction and approval
- **Single-Theory Tunnel Vision**: Building case strategy around one legal theory without developing alternative arguments; judges and juries often don't reach the outcome counsel expects

## Quality Indicators

- **Early Case Assessment Completion**: ECA completed within 30 days for all new matters above threshold
- **Budget Adherence**: Active matters within 10% of approved budget at each quarterly checkpoint
- **Settlement Rate and Timing**: Percentage of cases resolved through settlement vs. trial; favorable settlement cases closed before heavy litigation cost accumulates
- **Legal Hold Implementation Time**: Hold issued within 24 hours of litigation reasonably anticipated
- **Case Resolution Outcomes**: Win rate and settlement amounts compared to ECA predictions; measures assessment quality
- **Outside Counsel Cost vs. Benchmark**: Per-matter cost relative to industry benchmarks for matter type
- **Post-Litigation Recurrence Rate**: Whether issues resolved in litigation recur; recurring disputes indicate systemic problems not addressed by litigation resolution

## Collaboration Touchpoints

- **With General Counsel**: Receive settlement authority, strategy direction, and escalation for matters exceeding thresholds; GC approves major strategy decisions and board-level reporting
- **With Paralegal**: Coordinate on document management, deadline tracking, filing coordination, and discovery support; paralegal handles execution while litigation manager handles strategy
- **With Corporate Counsel**: Exchange information on corporate governance matters that intersect with litigation (shareholder disputes, derivative actions, securities litigation)
- **With Legal Analyst**: Receive litigation outcome analytics and benchmarking data to improve settlement strategy and cost estimation models
