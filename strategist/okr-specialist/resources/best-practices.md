# Best Practices: OKR Specialist

> Design principles, patterns, and frameworks that guide high-quality OKR planning, objective setting, key result definition, and progress tracking work.

## Design Principles

- **Objectives Inspire, Key Results Verify**: Objectives should be qualitative and motivating; Key Results should be quantitative and unambiguous — if you can't measure it, it's not a Key Result.
- **Comfort is the Enemy of OKRs**: If 100% of Key Results are routinely achieved, the goals are too easy — OKRs should stretch; a 70% achievement rate typically indicates appropriate ambition.
- **Align Vertically and Horizontally**: Company OKRs cascade down through teams, and shared dependencies must be visible horizontally — misaligned OKRs produce invisible conflict.
- **OKRs Are Not Performance Reviews**: Decoupling OKR achievement from compensation decisions preserves the honesty and ambition that make OKRs valuable — punitive OKR systems produce sandbagging.
- **Fewer, Better**: Three to five OKRs per level is the limit; more signals inability to prioritize. Everything important should fit within OKRs; everything outside OKRs is explicitly deprioritized.
- **Leading Indicators Over Lagging**: Key Results that are measurable in-cycle (leading) are more useful than those only visible after the quarter ends (lagging) — course-correct while there's time.
- **Weekly Check-Ins, Not Quarterly Surprises**: OKRs require regular rhythm — weekly confidence updates and blockers surfacing, not a quarterly review of what already happened.

## Key Patterns & Frameworks

- **OKR Hierarchy (Company → Team → Individual)**: Company OKRs set direction; team OKRs translate strategy into execution; individual OKRs align personal goals to team priorities. Each level connects to the level above.
- **SMART Key Results**: Specific, Measurable, Achievable (stretchy), Relevant, Time-bound — apply this filter to every proposed Key Result before finalizing.
- **Moonshot vs. Roofshot OKRs**: Moonshots are aspirational stretch goals (expected ~70% achievement); Roofshots are committed goals (expected ~100% achievement). Distinguish them explicitly so failure is interpreted correctly.
- **Health Metrics (Guardrails)**: Metrics that must not regress while pursuing OKRs (e.g., don't improve conversion at the cost of satisfaction). Define explicitly to prevent optimization that destroys adjacent value.
- **Bidirectional OKR Setting**: Company leadership sets top-level OKRs; teams propose their contribution OKRs bottom-up; final alignment achieved through negotiation. Pure top-down loses ownership; pure bottom-up loses alignment.
- **OKR Scoring (0.0–1.0)**: Grade each Key Result at 0.0 (did not start) through 1.0 (fully achieved). Grade 0.7 on a stretch goal = success. Grade 1.0 on every goal = insufficient ambition.
- **Confidence Indicator System**: Weekly 1-5 confidence rating per Key Result (1 = off track, 3 = uncertain, 5 = on track). Triggers discussion when confidence drops below 3 before crisis develops.
- **OKR Retrospective**: End-of-cycle review: What did we achieve? Where did we miss? What did we learn? What do we carry forward? Must be blameless and focused on improvement.
- **Dependency Mapping**: Before finalizing team OKRs, map cross-team dependencies — Key Results requiring another team's input must be agreed bilaterally, not assumed.

## Domain Concepts & Terminology

### OKR Structure
- **Objective**: Qualitative, inspiring statement of a desired achievement (what we want to accomplish)
- **Key Result**: Quantitative, measurable milestone that tracks progress toward the objective (how we'll know we achieved it)
- **Initiative / Project**: Work undertaken to achieve Key Results — the "how." Initiatives are not OKRs.
- **Stretch Goal**: Ambitious target expected to be achieved ~70% of the time — requires psychological safety to set without fear of failure penalty
- **Committed Goal**: Target expected to be achieved 100% of the time — organizational commitments, not OKRs
- **OKR Cadence**: The regular rhythm of setting, tracking, and reviewing OKRs (typically quarterly for teams, annually for company)

### Alignment
- **Vertical Alignment**: OKRs at lower levels connecting to and supporting OKRs at higher levels
- **Horizontal Alignment**: Dependencies and shared goals visible across peer teams at the same level
- **Cascading**: Process of translating company OKRs into team and individual OKRs through structured conversation
- **OKR Champion**: Organizational role responsible for facilitating OKR process quality and consistency across teams
- **Contribution OKR**: Team OKR explicitly linked to a company OKR — documents which team goals contribute to which company priorities

### Measurement
- **Leading Indicator**: Metric measurable during the cycle that predicts future outcomes (signal); enables course correction
- **Lagging Indicator**: Metric only visible after the period ends (result); confirms but cannot be corrected in-cycle
- **Baseline**: Measured starting value for a Key Result metric before the cycle begins — required for meaningful progress measurement
- **Target**: The specific value or outcome the Key Result aims to achieve by the end of the cycle
- **Scoring**: End-of-cycle 0.0–1.0 grade per Key Result reflecting achievement relative to target

### OKR Health
- **OKR Fatigue**: When OKR processes feel bureaucratic rather than energizing — caused by too many OKRs, punitive scoring, or disconnection from real work
- **Sandbagging**: Setting OKRs with targets already known to be achievable to guarantee 1.0 scores — destroys the stretch mechanism
- **Decoupling**: Explicit organizational policy that OKR scores do not directly determine compensation — preserves honesty and ambition
- **Weekly Check-In**: Short regular update (confidence score, blockers, progress notes) that maintains OKR visibility without requiring heavy reporting

## Anti-Patterns to Avoid

- **Task List OKRs**: Key Results that are binary to-dos ("launch feature X") rather than measurable outcomes ("increase feature adoption from 20% to 45%"). Fix: convert every to-do Key Result to an outcome measure with baseline and target.
- **Too Many OKRs**: Teams with 8-12 OKRs per quarter, preventing meaningful prioritization. Fix: enforce maximum 3-5 OKRs per team; force trade-off conversations to arrive at the vital few.
- **100% Achievement Rate**: Every quarter ends with all Key Results at 1.0 — signals goals were not ambitious enough. Fix: normalize 70% achievement as success on stretch goals; investigate consistently achieving teams for sandbagging.
- **OKRs Disconnected from Daily Work**: OKRs written in January and never referenced until December review. Fix: integrate OKRs into weekly team meetings, sprint planning, and prioritization discussions.
- **Individual OKR Performance Management**: Using individual OKR scores in performance reviews, creating gaming and sandbagging. Fix: explicitly decouple OKR scoring from compensation; reinforce through manager training.
- **Missing Baselines**: Key Results defined without documenting the starting value, making progress measurement impossible. Fix: require a baseline measurement before any Key Result is finalized.
- **Output OKRs at Company Level**: Company OKRs describing activities rather than impact ("run 50 customer interviews" vs. "increase NPS from 32 to 45"). Fix: ensure company-level OKRs are outcome-driven; activities belong at initiative level.

## Quality Indicators

- **OKR Alignment Rate**: % of team OKRs explicitly linked to a company-level OKR (target: >90%).
- **Average Achievement Score**: Mean end-of-cycle score across all Key Results — team average of 0.6-0.7 indicates healthy stretch.
- **Weekly Check-In Participation**: % of teams completing weekly confidence updates (target: >85%) — signals OKR process engagement.
- **Baseline Documentation Rate**: % of Key Results with documented baseline values before cycle start (target: 100%).
- **OKR Freshness**: % of OKRs updated (confidence, progress notes) in the last 2 weeks — stale OKRs indicate process disengagement.
- **Cross-Team Dependency Coverage**: % of cross-team OKR dependencies with bilateral agreement (target: 100%) — undocumented dependencies become Q4 surprises.
- **Retrospective Action Closure**: % of OKR retrospective actions completed before next cycle kick-off.

## Collaboration Touchpoints

- **With Strategic Planner**: Quality looks like company OKRs derived from the strategic plan, with clear line-of-sight from annual strategy to quarterly objectives, and key strategic bets visible in the OKR hierarchy.
- **With Operations Manager**: Quality looks like operational efficiency targets expressed as measurable Key Results with baselines, and OKR tracking integrated into operational review cadence.
- **With HR / Performance Management**: Quality looks like explicit decoupling policy between OKR scores and compensation decisions, manager training on OKR facilitation, and team OKRs visible in performance context only as directional signals.
- **With Product Owner**: Quality looks like product roadmap priorities reflected in product team OKRs, user outcome metrics chosen as Key Results rather than feature completion counts.
