# Best Practices: Voice Coach

> Design principles, patterns, and frameworks that guide high-quality authorial voice development, character voice training, tonal consistency, and voice differentiation work.

## Design Principles

- **Voice Is Not Style**: Voice is the perceived personality of the implied author — the intelligence, attitude, and way of seeing the world that comes through the prose. Style is the technical means by which voice is produced. A writer can change style deliberately; genuine voice is harder to fake.
- **Voice Consistency Is Built, Not Found**: Writers who "can't find their voice" often mean they haven't committed to a perspective. Voice emerges from consistent decisions about what the author notices, how they value things, and how they want to be in relation to the reader.
- **Every Character Has a Distinct Voice**: In fiction, voice applies to each character's narration and dialogue as well as to the authorial narrator. A cast of characters who all sound the same is a voice failure; each character needs their own lexical range, syntactic habits, and perceptual priorities.
- **Voice Authenticity Over Voice Excellence**: An authentic mediocre voice is more engaging than a technically excellent generic voice. Readers form relationships with specific presences; they cannot attach to perfectly correct but characterless prose.
- **Voice Is Observable Before It's Nameable**: Voice can be felt and identified before it can be analytically described. The starting point is identifying examples of the target voice — passages where it's working — and analyzing what's happening technically.
- **Voice Breaks Are Not Always Errors**: Deliberate voice modulation (the narrator stepping back for distance, the character performing a different persona) is not a voice inconsistency. The skill is distinguishing deliberate modulation from accidental drift.
- **Voice Must Fit Its Content**: A light, ironic voice applied to tragedy produces bathos; a solemn voice applied to comedy kills the joke. Voice development must account for the register requirements of the content.

## Key Patterns & Frameworks

- **Voice Parameter Profile**: For any authorial or character voice, document six parameters — (1) Lexical range (simple to complex vocabulary), (2) Syntactic preference (short/declarative to long/periodic), (3) Tonal register (intimate to formal), (4) Attitude toward subject (reverent to ironic), (5) Relationship to reader (inclusive to exclusive), (6) Perceptual priority (what this voice notices first: people, objects, ideas, feelings). The parameter profile is the voice's fingerprint.
- **Voice Example Collection**: Before developing a voice profile analytically, collect 5–10 passages where the voice is working at its best. These examples are the empirical foundation for the analytical description. All subsequent voice development is measured against these exemplars.
- **Voice Consistency Audit**: Read the entire work and mark every sentence that sounds like a different implied author. Voice inconsistencies typically appear at scene transitions, emotional peaks (where the author reaches for elevation the voice can't sustain), and in dialogue-heavy sections (where the author forgets the narration voice).
- **Character Voice Differentiation Matrix**: For a cast of characters, create a matrix showing each character's position on the six voice parameters. Characters should be distinguishable across parameters — no two should have identical profiles.
- **Voice Stretch Exercises**: For voices that are technically adequate but lack distinctiveness, exercises that push toward the extremes — write the same scene with maximum ironic distance; write it with maximum intimacy; write it in the voice of someone who hates the protagonist. Extreme stretching reveals the voice's center.
- **Voice Register Calibration**: Identify the range of registers the voice must accommodate (the intimacy of private scenes, the formality of public scenes, the urgency of action sequences) and ensure the voice profile can accommodate all required registers while remaining recognizable.
- **Dialogue-Narration Voice Coherence**: In first-person and close-third narration, the narrator's voice and the protagonist's dialogue voice must be related without being identical. The narrator is the character reflecting on events; the character in dialogue is in the moment. Both voices must feel like the same person.

## Domain Concepts & Terminology

### Voice Architecture
- **Authorial Voice**: The implied author's presence in the prose — their personality, intelligence, and way of perceiving the world
- **Narrative Voice**: The specific manifestation of authorial voice in a narrative — first, second, or third person; reliable or unreliable; distant or intimate
- **Character Voice**: The specific linguistic and perceptual characteristics of an individual character's narration or dialogue
- **Narrator Voice**: The voice of the narrative agent telling the story; may or may not be a character in the story
- **Implied Author**: The persona constructed by the reader from the text's consistent stylistic and attitudinal choices; the "author" as perceived through the prose

### Voice Parameters
- **Lexical Range**: The vocabulary complexity level of a voice; a child narrator uses simple vocabulary; an academic uses technical vocabulary
- **Syntactic Preference**: The characteristic sentence structures of a voice — simple and declarative vs. complex and periodic
- **Tonal Register**: The formality level of a voice — intimate, conversational, formal, elevated
- **Ironic Distance**: The degree to which the narrator's perspective is detached from and observational of the characters and events
- **Perceptual Priority**: What this voice notices first — objects, people, ideas, sensory details, social dynamics

### Voice Problems
- **Voice Inconsistency**: Passages that sound like a different implied author; typically caused by register mismatches, vocabulary range violations, or tonal shifts
- **Voice Flatness**: Technically correct prose that lacks a distinctive perspective; generic rather than specific; no one's voice
- **Ventriloquism**: The author's voice substituting for a character's voice; the character sounds like the author rather than like themselves
- **Register Drift**: Gradual, unintentional shift in formality level; the voice becomes more elevated or casual without narrative motivation
- **Voice Homogeneity**: In multi-character fiction, all characters and the narrator using the same vocabulary, syntax, and perceptual approach

### Training Concepts
- **Voice Exemplar**: A passage where the target voice is working at its best; the reference point for voice consistency
- **Voice Profile**: The analytical description of a voice's characteristic parameters
- **Voice Stretch**: Exercise pushing the voice toward its boundaries to discover and develop its range
- **Voice Audit**: Systematic review of a text for passages that break the established voice profile

## Anti-Patterns to Avoid

- **Template Voice**: Advising writers to imitate a specific published author's style as their "voice"; imitation produces derivative work, not authentic voice.
- **Generic Excellence**: Pushing toward technically excellent but characterless prose; generic correctness is the enemy of distinctive voice.
- **Voice as Vocabulary List**: Reducing voice development to recommending specific words or phrases; vocabulary is a parameter, not the whole profile.
- **Consistency Policing Without Flexibility**: Flagging every register shift as a voice inconsistency without allowing for deliberate, motivated voice modulation.
- **Voice Without Content Fit**: Developing a voice profile without accounting for the register requirements of the content; even the most distinctive voice must be able to serve its material.
- **Character Voice Neglect**: Focusing exclusively on authorial/narrator voice without developing the distinct voices of individual characters; in fiction, character voice differentiation is equally important.
- **The Universal Voice Standard**: Evaluating all voices against a single standard of "good voice"; voice quality is relative to the content's needs, the genre's conventions, and the intended relationship with the reader.

## Quality Indicators

- **Voice Parameter Documentation**: A written voice profile exists for the authorial voice and each major character voice
- **Exemplar Collection**: At least 5 exemplar passages have been identified where the target voice is working at its best
- **Voice Audit Completion**: A systematic voice consistency audit has been conducted and all identified inconsistencies have been investigated and either resolved or classified as deliberate
- **Character Voice Differentiation**: Characters in the matrix have distinct profiles — no two characters share identical parameter settings
- **Register Range Demonstrated**: The voice profile can be demonstrated working across the required range of registers without losing its distinctive character
- **Voice Authenticity**: The voice profile produces prose that sounds like it comes from a specific, recognizable perspective rather than from an undifferentiated "author"
- **Dialogue-Narration Coherence**: In close first/third person narration, the narrator voice and the protagonist's dialogue voice feel like the same person at different moments

## Collaboration Touchpoints

- **With Prose Stylist**: Prose stylist works on sentence-level technical execution; voice coach defines the voice profile that technical execution must serve. The two roles require close coordination to ensure technical prose choices embody the voice rather than working against it.
- **With Dialogue Specialist**: Voice coach provides character voice profiles; dialogue specialist translates those profiles into actual dialogue. Voice coach validates that dialogue specialist's output maintains voice consistency.
- **With Narrative Director**: Director holds the tonal vision; voice coach ensures individual voice development serves the project's tonal register. Director approves authorial voice direction before detailed voice development begins.
- **With AI Writing Rewriter**: When humanizing AI text, the voice coach defines the target human voice profile; rewriter applies specific techniques to achieve that profile. Voice coaching provides the target; rewriting achieves it.
