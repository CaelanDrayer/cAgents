# Best Practices: Partnership Marketing Manager

> Design principles, patterns, and frameworks that guide high-quality co-marketing partnerships, joint campaigns, and alliance marketing work.

## Design Principles

- **Mutual Value is the Foundation**: Partnerships only survive if both parties are getting clear, measurable value — identify the value exchange before any commitment
- **Audience Complementarity, Not Overlap**: The best co-marketing partners have audiences that are adjacent to yours, not identical — you're looking for reach extension, not duplication
- **Brand Alignment is Non-Negotiable**: A partner whose brand quality or reputation doesn't match yours reflects on you in their audience's eyes
- **Start Small, Scale Proven**: Begin with a low-commitment joint activity (blog post, webinar), measure results, then propose bigger joint investments
- **Shared Goals Prevent Misalignment**: Define specific, agreed KPIs for every partnership activity before execution — prevents "we got value, they didn't feel it" post-mortems
- **Relationship Before Transaction**: Partnership marketing works through trust; prioritize the relationship quality even when any individual campaign underperforms
- **Exclusivity Requires Justification**: Exclusive partnership arrangements limit flexibility; require proportional investment in return for exclusivity commitments

## Key Patterns & Frameworks

- **Partnership Qualification Framework**: Audience fit (ICP overlap?), brand alignment (quality tier match?), content capability (can they produce?), promotion reach (audience size and engagement?), competitive risk (direct competitor or not?) — evaluate before outreach
- **Co-Marketing Asset Menu**: Blog post exchange, co-authored content, joint webinar, partner newsletter feature, co-branded landing page, joint case study, shared social promotion, co-created research report — ranked by effort and expected reach
- **Partnership Brief Template**: Objective, audience segment targeted, each partner's contribution, joint messaging, promotion plan, timeline, success metrics, attribution model, and editorial approval process
- **Joint Campaign Attribution**: Unique UTM parameters, landing page variants, or promo codes per partner — enables individual performance measurement for each party
- **Partner Content Calendar**: Shared document tracking all joint content commitments, production deadlines, review gates, and publication dates for all active partnerships
- **Partnership Tier Framework**: Strategic (dedicated co-selling + marketing investment), preferred (regular joint programming), and associate (ad hoc content exchange) — different investment levels with different expectations
- **Partner Email Sponsorship Framework**: Criteria for when brand appears in partner emails — audience size minimum, ICP match threshold, content context requirements, and mutual sponsorship exchange model
- **Outcome Report Template**: Post-activity summary delivered to partner — impressions, leads generated per party, landing page conversions, social engagement — makes the value tangible and sets up renewal discussion
- **Content Rights Agreement**: Clear upfront agreement on who owns what content, how it may be repurposed, and how both brands must be attributed — prevents post-publication disputes
- **Partnership Health Check**: Quarterly scorecard reviewing pipeline contribution, content volume, audience reach, and relationship engagement — determines whether to invest more, maintain, or phase out

## Domain Concepts & Terminology

### Partnership Types
- **Co-Marketing**: Joint marketing activities where both brands contribute and both benefit from audience exposure
- **Content Partnership**: Collaboration to create jointly authored, co-branded, or promotional content
- **Integration Marketing**: Marketing partnerships built around product integrations — each partner promotes the joint solution to their user base
- **Technology Alliance**: Formal partnership between software companies with complementary products, typically including co-selling and co-marketing commitments
- **OEM / White Label Partner**: Partner who embeds your product in their offering; marketing supports awareness of the underlying partnership

### Campaign Types
- **Joint Webinar**: Co-hosted virtual event where both brands contribute speakers and promotion — high engagement, generates shared leads for both parties
- **Co-Authored Content**: Research report, e-book, or guide produced jointly — higher authority signal than solo content due to two-brand endorsement
- **Partner Newsletter Feature**: Appearing in a partner's email newsletter to reach their subscriber base — often reciprocal
- **Co-Branded Landing Page**: A dedicated page promoting the joint offer or integration, hosted and promoted by both parties
- **Joint Case Study**: Customer story highlighting how the two products work together to deliver a combined outcome

### Measurement
- **Partner-Sourced Leads**: Net new contacts acquired via partner promotion activities
- **Partner-Influenced Pipeline**: Opportunities where a partner marketing touchpoint was part of the buyer journey
- **Co-Marketing ROI**: Revenue or pipeline attributed to joint activities relative to the internal investment in managing and executing them
- **Brand Reach Extension**: Total unique audience reached through partner channels that would not have been reached through own channels

## Anti-Patterns to Avoid

- **Partnerships Without Shared Goals**: Proceeding with co-marketing activities without agreed success metrics creates inevitable disagreement about whether it worked
- **Audience Assumption Without Data**: Assuming a partner's audience matches your ICP without validating demographic and firmographic data leads to irrelevant promotion
- **One-Sided Value Exchange**: Partnerships where one party consistently does more work or generates more value erode over time — monitor balance
- **No Content Approval Process**: Publishing co-branded content without a joint review cycle risks brand guideline violations and factual errors
- **Over-Promising Reach Commitments**: Guaranteeing lead volume or impression numbers in a co-marketing agreement without data to support the commitment damages trust when actuals fall short
- **Ignoring Partnership Performance**: Running co-marketing activities without measuring results prevents learning which partner types and activity formats are worth investing in
- **Treating Partnerships as Transactions**: Sending your partner only campaign briefs without investing in relationship building produces technically compliant but low-enthusiasm execution

## Quality Indicators

- **Active Partnership Rate**: Percentage of formal partners who participated in at least one joint marketing activity in the past quarter
- **Partner Lead Quality**: MQL-to-SQL conversion rate for partner-sourced leads vs. other acquisition channels — measures audience alignment
- **Co-Marketing ROI**: Pipeline generated per dollar of internal investment in partnership marketing activities
- **Partner NPS**: Satisfaction score from partner contacts; below 30 indicates relationship investment is needed
- **Content Production Adherence**: Percentage of co-authored content delivered on schedule without attribution or approval issues
- **Audience Reach Extension**: Percentage of partner-campaign-reached audience that represents new, previously unreachable contacts
- **Partnership Renewal Rate**: Percentage of active partnerships from last year that continue this year — the relationship health signal

## Collaboration Touchpoints

- **With Channel Partner Manager**: Channel partnerships often include co-marketing components — align on which joint activities serve channel goals vs. direct marketing goals and avoid duplication
- **With Campaign Manager**: Joint campaigns must align with the broader campaign calendar and messaging architecture — brief campaign manager before committing to partner timelines
- **With Copywriter**: Co-authored content requires copy that represents both brands authentically; brief the copywriter on the partner's voice guidelines before drafting
- **With Marketing Analyst**: Partnership attribution requires pre-agreed UTM structure and lead source tagging; co-design the tracking approach before each major activity
- **With Brand Manager**: Co-branding guidelines (how both logos appear, whose brand standards govern the joint content) must be established before partner outreach begins
