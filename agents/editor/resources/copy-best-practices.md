# Best Practices: Copywriter

> Design principles, patterns, and frameworks that guide high-quality marketing copy, persuasive writing, and conversion-focused content.

## Design Principles

- **Clarity Before Cleverness**: A clear message that converts beats a clever message that confuses
- **Write to One Person**: Address the ideal reader as an individual, not an anonymous demographic
- **Benefits, Not Features**: What the product does matters less than what it does for the reader
- **The Big Idea First**: Lead with the single most compelling idea — don't bury it in the third paragraph
- **Voice Consistency**: Copy must sound like the brand, not like the individual copywriter
- **CTA Specificity**: "Get started free" outperforms "Submit" every time — be specific about what happens next
- **Read It Aloud**: If it sounds awkward spoken, it reads awkwardly too

## Key Patterns & Frameworks

- **AIDA Framework**: Attention → Interest → Desire → Action — the classic copy structure that guides the reader from awareness to conversion
- **PAS Framework**: Problem → Agitation → Solution — lead with the reader's pain, amplify its urgency, then position your offer as the relief
- **The 4 U's**: Make copy Useful, Unique, Ultra-specific, and Urgent — a checklist for subject lines, headlines, and CTAs
- **Headline Formulas**: "How to [achieve desired outcome] without [common obstacle]", "[Number] ways to [benefit]", "What [authority] doesn't want you to know about [topic]"
- **FAB Copy Structure**: Features → Advantages → Benefits — translates technical attributes into customer-relevant outcomes
- **Emotional Trigger Mapping**: Identify primary emotional drivers (fear, desire, social proof, authority, scarcity) and match copy tone to the dominant trigger for each audience
- **The Inverted Pyramid**: Lead with the most important information; details follow — used for digital copy where readers scan before committing to read
- **Before/After Bridge**: Open by describing the reader's current painful state, describe the ideal future state, bridge to your offer as the path between them
- **StoryBrand Framework**: Position the customer as the hero, your brand as the guide, and structure the narrative around a clear call to action and stakes
- **Email Copywriting Stack**: Subject line (open) → preview text (reinforce open) → hook (first line that earns the read) → body (build desire) → CTA (one clear action)

## Domain Concepts & Terminology

### Copy Types
- **Long-Form Copy**: Sales pages, white papers, email sequences — length is justified by the level of conviction required (high-commitment offers)
- **Short-Form Copy**: Ads, subject lines, CTAs, social posts — maximum impact in minimum words
- **Conversion Copy**: Copy optimized specifically for driving a single action (sign-up, purchase, download)
- **Nurture Copy**: Email and content copy designed to build relationship and move prospects down the funnel over time
- **UX Copy / Microcopy**: Button labels, error messages, onboarding prompts — small words with outsized impact on user behavior

### Persuasion Mechanics
- **Social Proof**: Testimonials, case studies, reviews, user counts — others' behavior validates the prospect's decision
- **Authority**: Expert endorsements, credentials, media mentions, certifications — establishes credibility
- **Scarcity**: Limited availability (genuine) creates urgency; overused scarcity destroys trust
- **Specificity**: Specific numbers ("37% more conversions") are more persuasive than vague claims ("significantly more conversions")
- **Objection Handling**: Anticipating and addressing reasons not to act before they arise in the reader's mind

### Technical Copy Elements
- **Subject Line**: The single line of text that determines whether an email is opened
- **Preview Text**: The snippet shown after the subject line in email clients — should complement, not repeat, the subject
- **Above-the-Fold Hook**: The headline and first sentence visible without scrolling — must earn the read
- **CTA (Call to Action)**: The instruction that tells the reader exactly what to do next and frames the benefit of doing it
- **Value Proposition**: The clear, concise statement of the specific benefit your offer delivers to a defined audience

### Brand Voice
- **Tone of Voice**: The personality and emotional register expressed in copy — must remain consistent across touchpoints
- **Brand Voice Guidelines**: The documented rules governing vocabulary, sentence structure, and personality for all brand copy
- **On-Brand vs. Off-Brand**: Distinction between copy that embodies brand personality and copy that sounds generic or contradictory

## Anti-Patterns to Avoid

- **Jargon Overload**: Industry buzzwords signal sophistication to insiders but alienate everyone else — write for the informed layperson
- **Feature-First Copy**: Leading with what the product is rather than what problem it solves misses the reader's actual self-interest
- **Passive Voice Epidemic**: Passive constructions drain energy from copy — "Results are achieved" vs. "You'll achieve results"
- **Buried CTAs**: Expecting readers to scroll to find the action step — place CTAs above the fold and repeat them
- **Trying to Speak to Everyone**: Copy that speaks to everyone speaks to no one; get specific about who you're addressing
- **The Hedge**: Softening every claim with qualifiers ("may help you possibly consider…") destroys confidence and purchase intent
- **Ignoring the Subject Line**: Spending hours on email body copy and minutes on the subject line inverts where the leverage is

## Quality Indicators

- **Headline Hook Rate**: Does the headline immediately communicate a benefit or spark curiosity specific to the target audience?
- **CTA Clarity Score**: Is it obvious what will happen when the reader clicks — no ambiguity about the next step?
- **Voice Consistency**: Does the copy sound like the brand, not a generic template?
- **Benefits-to-Features Ratio**: Are there more benefit statements than feature descriptions?
- **Objection Coverage**: Does the copy address the top 2–3 reasons the audience might not convert?
- **Readability Score**: Is the Flesch-Kincaid reading level appropriate for the target audience?
- **A/B Test Lift**: When copy variants are tested, does the data validate the copy choices or suggest iteration?

## Collaboration Touchpoints

- **With Brand Manager**: Copy must reflect brand voice guidelines — review guidelines before starting any new campaign; flag when brand voice conflicts with conversion best practices
- **With Campaign Manager**: Receive campaign briefs with audience, objective, and message framework before writing; present drafts with strategic rationale tied back to the brief
- **With Creative Director**: Copy and visual design must be developed in relationship — headline changes can invalidate visual direction and vice versa
- **With Email Marketing Specialist**: Email copy, subject lines, and preview text must be written with ESP character limits, rendering, and deliverability in mind
- **With Conversion Rate Optimizer**: Provide multiple headline and CTA variants for A/B testing; debrief after tests to understand which copy approaches perform and why
