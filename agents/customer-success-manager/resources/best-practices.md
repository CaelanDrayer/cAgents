# Best Practices: Customer Success Manager

> Design principles, patterns, and frameworks that guide high-quality customer success and value realization work.

## Design Principles

- **Proactive Over Reactive**: Great customer success prevents problems before they surface; by the time a customer signals distress, the underlying issue has been developing for weeks
- **Value Realization First**: Everything a CSM does should connect to the customer achieving their stated business goals; activity without outcome measurement is busy work
- **Business Outcomes, Not Features**: Customers buy outcomes, not products; frame every conversation around the business result being achieved, not the features being used
- **Health Score as Early Warning System**: Don't wait for customers to tell you something is wrong; health signals reveal risk early enough to intervene effectively
- **Success Plan as Living Document**: A success plan that isn't reviewed and updated quarterly is a false sense of security; it must reflect the customer's current reality and goals
- **Champion Development**: Building relationships with one sponsor is fragile; invest in developing multiple internal champions at different levels
- **Time-to-Value Obsession**: Every day a new customer hasn't achieved their first value milestone is a day churn risk is accumulating; accelerate time-to-value relentlessly

## Key Patterns & Frameworks

- **Customer Health Score Model**: Composite signal from product usage depth, login frequency, feature adoption rate, support ticket sentiment, NPS, and engagement responsiveness; score drives CS prioritization
- **Success Plan Framework**: Document customer goals, success metrics, milestones with owners, identified risks, and planned touchpoints; review quarterly with the customer
- **Adoption Milestone Mapping**: Define product adoption stages (activated, engaged, adopted, expanded) with specific behaviors that define each; track customers against this map
- **QBR (Quarterly Business Review)**: Structured executive review of value delivered against goals, product roadmap alignment, and mutual commitments for the next quarter; strongest retention mechanism available
- **LAER Model**: Land → Adopt → Expand → Renew; customer success drives the Adopt and Expand stages of the commercial lifecycle
- **Churn Risk Signal Framework**: Leading indicators (declining usage, champion departure, support escalations, missed milestones) vs. lagging indicators (NPS drop, renewal negotiation posture); act on leading signals
- **Segmentation-Driven Engagement Model**: High-touch (dedicated CSM, weekly or bi-weekly contact) for enterprise, low-touch (digital nudges, monthly check-ins) for SMB; match engagement intensity to account value
- **Playbook-Driven Consistency**: Document plays for common scenarios (new customer onboarding, renewal risk, champion departure, expansion opportunity); consistency ensures quality doesn't depend on individual heroics
- **Customer Journey Mapping**: Identify every touchpoint from sale to renewal, assess customer emotional state at each stage, and design interventions to improve the lowest-performing moments

## Domain Concepts & Terminology

### Health & Risk
- **Customer Health Score**: Composite metric combining usage, engagement, support, and sentiment to predict renewal probability
- **Churn Risk Flag**: Threshold or signal that triggers a proactive intervention playbook
- **At-Risk Account**: Customer with health score below threshold or exhibiting warning signals requiring immediate action
- **Champion**: Internal customer advocate who drives adoption and defends the renewal; losing a champion is a leading churn signal
- **Executive Sponsor**: Senior customer stakeholder who has staked their credibility on the product's success
- **NPS (Net Promoter Score)**: Customer loyalty metric based on likelihood to recommend; 9-10 = Promoter, 7-8 = Passive, 0-6 = Detractor

### Success Planning
- **Success Plan**: Documented agreement between CSM and customer capturing goals, milestones, success metrics, and mutual commitments
- **Mutual Action Plan (MAP)**: Time-bound checklist co-owned with the customer tracking specific actions required from both parties
- **Time-to-Value (TTV)**: Time from contract close to customer achieving their first measurable business outcome
- **Value Realization**: The customer achieving the business outcomes they purchased the product to achieve
- **Milestone**: Specific, measurable achievement that marks progress toward the customer's ultimate success goal

### Adoption & Engagement
- **Product Adoption Rate**: Percentage of licensed users actively using the product; low adoption is a churn predictor
- **Feature Adoption Depth**: Breadth of product features a customer regularly uses; shallow adoption indicates underdeveloped value realization
- **Daily/Monthly Active Users (DAU/MAU)**: Usage frequency metrics indicating customer engagement level
- **Onboarding**: Structured process to get a new customer to their first value milestone; typically 30-90 days
- **Activation**: First moment when a user completes a key action that correlates with long-term retention

### Commercial
- **Net Revenue Retention (NRR)**: Revenue from existing customers including expansion minus contraction and churn; primary CSM commercial metric
- **Renewal**: Extension of a customer's contract at expiration; primary near-term objective when within 90 days
- **Expansion**: Revenue growth within an existing customer (additional seats, products, use cases)
- **QBR (Quarterly Business Review)**: Structured meeting with customer leadership to review value delivered and plan next quarter
- **EBR (Executive Business Review)**: Variant of QBR with C-suite or VP-level participation from both sides

## Anti-Patterns to Avoid

- **Reactive Support CSM**: Functioning primarily as an escalation path rather than proactively driving adoption and outcomes; customers don't need another support tier, they need a strategic partner
- **Vanity Metric Reporting**: Showing customers feature utilization counts rather than business outcomes achieved; customers care about their results, not your feature analytics
- **Single-Threaded Relationships**: Building the entire customer relationship through one contact; when that person leaves, the account is at severe risk
- **Annual-Only Success Plan Reviews**: Creating success plans at onboarding and never updating them; goals and priorities change, success plans must change with them
- **Renewal as a Transaction**: Treating the renewal as an end-of-year event to close rather than a natural outcome of ongoing value delivery
- **Adoption Without Purpose**: Driving feature adoption as a goal in itself without connecting feature use to the customer's specific business outcomes
- **Ignoring Health Score Signals**: Noticing declining usage or NPS drop but delaying intervention; health signals degrade faster than they recover

## Quality Indicators

- **NRR Above 110%**: Expansion more than offsets any churn; strongest indicator of customer success program effectiveness
- **Success Plan Coverage**: Percentage of accounts with current, reviewed success plans
- **QBR Completion Rate**: Percentage of accounts completing quarterly business reviews on schedule
- **Time-to-First-Value Under Target**: New customers achieving their first milestone within the defined onboarding window
- **Churn Prediction Accuracy**: Percentage of churned accounts that were flagged at-risk 90+ days before renewal; measures health score effectiveness
- **Intervention Conversion Rate**: Percentage of flagged at-risk accounts that renew after proactive CSM intervention
- **Champion Depth Per Account**: Average number of active contacts per account; multi-threaded relationships are more resilient

## Collaboration Touchpoints

- **With Account Manager**: Hand off expansion opportunities when customers are ready to grow; CSM surfaces the signal, AM owns the commercial conversation
- **With Customer Education Specialist**: Trigger training recommendations at specific adoption milestones; education accelerates feature adoption and value realization
- **With Support Director**: Escalate support patterns that threaten customer success; persistent unresolved support issues are a direct churn driver
- **With Product**: Relay customer feedback, feature requests, and adoption blockers; CSM has the most direct insight into what customers actually need from the product
