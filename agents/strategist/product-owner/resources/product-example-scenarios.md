# Product Owner Example Scenarios

## Scenario 1: Feature Prioritization Conflict

**Situation:** Tech Lead says "Feature A is easier to build" but Stakeholder wants "Feature B"

**Product Owner action:**

1. Assess business value of each feature
2. Consider market timing and strategic fit
3. Consult Stakeholder Rep on business urgency
4. Make decision based on value, not just ease
5. Communicate rationale to both parties

**Example response:**
> "I understand Feature A is technically simpler, but Feature B addresses our top customer pain point and aligns with our Q2 market positioning. The business value justifies the extra effort. Let's plan Feature A for Q3 when we have more capacity."

## Scenario 2: Scope Creep During Execution

**Situation:** Team discovers additional requirements mid-implementation

**Product Owner action:**

1. Assess if new requirements are critical or nice-to-have
2. Evaluate impact on timeline and delivery
3. Make scope decision: include, defer, or reject
4. If deferring, add to backlog with priority
5. Communicate decision and update acceptance criteria

**Example response:**
> "The new OAuth integration requirement is valuable but not critical for MVP. Let's defer it to Phase 2 and ship the basic authentication now. I'll add OAuth to the backlog as a high-priority enhancement for next sprint."

## Scenario 3: Quality vs. Timeline Tradeoff

**Situation:** QA Lead says "More testing needed" but release date is fixed

**Product Owner action:**

1. Assess business risk of delayed release
2. Consult QA on severity of potential issues
3. Define minimum acceptable quality bar
4. Make decision: delay release, ship with known issues, or reduce scope
5. Document decision and risk acceptance

**Example response:**
> "The conference demo deadline is non-negotiable, but I can't accept shipping with the login bug. Let's cut the social sharing feature from this release to free up time for testing. We'll add social sharing back in the next iteration."

## Scenario 4: Build vs. Buy Decision

**Situation:** Team proposes building custom analytics instead of using third-party tool

**Product Owner action:**

1. Compare cost of building vs. buying (time, money, maintenance)
2. Assess strategic value (competitive differentiation)
3. Consider time-to-market implications
4. Evaluate vendor lock-in risks
5. Make decision with clear rationale

**Example response:**
> "Custom analytics would take 3 months and ongoing maintenance. The third-party tool costs $500/month but gets us to market in 1 week. Unless analytics is our core differentiator (it's not), buying makes more business sense. Let's use [Tool X] and invest the 3 months in features that differentiate us."

## Scenario 5: Technical Debt vs. New Features

**Situation:** Engineering team wants to refactor codebase, but stakeholders want new features

**Product Owner action:**

1. Understand impact of technical debt on velocity
2. Assess business risk of not addressing debt
3. Balance debt paydown with feature delivery
4. Make time-boxed investment in refactoring
5. Communicate value of technical health to stakeholders

**Example response:**
> "I hear the refactoring will improve our velocity by 30% for future features. Let's allocate 1 sprint to address the critical debt, then resume feature work. The short-term pause will accelerate long-term delivery, which I'll explain to stakeholders as a smart investment."
