# Best Practices: Learning Specialist

> Design principles, patterns, and frameworks that guide high-quality learning program design, career development, and training effectiveness work.

## Design Principles

- **Learning Transfer is the Goal**: The measure of effective training is behavior change on the job — not completion rates, satisfaction scores, or test scores; design backward from application, not from content.
- **70-20-10 Allocation**: 70% of learning happens through on-the-job experience (stretch assignments, projects), 20% through relationships (coaching, mentoring, peer learning), 10% through formal training; most training budgets invert this.
- **Needs Analysis Precedes Design**: Building training without diagnosing the root cause of the performance gap wastes money; training fixes knowledge and skill gaps, not motivation or system gaps.
- **Learner-Centered Design**: Adult learners (andragogy) are self-directed, motivated by relevance to their work, and bring experience to build on; design for application, not just knowledge transfer.
- **Shorter is More Effective**: Cognitive load limits how much people learn in a single session; microlearning (5-15 minute modules) and spaced repetition outperform full-day workshops for skill building.
- **Measurement Enables Investment**: Learning programs without evaluation data cannot prove their value; build Kirkpatrick Level 1-4 measurement into program design before launch.
- **Career Frameworks Create Clarity**: Employees who understand what mastery looks like and how to progress are more engaged, better performers, and more likely to grow within the organization.

## Key Patterns & Frameworks

- **ADDIE Instructional Design Model**: Analysis (identify performance gaps) → Design (learning objectives, content structure) → Development (create content) → Implementation (deliver) → Evaluation (measure outcomes); the backbone of professional learning design.
- **Kirkpatrick Four-Level Evaluation**: Level 1 (Reaction/satisfaction) → Level 2 (Learning/knowledge gained) → Level 3 (Behavior/on-the-job application) → Level 4 (Results/business impact); most programs only measure L1 and L2.
- **Bloom's Taxonomy**: Hierarchy of learning objectives — Remember → Understand → Apply → Analyze → Evaluate → Create; effective training objectives target Apply and above, not just Remember.
- **Spaced Repetition Design**: Distribute learning over time (initial session → 3-day review → 1-week reinforcement → 1-month application check) to exploit the spacing effect and improve retention.
- **Learning Path Architecture**: Structured progression from foundational to advanced competencies for a role or skill domain; makes development intentional rather than ad-hoc.
- **Competency Model Framework**: Defined set of knowledge, skills, and behaviors expected at each level in a role family; enables consistent selection, development, and performance assessment.
- **Manager Effectiveness Program Model**: Separate curriculum track for people managers covering foundational skills (feedback, 1:1s, goal setting, coaching) and advanced skills (talent development, team building, navigating conflict).
- **LMS Governance Framework**: Defined roles (catalog owner, course approver, learner admin), content lifecycle (active → review → retire), and reporting standards for the learning management system.

## Domain Concepts & Terminology

### Instructional Design
- **Learning Objectives**: Specific, measurable statements of what learners will be able to do after a learning experience; the design anchor for all content and assessment decisions
- **Performance Gap**: The difference between current and desired performance; training is only appropriate when the gap is caused by a knowledge or skill deficiency
- **Needs Assessment**: Systematic process of identifying performance gaps, root causes, and optimal solutions before designing training
- **SME (Subject Matter Expert)**: Domain expert who provides content knowledge for learning design; the instructional designer shapes that knowledge into learning experiences
- **Storyboard**: Pre-production blueprint for e-learning courses showing screen layout, narration script, interactions, and media elements
- **Scenario-Based Learning**: Training methodology using realistic situations to teach decision-making and application; more effective than lecture for behavior change

### Delivery Methods
- **ILT (Instructor-Led Training)**: Synchronous in-person classroom training; high interaction, high cost, best for complex skills and team-based content
- **VILT (Virtual Instructor-Led Training)**: Synchronous online training via video conferencing; enables remote delivery but requires stronger facilitation skills
- **E-Learning / CBT (Computer-Based Training)**: Asynchronous self-paced digital courses; scalable and consistent but limited interactivity
- **Blended Learning**: Combination of multiple delivery methods (pre-work e-learning + live workshop + post-training coaching); best results for complex skill development
- **Microlearning**: Short, focused learning modules (5-15 minutes) addressing a single learning objective; effective for reinforcement, quick reference, and mobile learning
- **Social Learning**: Learning through observation, discussion, and collaboration with peers; facilitated through communities of practice, peer coaching, and collaborative tools

### Learning Technology
- **LMS (Learning Management System)**: Platform for hosting, delivering, assigning, and tracking training; examples: Cornerstone, TalentLMS, Docebo, Lessonly, Litmos
- **SCORM**: Technical standard for e-learning content packaging; enables completion tracking and scoring in any compliant LMS
- **xAPI / Tin Can**: Modern standard capturing learning data beyond LMS (simulations, on-the-job performance, social learning); enables Learning Record Store (LRS)
- **Learning Record Store (LRS)**: Database storing xAPI learning data from multiple sources; enables comprehensive learning analytics

### Career Development
- **Career Lattice**: Non-linear career development model where employees move laterally, diagonally, or vertically based on skills and interests (not just upward)
- **Individual Development Plan (IDP)**: Employee-owned document capturing development goals, learning activities, and timelines; effective IDPs are specific, aligned to career goals, and reviewed regularly
- **Stretch Assignment**: Developmental job assignment slightly beyond current capability; the most effective development intervention (70%)
- **Mentoring**: Developmental relationship where a more experienced person shares knowledge and perspective; longer-term, broader in scope than coaching
- **Coaching**: Structured process where a trained coach helps an individual develop skills through questioning and reflection; performance-focused and time-bounded

## Anti-Patterns to Avoid

- **Training as the Default Solution**: Reflexively recommending training for any performance problem without needs analysis; training fixes skill gaps but not motivation, process, or system issues.
- **Completion Rate as the Success Metric**: Optimizing for course completion rates rather than learning transfer and behavior change; 100% completion of ineffective training is worse than 50% completion of effective training.
- **Event-Based Learning Architecture**: Designing learning as isolated events (annual training day) rather than continuous learning experiences with reinforcement; single events produce minimal retention.
- **Content Dump Design**: Building courses that transfer all available information about a topic rather than targeting the specific behaviors learners need to perform; cognitive overload prevents learning.
- **SME-Only Content Development**: Letting subject matter experts design and deliver training without instructional design input; results in lecture-heavy, learner-passive experiences with low application rates.
- **Learning Without Career Framework Integration**: Building training programs disconnected from competency models and career paths; learners don't know what development is expected and development feels random.
- **Neglecting Level 3-4 Evaluation**: Measuring only learner satisfaction (Level 1) or knowledge gain (Level 2) without measuring on-the-job behavior change (Level 3) or business results (Level 4); prevents demonstrating ROI.

## Quality Indicators

- **Kirkpatrick L1 Satisfaction**: Average satisfaction score ≥4.0/5.0 for facilitated training programs
- **Kirkpatrick L2 Knowledge Gain**: Pre/post assessment improvement ≥20% on knowledge checks for targeted programs
- **Kirkpatrick L3 Application Rate**: 60%+ of learners report applying specific skills within 30 days (measured via follow-up survey or manager observation)
- **Training Hours per Employee**: 40+ hours of learning activity per employee annually (cross-referencing formal, informal, and experiential)
- **Completion Rate**: 80%+ course completion rate for assigned mandatory and recommended training
- **Internal Promotion Rate**: % of roles filled internally trend upward as learning investment increases; indicator of career development program effectiveness
- **Time to Productivity**: New hire time to full productivity decreasing year-over-year; indicator of onboarding and role-specific training effectiveness

## Collaboration Touchpoints

- **With Onboarding Specialist**: New hire learning content (role-specific training, culture, tools) is co-designed; onboarding specialist owns the new hire experience arc, learning specialist owns content quality and curriculum design.
- **With Performance Management Specialist**: Development planning is the connection between performance review findings and targeted learning; co-design development plan templates and manager coaching guides.
- **With HR Business Partner**: HRBPs identify capability gaps at the business unit level; learning specialist translates those gaps into learning solutions and measures outcomes against business unit goals.
- **With HR Manager**: Learning strategy and budget are approved through HR manager; align on learning priorities against people strategy and headcount growth plans.
- **With Organizational Development Specialist**: Leadership development and change capability programs are joint territory; OD specialist owns organizational context and change strategy, learning specialist owns instructional design and delivery.
