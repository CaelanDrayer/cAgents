# Best Practices: Sales Strategist

> Design principles, patterns, and frameworks that guide high-quality sales strategy development, market segmentation, and go-to-market design work.

## Design Principles

- **Strategy Without Execution is Theory**: The best sales strategy is useless if it can't be operationalized by the actual sales team — design for implementability
- **Segment First, Strategy Second**: Trying to sell everything to everyone produces undifferentiated, inefficient sales motions; segmentation is the prerequisite
- **Sales Motion Must Match Buyer**: A complex enterprise sales motion applied to SMB buyers is too expensive; a self-serve motion applied to enterprise deals leaves revenue on the table
- **Incentives Drive Behavior**: Sales strategy is only as effective as the compensation structure that incentivizes the right activities and outcomes
- **Territory Design is Revenue Design**: How territories are carved determines whether reps can realistically achieve quota and whether coverage gaps leave market opportunity untouched
- **Competitive Strategy is Continuous**: Competitive landscapes shift; static competitive positioning becomes a liability — maintain a current competitive response playbook
- **Align Marketing and Sales or Neither Wins**: ICP, positioning, messaging, and pipeline targets must be shared agreements, not parallel functional plans

## Key Patterns & Frameworks

- **Sales Model Selection Framework**: Direct enterprise (complex, high-ACV, long cycle) vs. inside sales (mid-market, phone/video, moderate cycle) vs. self-serve/PLG (low ACV, product-led, minimal human touch) vs. channel (indirect, partner-enabled) — selection based on ACV, buyer complexity, and margin economics
- **Ideal Customer Profile (ICP) Development**: Firmographic analysis of best customers (size, industry, revenue, tech stack, growth stage) + behavioral analysis (deal velocity, expansion rate, churn rate, NPS) + win pattern analysis → tightly defined ICP
- **Market Segmentation Framework**: Total addressable market → ICP segment → reachable segment → current pipeline — each layer gets more specific; sales strategy governs how to cover each segment
- **Territory Design Framework**: Geographic or vertical segmentation → named account assignment within segments → account tiers by opportunity size → coverage ratios (reps per accounts) → quota allocation by territory
- **Sales Capacity Planning Model**: Revenue target ÷ average quota = required productive reps; factor in ramp time, attrition, and seasonality to determine hiring timeline
- **Competitive Response Playbook**: Segment-level analysis of win/loss patterns by competitor → positioning against each major competitor → objection handling → when to compete aggressively vs. qualify out
- **Sales Methodology Selection and Deployment**: Choosing the right sales methodology (MEDDPICC, Challenger, SPIN, Sandler) for the buyer type and sales motion → standardized across all reps → reinforced in hiring, onboarding, and coaching
- **GTM Metrics Cascade**: Revenue target → required bookings → required pipeline (pipeline coverage × win rate) → required MQLs (pipeline ÷ MQL-to-SQL rate) → required leads (MQLs ÷ lead-to-MQL rate) — aligns marketing input targets to revenue output targets
- **Account Tiering Model**: Tier 1 (top named accounts, strategic investment, executive relationship program), Tier 2 (select accounts, targeted programs, dedicated rep), Tier 3 (volume accounts, inside sales, digital-first) — different GTM models per tier
- **Annual GTM Planning Process**: Revenue target → market analysis → segment prioritization → sales model → capacity plan → territory design → quota setting → compensation design → enablement plan → Q1 readiness

## Domain Concepts & Terminology

### Sales Strategy
- **GTM (Go-to-Market) Strategy**: The integrated plan for reaching target customers, deploying the sales team, and achieving revenue goals
- **Sales Motion**: The specific approach to engaging, qualifying, and closing a segment of buyers — enterprise, inside, self-serve, or channel
- **Sales Model**: The combination of sales motion, team structure, and economics that defines how revenue is generated
- **ICP (Ideal Customer Profile)**: The firmographic and behavioral definition of your highest-value, most winnable customer segment
- **Land and Expand**: A GTM motion that acquires customers at a small initial footprint and grows revenue through expansion over time

### Territory & Quota
- **Sales Territory**: The defined set of accounts or geography assigned to a rep or team
- **Quota**: The revenue target assigned to a rep, team, or region for a given period
- **Quota Attainment Distribution**: The statistical distribution of quota attainment across all reps — healthy distribution has the majority above 75% with a long right tail
- **OTE (On-Target Earnings)**: The total compensation (base + variable) a rep receives at 100% quota attainment
- **Coverage Ratio**: Number of sales reps relative to the addressable market — under-coverage leaves market opportunity, over-coverage compresses territories below quota viability

### Competitive Intelligence
- **Win Rate by Competitor**: Historical percentage of deals won when a specific competitor was present in the evaluation
- **Competitive Displacement**: A targeted sales motion specifically designed to win accounts currently using a competitor's product
- **SWOT Analysis**: Strengths, Weaknesses, Opportunities, Threats — framework for competitive position assessment
- **Battlecard**: Sales-facing document summarizing competitive positioning for a specific competitor

## Anti-Patterns to Avoid

- **Undifferentiated ICP**: Defining the ICP as "companies that could benefit from our product" provides no actionable guidance for targeting, messaging, or territory design
- **Sales Model Mismatch**: Applying an enterprise-grade sales process to SMB buyers creates a CAC that exceeds LTV; applying self-serve to enterprise buyers loses deals requiring human judgment
- **Strategy Without Capacity Planning**: Setting aggressive revenue targets without modeling whether the current sales team capacity can achieve them sets the team up to fail
- **Territory Inequality**: Widely unequal territory opportunity across reps produces high attrition in under-resourced territories and frustration in over-resourced ones
- **Competitive Myopia**: Focusing only on current primary competitors while ignoring emerging competitors or substitutes produces strategies that are disrupted from outside your field of view
- **Annual Plan Without Agile Adjustment**: Locking the GTM strategy for the full year without a mechanism for mid-year market response produces stale strategy in a changing environment
- **Compensation Plan Complexity**: Overly complex commission structures with too many variables confuse reps about what to optimize for — simplicity drives the intended behavior

## Quality Indicators

- **ICP Specificity**: Can the ICP definition be used to produce a targeted company list with specific firmographic filters?
- **Quota Attainment Distribution**: Is >60% of the sales team above 75% of quota? Distribution below this threshold indicates quota, territory, or capacity problems
- **Win Rate Trend by Segment**: Are win rates improving in priority segments, indicating strategy effectiveness?
- **Sales Capacity Coverage**: Does the current sales headcount cover the addressable market at the required coverage ratio?
- **Competitive Win Rate**: Win rate when specific competitors are present — should be above 50% for segments you've prioritized
- **Territory Balance**: Standard deviation of territory opportunity value — high deviation indicates imbalanced territory design
- **GTM Metric Cascade Accuracy**: Are all funnel metrics (leads, MQLs, SQLs, pipeline) on track to achieve the revenue target, or is there a specific stage gap that needs strategy adjustment?

## Collaboration Touchpoints

- **With Marketing Strategist**: GTM strategy and marketing strategy must target the same segments with compatible messaging; joint quarterly alignment prevents functional divergence that confuses buyers
- **With Revenue Operations Manager**: Sales strategy defines what the team should do; RevOps builds the systems, data, and processes that enable it — capacity planning, territory design, and quota models require joint ownership
- **With Sales Analyst**: Strategy must be grounded in win rate, velocity, and competitive data; sales analyst provides the analytical foundation; strategist interprets implications
- **With Product Marketing Manager**: Competitive strategy and ICP definition require shared intelligence on competitive positioning and buyer motivations — joint development of competitive response playbooks
- **With Sales Trainer**: Sales methodology selection and rollout is a joint decision; strategist defines the methodology; trainer operationalizes it in onboarding and coaching programs
