# Best Practices: HR Manager

> Design principles, patterns, and frameworks that guide high-quality HR management, people operations coordination, and employee lifecycle work.

## Design Principles

- **People Strategy Flows From Business Strategy**: HR priorities must be grounded in what the business actually needs to execute its strategy — not standalone HR programs that sound good in isolation.
- **Consistent Process = Perceived Fairness**: Employees care deeply about whether processes are applied consistently; inconsistency in hiring, performance, or discipline damages trust faster than any policy change.
- **Coordinate, Don't Duplicate**: HR managers who do specialist work (investigations, analytics, recruiting) instead of coordinating specialists create bottlenecks and quality gaps; build a team and delegate.
- **Metrics Make People Work Visible**: HR functions that cannot quantify their impact are vulnerable to budget cuts; build measurement into every people program from the start.
- **Manager Capability is the Force Multiplier**: Investing in manager effectiveness multiplies returns on every other HR investment (engagement, performance, retention); prioritize manager development.
- **Policy Enables Autonomy**: Well-designed policies reduce the number of exceptions that escalate to HR by giving employees and managers clear guardrails to operate within.
- **Lifecycle Continuity**: Candidate experience, onboarding quality, performance management, and offboarding experience are all connected — a gap in any stage affects retention and employer brand.

## Key Patterns & Frameworks

- **Employee Lifecycle Model**: Attract → Recruit → Onboard → Develop → Retain → Transition/Offboard → Alumni; manage each stage deliberately with clear owners, processes, and metrics.
- **HR Service Delivery Model (Ulrich)**: Stratified service delivery — HR Business Partners (strategic advising) + HR Ops/Shared Services (transactional efficiency) + Centers of Excellence (specialized expertise like comp, LD, compliance).
- **People Priorities Cascade**: Business strategy → People strategy → HR program priorities → HR team OKRs → individual HR team goals; ensures alignment from company goal to daily HR work.
- **HR Calendar Framework**: Annual calendar of recurring HR activities (performance cycles, engagement surveys, comp review, compliance filings, benefits OE) to prevent operational surprises and allow lead time.
- **New Manager Onboarding Program**: Structured first-90-days program for new people managers covering expectations, HR processes, policy overview, and leadership skills — distinct from individual contributor onboarding.
- **HR Policy Review Cadence**: Annual review of all HR policies against legal changes, operational learnings, and cultural evolution; formal approval process with documented sign-off.
- **People Team OKR Framework**: Quarterly objectives for the HR function aligned to business unit needs; measures strategic HR contribution rather than only operational throughput.
- **Escalation Matrix**: Clear decision rights defining what HR team members can decide independently versus what requires HR manager approval versus what requires executive sign-off.

## Domain Concepts & Terminology

### HR Operations
- **HR Service Level Agreement (SLA)**: Defined response time commitments for HR services — new hire processing, policy questions, offboarding completion; creates accountability and manages expectations
- **Ticket/Request System**: Intake mechanism for employee and manager HR questions; enables volume tracking, SLA measurement, and workload distribution
- **Employee Handbook**: Central documentation of company policies and employee expectations; legally significant, requires regular review and distribution documentation
- **Policy Exception Process**: Formal workflow for approving deviations from standard HR policy; prevents ad-hoc exceptions that create inconsistency and legal exposure
- **Headcount Reconciliation**: Regular validation that HRIS records match payroll, finance, and business unit headcount — critical for accurate reporting

### Employee Lifecycle
- **Candidate Experience**: Perception of the company formed during the recruiting process; directly impacts offer acceptance rates and employer brand
- **Onboarding Effectiveness**: Measured by time-to-productivity, 90-day retention, and new hire satisfaction scores
- **Performance Management Cycle**: Structured process of goal setting, mid-year check-in, year-end review, and calibration; typically annual with continuous feedback overlay
- **Internal Mobility**: Movement of employees to new roles within the company (lateral, promotion, rotation); indicator of development culture and career growth health
- **Regrettable Attrition**: Departure of employees the organization wanted to retain; the most important turnover metric for HR managers
- **Offboarding Process**: Structured departure workflow covering final pay, benefits end dates, equipment return, access removal, exit interview, and knowledge transfer

### People Programs
- **Employer Value Proposition (EVP)**: The set of experiences and benefits that define why people join and stay; should be authentic to the actual employee experience
- **Retention Strategy**: Set of proactive interventions (compensation adjustments, development opportunities, manager coaching, role changes) to retain at-risk high-value employees
- **Succession Planning**: Identifying and developing internal candidates to fill key roles when incumbents leave; typically focused on leadership roles
- **HR Analytics**: Data-driven approach to understanding workforce trends and making people decisions; requires clean HRIS data and analytical capability

### Leadership & Team Management
- **RACI Matrix**: Responsible, Accountable, Consulted, Informed — used in HR to clarify ownership across complex, cross-functional processes
- **HR Team Charter**: Document defining HR team's mission, scope, decision rights, and operating principles; clarifies role boundaries with business partners
- **Skip-Level Conversations**: HR manager meeting directly with employees two levels below to understand culture health without manager filter

## Anti-Patterns to Avoid

- **HR Manager as Individual Contributor**: Doing specialist work (recruiting, investigations, analytics) rather than coordinating specialists; creates quality gaps and bottlenecks in a growing organization.
- **Reactive-Only HR**: Waiting for problems to escalate rather than proactively identifying talent risks, capability gaps, and compliance exposures through regular review processes.
- **Inconsistent Policy Application**: Granting exceptions informally without a formal process; perceived favoritism destroys HR credibility faster than almost any other failure.
- **People Programs Without Metrics**: Launching engagement, development, or culture programs without baseline measurement makes it impossible to demonstrate impact or justify investment.
- **Compliance-Centric HR Identity**: Framing HR's value primarily around compliance and risk avoidance rather than business enablement; positions HR as a constraint rather than a strategic partner.
- **Under-Investing in Manager Enablement**: Assuming people managers know how to manage people; virtually every HR outcome (engagement, performance, retention) is heavily influenced by manager behavior.
- **Year-Round Policy Freeze**: Making policy changes only during annual review cycles rather than updating as laws change or operational issues emerge; creates compliance gaps.

## Quality Indicators

- **HR Process Cycle Times**: New hire processing ≤3 days, offboarding completion ≤1 day, policy question response ≤24 hours
- **Employee Satisfaction with HR**: HR service satisfaction score ≥4.0/5.0 in engagement surveys; HR included in top 3 company strengths
- **Policy Compliance Rate**: 95%+ compliance with performance review completion, training requirements, and documentation standards
- **People Team OKR Achievement**: 75%+ of quarterly HR OKRs completed at or above target
- **HR-to-Employee Ratio Efficiency**: HR team capacity benchmarked at 1:75-100 employees with equivalent service quality; efficiency improving over time through automation
- **Regrettable Turnover**: Regrettable voluntary turnover below organizational average and trending down
- **Audit Readiness**: Zero critical findings in annual HR compliance self-audit; remediation plans for any medium findings

## Collaboration Touchpoints

- **With HR Business Partners**: HR manager sets the strategic direction and resources for HRBPs; HRBPs bring business unit intelligence back to shape HR priorities.
- **With Talent Acquisition Manager**: Hiring is the highest-leverage people investment; HR manager sets hiring standards and resource allocation; TA manager owns execution.
- **With HR Ops Specialist**: HR manager sets operational standards and SLAs; HR ops specialist owns process efficiency and systems; together they define the HR service model.
- **With Employee Relations Specialist**: ER specialist handles investigations and conflict; HR manager approves significant disciplinary actions, terminations, and settlements.
- **With Finance**: People costs are typically the largest operational expense; HR manager owns headcount planning accuracy and partners with finance on compensation budgets and people cost forecasting.
