# Best Practices: Competitive Intelligence Analyst

> Design principles, patterns, and frameworks that guide high-quality competitive intelligence, market monitoring, and strategic positioning work.

## Design Principles

- **Signal Over Noise**: Prioritize actionable intelligence over comprehensive coverage — a concise briefing with 3 key insights beats a 40-page report nobody reads
- **Timeliness Trumps Perfection**: A battlecard updated within 48 hours of a competitor announcement is more valuable than a perfectly researched one delivered in two weeks
- **Source Triangulation**: Never rely on a single source; corroborate findings across at least 3 independent sources before drawing conclusions
- **Hypothesis-Driven Analysis**: Start with a hypothesis about competitor intent, then gather evidence to confirm or refute — avoids boiling the ocean
- **Audience-Specific Deliverables**: Sales battlecards, executive briefings, and product team competitive maps each need different formats and depths
- **Living Intelligence**: Competitive landscapes change quarterly; treat CI as a continuous process, not a one-time report
- **Objective Framing**: Separate observed facts from inferred intent; clearly label what is known vs. what is assessed

## Key Patterns & Frameworks

- **Porter's Five Forces**: Analyze competitive intensity via supplier power, buyer power, threat of substitutes, threat of new entrants, and rivalry — use to frame where competitive pressure is highest
- **SWOT Analysis**: Structured strengths/weaknesses/opportunities/threats assessment per competitor; most useful when built from primary evidence, not assumptions
- **Competitive Positioning Map**: Two-axis chart plotting competitors on dimensions that matter to buyers (e.g., price vs. capability) — reveals white space and crowded segments
- **Win/Loss Analysis**: Systematic post-deal debriefs with sales reps and (when possible) prospects/customers to identify competitive win/loss patterns
- **Battlecard Framework**: One-page competitor snapshot structured as: their pitch → our counter-pitch → proof points → landmines → questions to ask — designed for sales use in the field
- **Competitive Moat Taxonomy**: Classify competitor advantages as network effects, switching costs, scale economies, brand, regulatory, or IP — each requires a different counter-strategy
- **Jobs-to-be-Done Competitive Lens**: Analyze competitors through the lens of what customer job they're solving, not just feature comparison — reveals non-obvious substitutes
- **Share of Voice Monitoring**: Track brand mention frequency and sentiment across review sites, social, and press — leading indicator of competitor momentum
- **Technology Stack Fingerprinting**: Identify competitor tech choices (job postings, open-source contributions, infrastructure clues) to infer product roadmap direction
- **Analyst Relations Mapping**: Track which industry analysts cover your space, their coverage of competitors, and their recent positioning to anticipate analyst influence on deals

## Domain Concepts & Terminology

### Competitive Analysis Frameworks
- **Competitive Moat**: A durable structural advantage that makes it difficult for competitors to erode market position
- **Differentiation**: The attributes that make a product/service meaningfully distinct to buyers in ways they value
- **Feature Parity**: The state where a product matches a competitor on a specific capability — parity removes a disqualifier but rarely wins deals
- **Positioning**: How a company wants buyers to perceive it relative to alternatives; effective positioning is about trade-offs, not superlatives
- **Category Creation**: A strategic move to define a new market category where the company is automatically the leader
- **Flanking Strategy**: Attacking a competitor's weakness or an underserved segment rather than engaging head-to-head
- **Competitive Displacement**: A targeted effort to replace an incumbent competitor in specific accounts

### Market Monitoring
- **Share of Voice (SOV)**: Percentage of total brand mentions in a market that belong to a given company
- **Sentiment Analysis**: Quantifying tone (positive/negative/neutral) of market commentary about a competitor
- **Job Posting Signals**: Patterns in competitor job postings that reveal investment areas (e.g., hiring 10 ML engineers signals AI product bet)
- **Funding Signals**: Competitor funding rounds, valuations, and investor base as indicators of runway and strategic ambition
- **G2 / Capterra / Review Site Patterns**: Aggregate customer reviews as real-time qualitative intelligence on competitor strengths and complaints
- **SEO Keyword Overlap**: Shared keywords and content gaps reveal how competitors are positioning and what buyers are searching for

### Win/Loss Analysis
- **Win Rate**: Percentage of competitive opportunities where the company wins; should be tracked per competitor, not in aggregate
- **Loss Reason Taxonomy**: Structured classification of loss reasons (price, feature gap, relationship, timing, incumbent) to identify systemic patterns
- **Deal Influence**: The degree to which competitive intelligence directly shaped a won or saved deal
- **Competitive Displacement Rate**: Percentage of new wins that came from displacing a named competitor

### Intelligence Products
- **Battlecard**: A concise, sales-ready reference document summarizing how to win against a specific competitor
- **Competitive Briefing**: An executive-level synthesis of the competitive landscape with strategic implications
- **Competitive Landscape Map**: Visual overview of all players in a space, segmented by approach, size, or focus area
- **Competitor Profile**: A detailed dossier on a single competitor covering product, go-to-market, financials, culture, and trajectory

## Anti-Patterns to Avoid

- **Feature-List Comparison Tables**: Side-by-side feature checklists give a false sense of objectivity and miss the nuance of how well each product executes — supplement with qualitative assessment and customer evidence
- **Stale Battlecards**: Distributing battlecards that haven't been updated after a major competitor product launch — outdated CI is worse than no CI because it creates false confidence
- **Competitor Obsession Over Customer Focus**: Spending more time tracking what competitors are doing than understanding why customers buy — competitive strategy should serve customer insight, not replace it
- **Single-Source Intelligence**: Drawing conclusions from one analyst report or press release without triangulation — sources have biases; corroborate before presenting findings
- **Vanity Metric Competitive Tracking**: Tracking metrics like LinkedIn follower growth or press mention count that don't correlate with competitive threat — focus on deal win/loss signals and product trajectory
- **Internal Echo Chamber**: Relying solely on sales rep impressions for CI — reps have selection bias toward deals they remember; balance with direct buyer interviews and review site data
- **Treating CI as a One-Time Project**: Building a competitive landscape report once a year and calling it done — competitive intelligence has a shelf life measured in weeks, not months

## Quality Indicators

- **Battlecard Freshness**: Average days since last update for all active competitor battlecards — target under 30 days for tier-1 competitors
- **Sales Adoption Rate**: Percentage of sales reps who accessed a battlecard in the last 30 days — low adoption signals format or distribution problems
- **Win Rate Impact**: Change in win rate vs. a competitor before/after CI program intervention — the ultimate measure of CI value
- **Intelligence Timeliness**: Average hours between a competitor announcement and a CI team briefing to sales — target under 48 hours for major moves
- **Source Diversity Score**: Number of distinct source types (primary, secondary, review sites, analyst, job postings) used per competitor profile — single-source profiles are fragile
- **Deal Influence Attribution**: Number of deals per quarter where CI is cited in the win/save debrief — tracks business impact of CI program

## Collaboration Touchpoints

- **With Sales Enablement**: Battlecards are only valuable if they reach reps before a competitive conversation — work on delivery format (CRM integration, Slack alerts) as much as content quality
- **With Product Marketing**: Competitive positioning and messaging must be consistent; CI findings should feed directly into positioning updates and GTM materials
- **With Market Research Analyst**: Primary customer research often surfaces competitive insight that secondary monitoring misses — share findings bidirectionally to build a fuller picture
- **With Product Management**: Win/loss patterns and competitor roadmap signals should translate into product prioritization inputs — formalize a quarterly CI → product feedback loop
