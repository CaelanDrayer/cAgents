# Best Practices: Organizational Development Specialist

> Design principles, patterns, and frameworks that guide high-quality organizational design, change management, and team effectiveness work.

## Design Principles

- **Structure Follows Strategy**: Organizational design is not an end in itself — the right structure enables the company's strategy; restructure when strategy changes, not because of executive preferences or political dynamics.
- **Design for Decisions, Not for Activities**: The purpose of an org structure is to enable fast, high-quality decisions at the right level; assess every design option against decision velocity and accountability clarity.
- **Change Begins Before the Announcement**: Effective change management starts months before the announcement — stakeholder alignment, pilot testing, and leader preparation determine adoption, not the launch communication.
- **Resistance is Information**: Employee resistance to change is rarely irrational; it contains important information about implementation risks, communication gaps, and change fatigue that must be diagnosed and addressed.
- **Team Effectiveness Requires Both Structure and Dynamics**: Redesigning roles and reporting relationships without addressing team dynamics (trust, communication patterns, decision processes) rarely improves performance.
- **Evidence-Based OD**: Organizational interventions should be grounded in organizational diagnosis (data, observations, validated frameworks) rather than consultant fads or executive intuition.
- **Development is Contextual**: Leader development programs that don't account for the specific organizational context and challenges of the leaders being developed have low transfer rates.

## Key Patterns & Frameworks

- **McKinsey 7S Framework**: Seven interdependent organizational elements — Strategy, Structure, Systems, Shared Values, Style (leadership), Staff, and Skills; change in one element requires adjustments in others.
- **ADKAR Change Management Model**: Prosci model — Awareness (of need to change), Desire (to participate), Knowledge (of how to change), Ability (to implement), Reinforcement (to sustain); each barrier requires a different intervention.
- **Kotter's 8-Step Change Model**: Create urgency → Build coalition → Form vision → Communicate vision → Remove obstacles → Create short-term wins → Build on wins → Anchor change; longer-horizon transformation model.
- **Bridges Transition Model**: Distinguishes change (external event) from transition (internal psychological process); Endings → Neutral Zone → New Beginnings; people move through transition at different speeds.
- **Org Design Trade-off Matrix**: Evaluate org design options against dimensions of customer centricity, decision speed, talent development, cost efficiency, and strategic alignment.
- **Lencioni Team Effectiveness Model**: Five Dysfunctions — Absence of Trust → Fear of Conflict → Lack of Commitment → Avoidance of Accountability → Inattention to Results; team diagnosis framework.
- **High-Performance Team Assessment**: Structured diagnostic covering team purpose clarity, role clarity, decision processes, communication quality, conflict resolution, and psychological safety.
- **Succession Planning Depth Chart**: Ready-now, 1-2 year, and 3-5 year successors for critical roles; identifies development investment priorities and bench strength gaps.

## Domain Concepts & Terminology

### Organizational Design
- **Organizational Structure**: The formal system of roles, responsibilities, reporting relationships, and decision rights that shapes how work gets done
- **Functional Structure**: Organized by function (Engineering, Marketing, Finance, HR); enables specialization but creates silos between functions
- **Divisional Structure**: Organized by business unit, product line, or geography; enables customer focus but duplicates functions
- **Matrix Structure**: Dual reporting relationships (functional + project or product); enables resource sharing and cross-functional collaboration at the cost of clarity
- **Network Structure**: Flexible arrangements of specialized teams, project-based organization; high adaptability but requires strong coordination mechanisms
- **Span of Control**: Number of direct reports per manager; too wide (>12) reduces management quality; too narrow (<4) creates bureaucracy
- **Delayering**: Removing management layers to speed decisions, reduce cost, and increase accountability; common in post-acquisition integration and efficiency transformations
- **Role Clarity**: Degree to which employees understand their responsibilities, decision rights, and success criteria; foundational to organizational health

### Change Management
- **Change Saturation**: Organizational state where the number or pace of changes exceeds the organization's capacity to absorb them; leads to change fatigue and passive resistance
- **Change Readiness**: An organization's or individual's capacity and willingness to absorb and implement a specific change
- **Sponsorship Spine**: Chain of active, aligned sponsors from senior leadership through middle management required for successful change implementation
- **Change Coalition**: Influential group of formal and informal leaders who champion a change across the organization
- **Resistance to Change**: Any behavior that delays or prevents adoption of a change; ranges from passive non-compliance to active opposition; must be diagnosed before treated
- **Adoption Curve**: Distribution of how quickly different employee segments adopt change; early adopters can be leveraged as champions; late adopters require additional support

### Team Effectiveness
- **Psychological Safety**: Amy Edmondson's concept — the belief that the team is safe for interpersonal risk-taking (speaking up, challenging ideas, admitting mistakes); prerequisite for high performance
- **Team Charter**: Document defining team purpose, scope, norms, decision rights, and success metrics; most valuable when created collaboratively by the team
- **Working Styles Assessment**: Individual preference assessments (MBTI, DiSC, StrengthsFinder) used for team development; valuable for creating shared language, dangerous when used for role assignment
- **Team Retrospective**: Structured reflection on recent work to identify what's working, what isn't, and what to change; regular retrospectives sustain team learning

### Leadership Development
- **70-20-10 Development Model**: 70% through experience (stretch assignments, new roles), 20% through relationships (coaching, mentoring, feedback), 10% through formal learning
- **Leadership Pipeline**: Framework defining the skill shifts required at each management transition (individual contributor → manager → manager of managers → executive)
- **Succession Readiness**: Assessment of how prepared identified successors are to step into a role; typically rated as ready now, ready in 1-2 years, ready in 3-5 years

## Anti-Patterns to Avoid

- **Structure-as-Conflict-Resolution**: Reorganizing teams to solve interpersonal or political conflicts; structural changes don't resolve human dynamics, they just relocate them.
- **Change Without Diagnosis**: Implementing org changes based on benchmarks or best practices from other companies without diagnosing the specific root causes of current underperformance.
- **Communication-Only Change Management**: Treating change management as a communications plan; communications create awareness but don't build desire, knowledge, or ability.
- **Neglecting the Neutral Zone**: Rushing through the transition period between old and new state without acknowledging the disorientation and loss that employees experience; accelerating past transition extends it.
- **Team Off-Site Without Follow-Through**: Running team effectiveness workshops that produce insights and commitments that are never implemented; team interventions require 30/60/90 day follow-through plans.
- **Design Without Input**: Designing org structures without input from people doing the work; creates designs that are theoretically elegant but operationally dysfunctional.
- **Perpetual Reorganization**: Restructuring every 12-18 months without allowing time for changes to take effect; continuous reorganization creates change fatigue and prevents capability building.

## Quality Indicators

- **Role Clarity Score**: 80%+ favorable on "I understand my responsibilities and decision rights" in engagement surveys; improvement trend post-redesign
- **Change Adoption Rate**: 75%+ of employees demonstrating target behaviors within 90 days of change implementation; measured by manager observation and behavioral indicators
- **Team Engagement Scores**: Teams that receive OD interventions improve engagement scores by ≥5 points within two quarters
- **Internal Promotion Rate**: % of roles filled internally (vs. external hiring) trending upward; indicates succession planning and development program effectiveness
- **Leadership Effectiveness Scores**: Manager effectiveness survey items improving year-over-year; 75%+ favorable across all manager categories
- **Time to Productivity Post-Reorg**: New org model operating at expected performance levels within 90 days; measure against pre-reorg baseline
- **Span of Control Health**: 85%+ of managers with span of control between 5-10; outliers have documented justification (new team build, transition period)

## Collaboration Touchpoints

- **With HR Business Partner**: OD interventions are deployed through HRBP relationships with business unit leaders; HRBP owns the leader relationship, OD specialist owns the methodology and intervention design.
- **With Learning Specialist**: Leadership development programs are co-designed; OD specialist defines capability requirements and organizational context, learning specialist designs instructional approach.
- **With Culture and Engagement Manager**: Culture change and organizational health are intersecting mandates; co-design culture transformation initiatives that address both structural (OD) and experiential (C&E) dimensions.
- **With HR Manager**: Significant org redesigns require HR manager approval and resource allocation; HR manager provides executive sponsorship context and stakeholder alignment.
- **With Change Management Specialist (cross-domain)**: For enterprise-wide transformations, partner with change management specialist on methodology, tools, and stakeholder management for the change program.
