# Best Practices: Recruiter

> Design principles, patterns, and frameworks that guide high-quality full-cycle recruiting, candidate sourcing, and hiring execution work.

## Design Principles

- **Candidate Experience is Employer Brand**: Every candidate interaction — response time, communication quality, interview experience, feedback delivery — shapes employer brand perception; treat all candidates as future customers or referral sources.
- **Source Diversity Produces Slate Diversity**: Relying on LinkedIn and referrals alone produces homogeneous candidate pools; intentional multi-channel sourcing is required to build diverse, qualified slates.
- **Intake Quality Determines Search Quality**: Spending 60 minutes on a thorough intake meeting with the hiring manager prevents weeks of misaligned recruiting; understand the role deeply before sourcing.
- **Pipeline Management is Time Management**: Recruiting without systematic pipeline tracking produces missed follow-ups, dropped candidates, and lost offers; discipline in pipeline hygiene separates good recruiters from great ones.
- **Assess What Matters, Not What's Easy to Assess**: GPA, school prestige, and years of experience are easy to measure but poorly predict job performance; structured behavioral interviews against defined competencies predict far better.
- **Offer Negotiation is Mutual Value Creation**: The goal of offer negotiation isn't to minimize compensation — it's to create a package that the candidate accepts and feels fairly treated by; underpaying to win a negotiation creates early attrition.
- **Data Closes the Feedback Loop**: Recruiters who don't track time-to-fill, source effectiveness, and offer acceptance rates can't improve; measure everything that affects outcome quality.

## Key Patterns & Frameworks

- **Full-Cycle Recruiting Workflow**: Requisition intake → Role calibration → Multi-channel sourcing → Systematic screening → Structured interviewing → Debrief facilitation → Offer construction → Negotiation → Close → Handoff to onboarding.
- **Boolean Search Framework**: Advanced search string construction using AND, OR, NOT, parentheses, and site: operators to find qualified candidates on LinkedIn, GitHub, and open databases. Example: ("Product Manager" OR "PM") AND (SaaS OR B2B) AND "Series B" NOT intern.
- **Competency-Based Interview Design**: Identify 4-6 job-relevant competencies → Write behavioral interview questions (one per competency) → Define STAR-scored answer anchors (1-4 scale) → Train interviewers on scoring. Reduces bias and improves consistency.
- **Sourcing Channel Mix Strategy**: Active (LinkedIn Recruiter, Boolean search, sourcing tools) + Passive (job boards, careers page) + Relationship (referrals, events, alumni networks) + Diverse-specific channels (HBCU partnerships, coding bootcamps, veteran networks).
- **Candidate Pipeline Metrics Model**: Track candidates at each stage — sourced → screened → submitted → interviewed → offered → accepted; conversion rates at each stage reveal where pipeline breaks down.
- **Debrief Facilitation Protocol**: Structured post-interview debrief where interviewers vote before discussing (prevents anchoring), facilitator ensures each competency is addressed, notes captured in ATS. Prevents HIPPO (highest-paid person's opinion) bias.
- **Offer Construction Checklist**: Base salary (within approved range) + bonus target + equity grant + start date + title + benefits summary + relocation if applicable; present verbally before written to allow discussion.
- **Rejection Communication Standards**: All rejected candidates receive a response within 5 business days of decision; candidates who completed phone screens receive personalized rejection; rejected final-round candidates receive a call.

## Domain Concepts & Terminology

### Sourcing & Outreach
- **Boolean Search**: Logical operator-based search method for finding candidates using AND, OR, NOT on LinkedIn, GitHub, and Google X-ray; essential for passive candidate sourcing
- **LinkedIn Recruiter**: LinkedIn's paid sourcing tool providing InMail credits, candidate tracking, and advanced search filters
- **Passive Candidate**: Professional not actively job searching but open to opportunities; typically higher quality and harder to reach than active candidates
- **Active Candidate**: Professional actively applying to roles; higher volume, faster to engage, typically lower average quality than passive candidates
- **X-Ray Search**: Google search technique using site: operator to search a specific domain (e.g., site:linkedin.com/in "software engineer") to find publicly indexed profiles
- **Talent Mapping**: Proactive identification of target candidates before a requisition opens; builds pipeline before urgency creates pressure

### Pipeline Management
- **ATS (Applicant Tracking System)**: Software managing job postings, applications, candidate records, and workflow (Greenhouse, Lever, Ashby, Workday Recruiting, iCIMS)
- **Requisition (Req)**: Approved, open job with defined role, level, budget, and hiring manager; recruiting can't officially start without an approved req
- **Candidate Pipeline**: All active candidates in various stages of the recruiting process for an open role
- **Pipeline Hygiene**: Discipline of keeping ATS records current — updating candidate stages, dispositions, and notes in real-time; poor hygiene creates data quality and compliance issues
- **Disposition**: Formal status of a candidate in a process — advance vs. reject; required in ATS for EEOC compliance and reporting

### Interviewing
- **STAR Method**: Behavioral interview response framework — Situation, Task, Action, Result; tells the interviewer what the candidate actually did, not what they theoretically would do
- **Structured Interview**: Standardized questions applied to all candidates for a role, with consistent scoring criteria; reduces interviewer bias and improves predictive validity
- **Competency-Based Interview**: Interview design where each question tests a specific job-relevant competency; the gold standard for predictive validity
- **Scorecard**: Structured evaluation form with competencies, rating scales, and recommendation field; completed independently by each interviewer before debrief
- **Work Sample / Practical Exercise**: Job simulation where candidate performs a task representative of the actual job; highest predictive validity of any assessment type
- **Panel Interview**: Multiple interviewers present simultaneously; efficient but can intimidate candidates; best used for final rounds

### Offer & Close
- **Offer Letter**: Formal written offer of employment including title, start date, compensation, and conditions; binding when accepted
- **Verbal Offer**: Offer extended by phone before written offer letter; allows negotiation dialogue before formalizing; best practice is verbal before written
- **Counter-Offer**: Competing offer from the candidate's current employer in response to a job offer; candidates who accept counter-offers often leave within 12-18 months anyway
- **Start Date**: Agreed first day of employment; standard 2-week notice in the US; some exec roles require longer
- **Decline Rate**: % of candidates who decline offers after acceptance; tracks candidate experience quality and competitiveness

## Anti-Patterns to Avoid

- **Resume-Matching Screening**: Screening resumes by looking for exact experience matches rather than transferable competencies; eliminates excellent candidates with non-traditional backgrounds.
- **Ghosting Candidates**: Failing to communicate rejection decisions; 55% of candidates report never hearing back after submitting; destroys employer brand and creates Glassdoor risk.
- **Interview by Gut Feel**: Evaluating candidates on likability and cultural fit (usually affinity bias) rather than job-relevant competencies; produces homogeneous teams and poor performance prediction.
- **Over-Relying on Referrals**: Using employee referrals as the primary sourcing channel; produces high conversion rates but limits diversity and can create nepotism perception.
- **Debrief Anchoring**: Letting the first person to speak in debrief anchor the whole conversation; use independent scoring before group discussion to prevent this.
- **Rushing Offers to Beat Competition**: Extending offers before completing the full interview process to beat competing offers; compromises selection quality and creates legal risk if offer is rescinded.
- **ATS Neglect**: Conducting recruiting in email and spreadsheets instead of the ATS; prevents pipeline visibility, reporting, compliance documentation, and team coordination.

## Quality Indicators

- **Time-to-Fill**: Days from requisition opening to accepted offer; benchmark: 30-45 days for professional roles; track by role type and seniority
- **Offer Acceptance Rate**: % of extended offers accepted; benchmark: 85%+; below 75% indicates compensation misalignment or poor candidate experience
- **Pipeline Conversion Rates**: % advancing at each stage (sourced → screened → submitted → interviewed → offered); stage-level breakdowns reveal process efficiency and quality
- **Source Effectiveness**: Offer acceptance rate and 90-day retention by source channel; identifies highest-ROI sourcing investments
- **Hiring Manager Satisfaction**: Post-hire survey score ≥4.0/5.0 on "quality of candidates presented" and "process efficiency"
- **Diverse Slate Rate**: % of roles where final round interview slate includes 50%+ underrepresented candidates
- **90-Day Quality of Hire**: % of new hires rated as "meeting or exceeding expectations" at 90-day check-in; the lagging indicator of all recruiting quality investments

## Collaboration Touchpoints

- **With Recruiting Coordinator**: Logistics (scheduling, communications, ATS data entry) delegated to coordinator; recruiter focuses on relationship, assessment, and close while coordinator handles operational execution.
- **With Talent Acquisition Manager**: Recruiter receives strategic direction (sourcing priorities, offer approval authority, diversity goals) and escalates exceptions (out-of-range offers, hard-to-fill roles) to TA manager.
- **With Onboarding Specialist**: Recruiting-to-onboarding handoff determines onboarding quality; share candidate context, start date accuracy, and role-specific notes to enable personalized onboarding.
- **With Compensation Analyst**: Offer packages require current market data; recruiter relies on comp analyst for range guidance and seeks approval for exceptions.
- **With Hiring Manager**: The recruiter-hiring manager relationship is the most important partnership in recruiting; align on role definition, criteria, process, interview calibration, and decision timeline at intake.
