# Best Practices: HR Compliance Specialist

> Design principles, patterns, and frameworks that guide high-quality HR compliance, policy management, and regulatory risk work.

## Design Principles

- **Prevention Over Remediation**: Compliance programs that prevent violations cost a fraction of what investigations, settlements, and litigation cost; invest in prevention first.
- **Plain Language Policy**: Policies employees can't understand don't change behavior; translate legal requirements into accessible, action-oriented language without losing legal precision.
- **Risk-Stratified Attention**: Not all compliance obligations carry equal risk; prioritize attention based on probability × impact — federal law violations with class action exposure warrant more rigor than local ordinance nuances.
- **Compliance Enables Culture**: Frame compliance not as bureaucratic restriction but as the foundation that makes fair, respectful workplaces possible; leaders who see compliance as enabling culture champion it.
- **Document Everything Defensible**: The standard for employment law documentation is: could this document defend the company's decision in court? Apply that test before filing.
- **Regulatory Change is Constant**: Employment law evolves continuously (state-by-state, NLRB rule changes, EEOC guidance); build a monitoring system, not just a snapshot compliance program.
- **Multi-Jurisdiction Complexity**: Companies with employees in multiple states face layered compliance obligations; never apply the most permissive jurisdiction's standards to all employees.

## Key Patterns & Frameworks

- **Compliance Risk Assessment**: Annual evaluation of all HR compliance obligations by jurisdiction, scoring each on regulatory change velocity, violation probability, and consequence severity to prioritize audit focus.
- **Policy Development Lifecycle**: Trigger (legal change or issue) → Draft → Legal Review → Stakeholder Input → Approval → Communication → Training → Audit → Revision cycle; each stage has defined owners and timelines.
- **Employee Handbook Audit Protocol**: Annual review covering legal compliance, operational accuracy, outdated provisions, and missing required elements (state-specific). Document review and approval dates.
- **Classification Audit Process**: Systematic review of employee classifications — exempt/non-exempt under FLSA, contractor vs. employee — using economic reality and duties tests; document reasoning.
- **I-9 Compliance Program**: New hire form completion within 3 business days of hire, reverification tracking calendar, systematic storage (physical or electronic), and self-audit protocol.
- **EEO-1 Reporting Cycle**: Annual data collection, category mapping, submission workflow, and record retention for EEOC's workforce composition report (Component 1 required; Component 2 pay data when required).
- **Leave Administration Framework**: Documented procedures for FMLA, ADA accommodations, state leaves, and company leave policies covering eligibility determination, notice requirements, documentation, and return-to-work protocols.
- **Compliance Training Calendar**: Annual schedule of mandatory trainings (harassment prevention, data privacy, workplace safety) with completion tracking, manager completion requirements, and documentation.

## Domain Concepts & Terminology

### Federal Employment Law
- **Title VII of the Civil Rights Act**: Prohibits discrimination based on race, color, religion, sex, and national origin; covers employers with 15+ employees
- **ADA (Americans with Disabilities Act)**: Prohibits disability discrimination and requires reasonable accommodation; interactive process required for accommodation requests
- **ADEA (Age Discrimination in Employment Act)**: Protects employees 40+ from age-based discrimination; OWBPA adds specific requirements for severance agreement releases
- **FMLA (Family and Medical Leave Act)**: 12 weeks unpaid, job-protected leave for qualifying reasons; applies to employers with 50+ employees within 75 miles
- **FLSA (Fair Labor Standards Act)**: Governs minimum wage, overtime pay, child labor, and employee classification (exempt vs. non-exempt)
- **NLRA (National Labor Relations Act)**: Protects employees' rights to organize, bargain collectively, and engage in concerted activity; applies even without a union
- **OSHA (Occupational Safety and Health Act)**: Requires employers to provide safe workplaces; General Duty Clause covers all recognized hazards

### Classification & Pay
- **Exempt vs. Non-Exempt**: FLSA classification determining overtime eligibility; exempt employees must meet salary threshold ($684/week federal, higher in some states) and duties tests
- **Salary Basis Test**: Exempt employees must receive the same weekly salary regardless of hours worked; improper deductions destroy exemption
- **Duties Tests**: Job content requirements for executive, administrative, professional, computer, and outside sales exemptions
- **Independent Contractor (1099) vs. Employee (W-2)**: Determined by economic reality and behavioral control tests; misclassification creates substantial tax, benefit, and wage-hour liability
- **Joint Employer Doctrine**: Two companies may both be considered employers of the same workers; creates shared compliance obligations

### Compliance Programs
- **Affirmative Action Plan (AAP)**: Written program required of federal contractors (50+ employees, $50K+ contracts) analyzing workforce representation and setting placement goals
- **EEO-1 Report**: Annual demographic workforce report submitted to EEOC; required for employers with 100+ employees
- **COBRA Compliance**: Election notice timelines (14-day qualifying event notice, 60-day election window), premium administration, and record retention
- **ERISA Compliance**: Plan document and Summary Plan Description (SPD) requirements, fiduciary duties, prohibited transactions, and Form 5500 filing
- **Background Check Compliance**: FCRA requirements (disclosure, authorization, adverse action process), EEOC guidance on criminal history use, and ban-the-box laws by jurisdiction

### Documentation & Records
- **Record Retention Schedule**: Legal minimum periods for HR records — I-9s (3 years post-hire or 1 year post-termination), payroll records (3 years), FMLA records (3 years), personnel files (varies by state)
- **Litigation Hold**: Legal obligation to preserve records when litigation is reasonably anticipated; HR must coordinate with legal to freeze normal destruction schedules

## Anti-Patterns to Avoid

- **Compliance by Policy Alone**: Writing policies without training, auditing, or consequence management; paper compliance with no operational reality creates false security and legal exposure.
- **One-Policy-Fits-All States**: Applying California (or most permissive state) policies as the baseline for all states is inefficient; applying the most permissive standard where stricter law applies creates violations.
- **Ignoring NLRA for Non-Union Workforces**: Most HR professionals forget NLRA protects concerted activity in all workplaces (not just unionized); social media policies, confidentiality policies, and pay secrecy policies commonly violate NLRA.
- **Informal Accommodation Process**: Handling ADA accommodation requests without engaging the required interactive process documentation creates both legal exposure and inconsistent employee outcomes.
- **Annual Policy Review Theater**: Reviewing policies annually without actually analyzing regulatory changes or testing operational accuracy; produces a "reviewed" date with no real compliance improvement.
- **Over-Broad Confidentiality Policies**: Policies instructing employees not to discuss wages, terms of employment, or working conditions typically violate NLRA Section 7; common and frequently overlooked.
- **Late Adverse Action Process**: Skipping or compressing FCRA adverse action pre-adverse and adverse action notice timelines (5+ business days apart) during background check failures creates per-violation FCRA liability.

## Quality Indicators

- **I-9 Error Rate**: <2% error rate on I-9 forms in self-audits; 100% completion within 3 business days of hire
- **FMLA Documentation Completeness**: 100% of FMLA leaves have complete designation notices, medical certifications, and return-to-work documentation
- **Compliance Training Completion**: 90%+ completion of mandatory trainings (harassment, safety) within required windows; 100% for managers
- **Policy Currency**: All employee handbook policies reviewed and updated within 12 months; legal review documented for any policy governing legally sensitive areas
- **Classification Audit Coverage**: 100% of contractor relationships reviewed against classification criteria annually; zero misclassification findings in payroll audits
- **EEO-1 Filing Timeliness**: Submitted before EEOC deadline with <1% data entry errors
- **Audit Readiness Score**: Mock audit or self-assessment covering top 10 compliance risk areas completed annually with documented remediation plans

## Collaboration Touchpoints

- **With Employee Relations Specialist**: Investigations involving protected class complaints, harassment, or retaliation require compliance specialist guidance on EEOC obligations and documentation standards.
- **With HR Ops Specialist**: Compliance program operationalization (I-9 workflows, record retention, COBRA notices) runs through HR ops systems; compliance specialist defines requirements, HR ops implements.
- **With Employment Attorney**: Legal counsel interprets emerging law and provides privilege-protected guidance on sensitive compliance questions; specialist handles operational implementation.
- **With HR Manager**: Compliance policy updates and training requirements need HR manager prioritization and budget allocation; compliance specialist owns the risk identification and solution design.
- **With Benefits Administrator**: ACA compliance, COBRA, ERISA, and HIPAA obligations span both benefits and compliance functions; joint ownership of compliance calendar and audit readiness.
