# Best Practices: Employment Attorney

> Design principles, patterns, and frameworks that guide high-quality employment law advising and workplace legal matter management.

## Design Principles

- **Prevention Over Litigation**: Investment in compliant policies, manager training, and consistent documentation reduces employment disputes far more cost-effectively than litigation defense
- **Jurisdiction-Specific Analysis**: Employment law varies dramatically by state, country, and locality; always identify the governing jurisdiction before advising, as federal minimums are often superseded
- **Document Everything**: The employment relationship is documented through offer letters, performance reviews, warnings, and termination letters; gaps in documentation undermine defense of employment decisions
- **Investigation Integrity**: Workplace investigations must be conducted promptly, thoroughly, and impartially; an investigation that appears predetermined or biased often creates more liability than the underlying conduct
- **Consistent Policy Application**: The most common employment law violation isn't a bad policy — it's applying a good policy inconsistently; disparate treatment is discrimination
- **Privilege Discipline**: Attorney-client privilege protects legal advice communications; be intentional about when you are and are not providing legal advice, and document accordingly
- **Employee Experience Lens**: Employment law compliance isn't only about avoiding liability; compliant workplaces are also better-functioning workplaces with lower turnover

## Key Patterns & Frameworks

- **Policy Review Checklist**: Systematic review of employee handbook and policies against applicable federal, state, and local law requirements; updated annually and whenever regulations change
- **Employment Decision Documentation Protocol**: All significant employment decisions (termination, demotion, corrective action, promotion denials) require contemporaneous written documentation of the business reason
- **Workplace Investigation Framework**: Complaint intake → Scope determination → Investigator assignment (neutral and independent) → Evidence collection → Interviews → Findings memo → Remediation → Closure → Follow-up
- **Termination Risk Assessment**: Analyze every termination against seven risk factors: adverse impact on protected class, timing relative to protected activity, consistency with policy, quality of documentation, employee tenure and role, jurisdiction, and prior accommodation requests
- **Wage and Hour Compliance Audit**: Systematic review of FLSA/state law compliance across exempt/non-exempt classification, overtime calculation, meal and rest breaks, off-the-clock work, and pay stub requirements
- **Leave of Absence Matrix**: Map every applicable leave type (FMLA, ADA, state family leave, military leave, workers' comp) with eligibility, duration, and interaction requirements; overlapping leave entitlements create complex obligations
- **Non-Compete Enforceability Analysis**: Assess enforceability against jurisdiction-specific standards (reasonable scope, duration, geography, and protectable interest); several states (CA, MN, ND) effectively prohibit them
- **EEOC Charge Response Protocol**: Initial assessment of charge allegations → preservation hold → supervisor interviews → document collection → position statement drafting → settlement evaluation

## Domain Concepts & Terminology

### Discrimination & Harassment
- **Protected Class**: Group protected from employment discrimination under federal or state law (race, sex, age, disability, religion, national origin, and others)
- **Disparate Treatment**: Intentional different treatment of similarly situated employees based on protected class
- **Disparate Impact**: Facially neutral policy that disproportionately affects a protected class without business justification
- **Hostile Work Environment**: Pervasive or severe harassment based on a protected characteristic that creates an abusive work environment
- **Quid Pro Quo Harassment**: Conditioning employment benefits on submission to sexual or other harassment
- **Reasonable Accommodation**: Modification to enable an employee with a disability or religious requirement to perform essential job functions

### Wage & Hour
- **FLSA (Fair Labor Standards Act)**: Federal law governing minimum wage, overtime, recordkeeping, and youth employment
- **Exempt Employee**: Employee not entitled to overtime under FLSA; must meet salary threshold and duties test
- **Non-Exempt Employee**: Employee entitled to overtime pay at 1.5x regular rate for hours over 40 per week
- **Misclassification**: Treating an employee as exempt or as an independent contractor when they legally are not
- **Off-the-Clock Work**: Compensable work performed outside scheduled hours without pay; common violation

### Termination & Discipline
- **At-Will Employment**: Default US employment doctrine; employer or employee may terminate for any legal reason without notice
- **Progressive Discipline**: Graduated response to performance or conduct issues (verbal warning → written warning → final warning → termination); documents the business reason
- **For-Cause Termination**: Termination based on specific documented misconduct or performance failure
- **Constructive Discharge**: Situation where working conditions are so intolerable that a reasonable employee would feel compelled to resign; treated as an employer-initiated termination
- **Separation Agreement**: Post-termination agreement exchanging a release of claims for consideration (severance); must meet OWBPA requirements for ADEA releases

### Leave & Accommodation
- **FMLA (Family and Medical Leave Act)**: Federal entitlement to 12 weeks of unpaid, job-protected leave for qualifying reasons
- **ADA (Americans with Disabilities Act)**: Prohibits discrimination based on disability; requires reasonable accommodation
- **Interactive Process**: Legally required dialogue between employer and employee to identify effective accommodations
- **COBRA**: Federal law requiring continuation of group health coverage after qualifying events

## Anti-Patterns to Avoid

- **Pretextual Terminations**: Stating a false business reason for a termination to conceal the real (potentially discriminatory) reason; pretext is often revealed in litigation and dramatically increases liability
- **Delayed Investigations**: Failing to investigate workplace complaints promptly; delay implies indifference, allows evidence to disappear, and creates ongoing liability for workplace conditions
- **Inconsistent Discipline**: Disciplining employees differently for the same conduct based on protected characteristics, even unintentionally; creates strong disparate treatment evidence
- **Leave Interference**: Taking adverse action against employees for exercising FMLA or other leave rights, even unintentionally; retaliation and interference claims carry significant exposure
- **Handbook as Dead Letter**: Publishing employee handbooks and then failing to follow their own policies; creates both legal liability and employee morale issues
- **Settlement Without Release**: Paying severance without obtaining a valid release of claims; pays the cost without the protection
- **Privilege Waiver**: Copying business partners, consultants, or non-lawyer employees on privileged legal advice communications; waives privilege and makes advice discoverable

## Quality Indicators

- **Policy Current to Jurisdiction**: All handbook policies reviewed against current applicable federal, state, and local law requirements
- **Investigation Timeliness**: Workplace investigations initiated within 5 business days of complaint receipt
- **Documentation Completeness**: All terminations and significant employment decisions have contemporaneous written documentation of the business reason
- **Classification Audit Coverage**: Exempt and contractor classifications reviewed against current FLSA standards
- **EEOC Charge Response Quality**: Thorough position statements filed before deadlines with supporting documentation
- **Training Completion**: Managers trained on anti-discrimination, investigation procedures, and leave obligations
- **Settlement ROI**: Documented cost-benefit analysis for every settlement decision relative to estimated litigation cost

## Collaboration Touchpoints

- **With General Counsel**: Escalate class actions, agency investigations, and matters with >$100K exposure; GC coordinates outside litigation counsel selection and settlement authority
- **With HR Manager**: Co-develop and review handbook policies; employment attorney provides legal compliance while HR owns policy communication and administration
- **With HR Compliance Specialist**: Align on compliance audit scope for wage/hour, classification, and leave administration; both functions share responsibility for employment law compliance
- **With Litigation Manager**: Coordinate when employment matters move to litigation; employment attorney provides subject matter expertise while litigation manager handles case strategy and outside counsel management
