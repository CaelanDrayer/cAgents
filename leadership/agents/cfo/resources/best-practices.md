# Best Practices: Chief Financial Officer (CFO)

> Design principles, patterns, and frameworks that guide high-quality financial leadership, capital stewardship, and strategic financial decision-making.

## Design Principles

- **Capital Allocation as Strategy**: Every dollar deployed is a strategic decision. Treat the capital allocation process with the same rigor as product or go-to-market strategy.
- **Forecast Accuracy as Trust Currency**: The CFO's credibility with the board and CEO is measured by forecast accuracy. Build processes that deliver <5% variance consistently.
- **Cash is Reality, Profit is Opinion**: Manage to cash flow, not accounting profit. P&L optimization without cash flow discipline destroys companies.
- **Financial Literacy Across the Organization**: Invest in making every department head financially literate. Budget accountability requires financial understanding.
- **Proactive Risk Surfacing**: Identify and communicate financial risks before they become crises. The CFO who surprises the board has failed at the most fundamental duty.
- **Business Partner, Not Scorekeeper**: The best CFOs are strategic advisors who shape decisions, not gatekeepers who report outcomes after the fact.
- **Data Quality as Foundation**: Financial insights are only as good as the underlying data. Invest in systems, controls, and processes that ensure data integrity.

## Key Patterns & Frameworks

- **Zero-Based Budgeting (ZBB)**: Start each budget cycle from zero rather than prior-year actuals to eliminate embedded inefficiencies and force explicit justification of all spend.
- **Driver-Based Forecasting**: Build financial models where results flow from operational drivers (units sold, headcount, conversion rates) rather than top-down revenue assumptions.
- **Rolling Forecast (13-Week Cash)**: Maintain a 13-week cash flow forecast updated weekly to ensure runway visibility and early warning of liquidity issues.
- **Variance Analysis (Budget vs. Actual vs. Prior Year)**: Three-way variance analysis reveals whether performance gaps are caused by planning errors, execution gaps, or structural changes.
- **Unit Economics Framework**: Analyze CAC, LTV, payback period, and gross margin per product/segment/channel to identify which revenue streams compound value.
- **Three-Statement Model**: Income statement, balance sheet, and cash flow statement must be fully integrated so changes in one automatically flow to others — the foundation of credible financial analysis.
- **IRR/NPV Investment Evaluation**: Apply discounted cash flow analysis to all major capital investments to ensure they exceed the cost of capital and rank competing opportunities objectively.
- **Scenario Planning (Base/Bear/Bull)**: Maintain three financial scenarios with identified trigger points to enable fast executive response when conditions shift.
- **SAFE/Convertible Note vs. Priced Round**: For fundraising decisions, evaluate dilution, valuation caps, conversion mechanics, and investor rights before recommending structure.
- **FP&A Cadence (Monthly Business Review)**: Structured monthly financial review with department heads translating financial results into operational insight and forward-looking action.

## Domain Concepts & Terminology

### Financial Statements & Reporting
- **GAAP vs. Non-GAAP**: Generally Accepted Accounting Principles (GAAP) vs. adjusted metrics (ARR, adjusted EBITDA) used for investor communications — must clearly distinguish both
- **Revenue Recognition (ASC 606)**: Accounting standard defining when and how revenue is recorded; particularly complex for SaaS, professional services, and multi-element arrangements
- **EBITDA**: Earnings Before Interest, Taxes, Depreciation and Amortization; common proxy for operating cash generation
- **Gross Margin**: Revenue minus cost of goods sold (COGS); the fundamental measure of product economics before operating expenses
- **Working Capital**: Current assets minus current liabilities; measures short-term liquidity and operational efficiency

### Capital Structure & Fundraising
- **Pre-Money / Post-Money Valuation**: Valuation before (pre) and after (post) investment; determines investor ownership percentage
- **Dilution**: Reduction in ownership percentage from new share issuance; must be modeled across all scenarios including employee option pool
- **Pro Forma Capitalization Table (Cap Table)**: Forward-looking ownership structure showing impact of proposed transactions on all shareholders
- **Term Sheet**: Non-binding document outlining key economic and governance terms of a proposed investment
- **Waterfall Analysis**: Models payout to each class of shares in various exit scenarios (acquisition at various prices, IPO)

### Financial Planning & Analysis
- **ARR (Annual Recurring Revenue)**: Annualized value of subscription contracts; the primary SaaS health metric
- **NRR (Net Revenue Retention)**: Revenue from existing customers year-over-year including expansion, contraction, and churn; values above 120% indicate strong product-market fit
- **Burn Multiple**: Net burn divided by net new ARR; measures how efficiently capital is being converted to revenue growth
- **Rule of 40**: Revenue growth rate + EBITDA margin should exceed 40% for healthy SaaS companies
- **Magic Number**: Net new ARR divided by prior-quarter S&M spend; measures sales efficiency (>0.75 is healthy)

### Risk & Controls
- **Internal Controls**: Policies and procedures ensuring financial reporting accuracy, asset safeguarding, and regulatory compliance
- **SOX Compliance**: Sarbanes-Oxley Act requirements for public companies; establishes CEO/CFO certification requirements for financial statements
- **Audit Committee**: Board committee overseeing financial reporting integrity, external audit, and internal audit function
- **Treasury Management**: Active management of cash, investments, and foreign exchange exposure to optimize returns and minimize risk
- **Covenant Compliance**: Monitoring adherence to financial ratios required by debt agreements to avoid technical default

## Anti-Patterns to Avoid

- **Hockey Stick Forecasting**: Projecting near-term conservatism with steep back-half acceleration without operational rationale destroys credibility. Base forecasts on driver models with validated assumptions.
- **Spreadsheet-Only Finance**: Relying on Excel without an ERP or FP&A system creates version control issues, manual errors, and limits analytical capacity as the company scales.
- **Ignoring Unit Economics in Growth Mode**: Growing revenue while unit economics deteriorate is not growth — it's subsidized customer acquisition. Track CAC payback and LTV/CAC religiously.
- **Over-Optimizing for Tax**: Tax efficiency should never drive business decisions; let strategy drive structure, not the reverse.
- **Quarterly Tunnel Vision**: Optimizing Q-by-Q at the expense of investment in long-term growth (R&D, market development, talent) creates a "harvest" mentality that stunts compounding.
- **Opaque Budget Process**: Annual budgets built in finance silos without department head input create accountability gaps. Co-create budgets with operators.
- **Deferred Fundraising**: Waiting until 3 months of runway to start fundraising creates desperation dynamics and inferior terms. Start 12-18 months before capital is needed.

## Quality Indicators

- **Forecast Accuracy**: Variance between forecast and actuals should be <5% for the current quarter and <10% for the full year.
- **Days Sales Outstanding (DSO)**: Average days to collect on invoices; rising DSO signals customer health or billing process issues.
- **Gross Margin Trend**: Improving gross margin over time signals pricing power, scale economics, or cost discipline; declining margins require immediate investigation.
- **Cash Conversion Cycle**: Time from cash outflow (payroll, COGS) to cash inflow (customer payment); shorter cycles indicate operational efficiency.
- **Budget Cycle Completion Time**: Annual budget finalized and approved within 6 weeks of fiscal year-end signals a mature financial planning process.
- **Audit Clean Opinion**: Unqualified audit opinion with no material weaknesses is the baseline quality indicator for financial reporting integrity.

## Collaboration Touchpoints

- **With CEO**: The CFO translates between financial reality and strategic ambition. Quality looks like: joint ownership of the financial narrative, shared understanding of the capital allocation philosophy, and no financial surprises reaching the CEO.
- **With COO**: Finance-operations partnership drives cost discipline without operational rigidity. Quality looks like: operations teams with P&L ownership, shared unit cost metrics, and joint investment prioritization for operational improvements.
- **With CTO**: Technology investment decisions require financial rigor. Quality looks like: technology investments evaluated with IRR/NPV, cloud costs visible by product/feature, and R&D capitalization policy consistently applied.
- **With Board/Audit Committee**: The CFO's primary board relationship is through the audit committee. Quality looks like: audit committee receiving timely, accurate reporting with proactive disclosure of any control weaknesses or accounting judgments.
