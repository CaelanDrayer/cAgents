# Best Practices: Chief Marketing Officer (CMO)

> Design principles, patterns, and frameworks that guide high-quality marketing strategy, brand leadership, and demand generation.

## Design Principles

- **Revenue Attribution First**: Every marketing investment must connect to revenue. Build attribution models before scaling spend, not after.
- **Brand as Compounding Asset**: Brand equity compounds over time like interest. Short-term performance marketing erodes brand; long-term brand investment reduces CAC permanently.
- **Customer Insight as Strategy Foundation**: Deep understanding of the ideal customer profile — their jobs-to-be-done, pain points, and buying triggers — must precede any messaging or channel decision.
- **Pipeline Quality Over Quantity**: Marketing's job is to generate qualified demand, not just leads. Optimize for MQL-to-SQL conversion and pipeline-to-revenue, not top-of-funnel volume.
- **Consistent Positioning, Adaptive Messaging**: The core positioning (why you, why now) should be stable for 18-24 months. Messaging adapts to audience, channel, and context within that positioning.
- **Test and Learn Systematically**: Marketing effectiveness is empirical, not intuitive. Build hypothesis-driven experiments with control groups and statistical significance before scaling.
- **Marketing and Sales as One Revenue Team**: The CMO-CSO/CRO relationship determines revenue predictability. Shared ICP, pipeline definitions, and revenue ownership eliminate friction.

## Key Patterns & Frameworks

- **Jobs-to-Be-Done (JTBD)**: Understand what customers are "hiring" your product to do in their life or work. More predictive of purchase behavior than demographic profiles.
- **Category Design**: Rather than competing within existing categories, define a new category where your product is the reference standard. Creates durable competitive advantage.
- **Demand Waterfall (Sirius Decisions)**: Structured funnel model from Inquiry → MQL → SQL → SAL → SQO → Closed Won with defined criteria and conversion benchmarks at each stage.
- **Ideal Customer Profile (ICP) Matrix**: Segment target customers by firmographic (company size, industry, geography) and technographic (tech stack, maturity) criteria, then score by revenue potential and win rate.
- **Pyramid Principle for Executive Communication**: Lead with the strategic recommendation, support with evidence, close with next steps. Never bury the marketing strategy in slides of tactical metrics.
- **Porter's Five Forces (Marketing Lens)**: Assess competitive intensity, buyer power, supplier power, threat of substitutes, and barriers to entry to identify sustainable positioning.
- **Content Marketing Flywheel**: High-quality content attracts organic traffic → generates leads → converts to customers → customers become advocates → advocates generate referrals → flywheel accelerates.
- **Account-Based Marketing (ABM)**: Coordinate marketing and sales activities against a defined list of target accounts with personalized messaging, content, and outreach. Use for enterprise segments with long sales cycles.
- **Brand Architecture**: Defines how parent brand, sub-brands, and product lines relate to each other. House of Brands (P&G) vs. Branded House (Google) vs. Hybrid — must be decided before portfolio expands.
- **Multi-Touch Attribution**: Assign fractional revenue credit to each marketing touchpoint in the customer journey. Use linear, time-decay, or data-driven models to understand true channel contribution.

## Domain Concepts & Terminology

### Brand & Positioning
- **Brand Identity**: The visual, verbal, and experiential expression of the brand — logo, color palette, typography, tone of voice, and messaging architecture
- **Brand Equity**: The commercial premium customers will pay due to brand recognition and reputation; built over time, destroyed quickly
- **Positioning Statement**: "For [target customer] who [need], [product] is the [category] that [benefit] because [reason to believe]" — internal strategic anchor
- **Value Proposition**: The specific combination of benefits, features, and price that makes the offering compelling relative to alternatives
- **Messaging Hierarchy**: Umbrella message (brand promise) → pillar messages (key benefits) → proof points (specific evidence) — ensures consistent communication across all channels and audiences

### Demand Generation
- **MQL (Marketing Qualified Lead)**: A lead that meets defined engagement and fit criteria, indicating readiness to be passed to sales
- **Pipeline Coverage**: Ratio of pipeline value to revenue target; typically 3-4x coverage is required to achieve targets given conversion rates
- **CAC (Customer Acquisition Cost)**: Total sales and marketing spend divided by new customers acquired; measures efficiency of go-to-market investment
- **LTV/CAC Ratio**: Lifetime value divided by customer acquisition cost; >3x indicates a healthy, scalable go-to-market model
- **Attribution Model**: The method for assigning credit to marketing touchpoints that contributed to a conversion (first touch, last touch, multi-touch)

### Channel Strategy
- **Owned Media**: Channels the company controls — website, email list, app, social profiles
- **Earned Media**: Coverage generated through PR, word-of-mouth, and organic social — credibility at no direct cost but unpredictable
- **Paid Media**: Purchased advertising — digital ads, sponsored content, trade media — controllable and scalable but requires positive unit economics
- **Product-Led Growth (PLG)**: The product itself drives acquisition, activation, and expansion; freemium and free trial models are primary GTM motions
- **Channel Mix Optimization**: Allocating budget across channels based on marginal ROI, not average ROI — the last dollar spent on each channel should generate equal return

### Marketing Operations
- **Marketing Technology Stack (MarTech)**: The integrated set of tools enabling marketing execution — CRM, marketing automation, attribution, content management, analytics
- **Revenue Operations (RevOps)**: Unified operations function aligning sales, marketing, and CS on shared data, processes, and systems to improve revenue predictability
- **Lead Scoring**: Algorithmic ranking of leads by conversion likelihood based on behavioral signals (content engagement, web activity) and fit criteria (company size, title)
- **Conversion Rate Optimization (CRO)**: Systematic testing and improvement of website and landing page conversion rates through A/B testing and user experience improvements

## Anti-Patterns to Avoid

- **Brand Refresh Without Positioning Clarity**: Changing visual identity without resolving positioning confusion is expensive and ineffective. Fix the strategy before the aesthetics.
- **Activity-Based Marketing Reporting**: Reporting impressions, open rates, and social followers to the board without connecting to pipeline and revenue impact signals marketing is a cost center, not a growth driver.
- **Spray-and-Pray Channel Strategy**: Attempting to be present across all channels with insufficient investment in any creates mediocre performance everywhere. Focus investment until channels achieve efficiency, then expand.
- **Sales-Marketing Blame Game**: When pipeline is weak, marketing blames lead quality definitions and sales blames lead volume. Fix with shared ICP definition, joint pipeline review, and shared OKRs.
- **One-Size-Fits-All Messaging**: Using identical messaging across enterprise and SMB, new prospects and existing customers, or different industries destroys relevance. Segment messaging without fragmenting positioning.
- **Chasing Competitors**: Building strategy around matching competitor capabilities leads to commodity positioning. Lead with your unique strengths, not their weaknesses.
- **Ignoring Customer Marketing**: Allocating 100% of marketing budget to new customer acquisition while ignoring expansion and advocacy programs leaves the most efficient revenue source untapped.

## Quality Indicators

- **Pipeline Contribution**: Marketing-sourced pipeline as a percentage of total pipeline; benchmark varies by go-to-market motion (60-70% for PLG/inbound, 30-40% for enterprise/outbound).
- **Marketing Efficiency Ratio**: Revenue generated per dollar of marketing spend; should improve over time as brand equity builds and channels scale.
- **Brand Recall and NPS**: Tracked through quarterly brand studies; leading indicator of pipeline quality and CAC trends 12-18 months forward.
- **Content Conversion Rate**: % of content engagers who convert to MQL; measures content quality and audience relevance, not just volume.
- **Marketing-Sourced Revenue**: Revenue closed from marketing-generated pipeline; the ultimate accountability metric for marketing investment.
- **Team Retention Rate**: Marketing team attrition above 15%/year signals culture, leadership, or compensation issues that will disrupt program continuity.

## Collaboration Touchpoints

- **With CRO/CSO**: The marketing-sales alignment is the single greatest lever for revenue predictability. Quality looks like: shared ICP definition, agreed pipeline stage criteria, joint funnel review cadence, and aligned revenue targets with shared accountability.
- **With CEO**: Marketing strategy must be indistinguishable from business strategy. Quality looks like: the CMO can articulate how every major marketing investment maps to a business objective, and the CEO can explain the marketing strategy to the board.
- **With CPO**: Product marketing and product management must co-own the go-to-market for new features and products. Quality looks like: positioning developed collaboratively before engineering begins, launch plans integrated with product roadmap, and customer feedback loops flowing bidirectionally.
- **With CFO**: Marketing budget credibility requires clear ROI frameworks and attribution models. Quality looks like: every major budget request includes projected pipeline impact, cost-per-acquisition assumptions, and a measurement plan.
