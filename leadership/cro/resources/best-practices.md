# Best Practices: Chief Revenue Officer (CRO)

> Design principles, patterns, and frameworks that guide high-quality revenue strategy, sales-marketing alignment, and go-to-market execution.

## Design Principles

- **Own the Number**: The CRO's primary accountability is the revenue target — not activity, not pipeline, not leading indicators. Everything else is instrumental to hitting the number.
- **Pipeline as Inventory**: Revenue is a manufacturing problem. Build pipeline with the same discipline a manufacturer manages inventory — defined inputs, predictable throughput, measurable yield at each stage.
- **Sales and Marketing as One Revenue Team**: Organizational boundaries between marketing and sales are artificial. Shared ICP, shared pipeline definitions, and shared revenue OKRs create the alignment needed for predictable growth.
- **Customer Success as Revenue Engine**: Expansion revenue from existing customers is cheaper to acquire, faster to close, and more predictable than new logo revenue. Net revenue retention is the health metric that compounds.
- **Data-Driven Go-to-Market**: Revenue decisions backed by conversion data, win/loss analysis, and cohort analysis outperform those backed by intuition and anecdote. Build the data infrastructure before scaling GTM.
- **Predictability Over Peak Performance**: A revenue machine that consistently delivers 95% of target is more valuable than one that delivers 130% one quarter and 70% the next. Build for predictability.
- **Capacity Before Demand**: Hire and develop revenue capacity (salespeople, SDRs, CS managers) ahead of demand, not in response to it. Revenue capacity has a 90-day+ onboarding lag.

## Key Patterns & Frameworks

- **Revenue Waterfall (Demand Waterfall)**: Inquiry → MQL → SQL → SAL → Opportunity → Qualified Opportunity → Closed Won. Define criteria, conversion benchmarks, and ownership at each transition point.
- **ICP (Ideal Customer Profile) Definition**: Firmographic (company size, industry, geography, funding stage) + Technographic (tech stack, integrations) + Behavioral (signals indicating readiness to buy) definition that aligns marketing and sales targeting.
- **Pipeline Coverage Model**: Maintain 3-4x pipeline coverage against revenue targets to account for conversion rates. Model coverage by stage, segment, and rep to identify build requirements early.
- **Forecast Methodology (MEDDICC/MEDDPICC)**: Metrics, Economic Buyer, Decision Criteria, Decision Process, Identify Pain, Champion, Competition (+ Paper Process for MEDDPICC). Qualification framework for enterprise deals that improves forecast accuracy.
- **Account-Based Revenue (ABR)**: Coordinate marketing, SDR, AE, and CS activities against a defined list of strategic accounts with personalized outreach and expansion plans.
- **Customer Health Score**: Composite metric tracking product usage, support ticket frequency, NPS, engagement, and contract utilization to predict retention and expansion probability.
- **Quota Design Framework**: Territory-based quotas with market potential weighting; ramp schedules for new hires; variable structures that motivate volume, deal size, and retention goals simultaneously.
- **Win/Loss Analysis**: Systematic post-decision interview process with both won and lost customers to identify competitive positioning gaps, sales process issues, and ICP accuracy.
- **Revenue Operations (RevOps) Model**: Unified operations function (CRM admin, reporting, territory/quota, enablement) serving all revenue functions under single leadership. Eliminates data fragmentation and process inconsistency.
- **Churn Prediction Model**: Statistical model using usage, engagement, and relationship signals to identify at-risk accounts 60-90 days before renewal, enabling proactive intervention.

## Domain Concepts & Terminology

### Revenue Metrics
- **ARR (Annual Recurring Revenue)**: Annualized value of active subscription contracts; the primary health metric for subscription businesses
- **NRR (Net Revenue Retention)**: (Starting ARR + Expansion - Contraction - Churn) / Starting ARR; values above 120% indicate customers are growing and the product delivers increasing value
- **GRR (Gross Revenue Retention)**: Starting ARR minus churn and contraction, divided by starting ARR; measures pure retention without expansion; floor is 0%, ceiling is 100%
- **CAC (Customer Acquisition Cost)**: Total sales and marketing spend divided by new customers acquired in the same period; must be measured by segment and channel
- **LTV (Customer Lifetime Value)**: Expected total revenue from a customer over their relationship with the company; LTV/CAC >3x and CAC payback <18 months are healthy benchmarks
- **CAC Payback Period**: Months of gross margin from a customer required to recover the CAC; shorter payback periods enable faster reinvestment in growth

### Pipeline & Forecasting
- **Pipeline Coverage Ratio**: Total pipeline value divided by revenue target for the period; typically requires 3-4x coverage to hit targets given conversion rates
- **Pipeline Velocity**: Speed at which opportunities move through the sales process (Average Deal Size × Win Rate × Number of Opportunities) / Average Sales Cycle Length
- **Forecast Categories**: Commit (high confidence, 90%+), Best Case (likely if everything goes well, 70-80%), Pipeline (qualified but uncertain, 40-60%), Omitted (lost or disqualified)
- **Weighted Pipeline**: Pipeline value multiplied by probability of close at each stage; provides more accurate revenue projection than raw pipeline value
- **Win Rate**: % of qualified opportunities that result in a closed-won outcome; should be tracked by rep, segment, competition, and deal size to identify patterns

### Sales Operations
- **Territory Design**: Geographic, firmographic, or account-based assignment of market coverage to individual reps; must balance market potential with rep capacity
- **Quota Attainment Distribution**: Healthy distribution targets 60-70% of reps at or above quota; below 50% signals territory, product, or skill issues; above 80% signals targets are too easy
- **Sales Cycle Length**: Average days from opportunity creation to closed decision; reducing sales cycle length increases throughput without adding headcount
- **SPIFF (Special Performance Incentive Fund)**: Short-term incentive added to regular commission to accelerate specific behavior (product push, new segment penetration, Q-end acceleration)
- **Ramping Rep**: Newly hired sales representative during the 90-180 day period before reaching full productivity; ramping reps should have graduated quota expectations

### Customer Success
- **Churn**: Revenue lost from customers who cancel or significantly reduce their subscription; the primary destruction of revenue in subscription businesses
- **Expansion Revenue**: Additional revenue from existing customers through upsell (higher tiers), cross-sell (additional products), and seat/volume expansion
- **Health Score**: Composite index of product adoption, engagement, support history, and relationship quality that predicts renewal probability
- **QBR (Quarterly Business Review)**: Structured executive-level review with key customers to assess value delivered, align on goals, and identify expansion opportunities

## Anti-Patterns to Avoid

- **Top-of-Funnel Obsession**: Optimizing for lead volume without fixing conversion rates at later stages creates an illusion of growth while revenue predictability remains broken.
- **Ignored Churn**: Growing new ARR while churn accelerates is filling a leaky bucket. Fix churn before scaling acquisition — it's cheaper, faster, and compounds.
- **Sales-Marketing Blame Loop**: When pipeline is weak, marketing blames lead quality standards and sales blames lead volume. Fix with shared ICP definition, joint pipeline reviews, and shared revenue OKRs.
- **Forecast Sandbagging**: Sales reps who sandbagging forecasts to manage expectations produce inaccurate business planning and delayed resource decisions. Create psychological safety for accurate forecasting.
- **One-Size-Fits-All GTM**: Using the same sales motion, messaging, and process across enterprise, mid-market, and SMB segments produces mediocrity in all three. Segment deliberately and resource accordingly.
- **Reactive Capacity Planning**: Hiring salespeople only after quota attainment demonstrates need creates a permanent 90-180 day lag in revenue capacity. Model capacity requirements 2-3 quarters ahead.
- **Commission Plan Complexity**: Compensation plans with too many accelerators, decelerators, and special rules create confusion, reduce motivation, and are gamed in unexpected ways. Simplify to drive clear behavior.

## Quality Indicators

- **Revenue Attainment vs. Target**: Consistently achieving 95-105% of target signals healthy planning and execution; significant variance in either direction requires investigation.
- **Forecast Accuracy**: Commit-category forecast vs. actual close rate should exceed 85%; below 70% signals forecasting discipline or CRM hygiene issues.
- **Pipeline Coverage**: 3-4x coverage required; below 2.5x at mid-quarter indicates build problem; above 5x may indicate qualification discipline issues.
- **NRR (Net Revenue Retention)**: Above 120% indicates strong product-market fit and CS effectiveness; below 100% means the business is losing revenue from its existing customer base.
- **Quota Attainment Distribution**: 60-70% of reps at or above quota each quarter; consistent below 50% warrants territory, product, or hiring review.
- **Time to First Deal (New Reps)**: Average days from rep start date to first closed deal; decreasing trend indicates improving onboarding and enablement effectiveness.

## Collaboration Touchpoints

- **With CMO**: Revenue predictability depends on marketing-sales alignment. Quality looks like: shared ICP definition, agreed pipeline stage definitions, joint MQL quality review, shared pipeline OKRs, and monthly joint revenue review where both functions own the outcome together.
- **With CFO**: Revenue forecasting must align with financial planning. Quality looks like: CRO's revenue forecast is the foundation of the financial model, revenue capacity investments are modeled with expected payback periods, and commission and quota changes are financially analyzed before implementation.
- **With CPO (Product)**: Revenue teams need product-market fit and a product they can sell. Quality looks like: win/loss insights systematically fed into product roadmap, pricing strategy developed jointly with product and finance, and sales reps involved in product discovery for their key segments.
- **With CEO**: The CRO is accountable for the company's primary health metric. Quality looks like: CEO has real-time visibility into pipeline health without requiring CRO to present it, CRO can explain any revenue variance in operational terms, and revenue strategy is explicitly connected to the CEO's strategic vision.
